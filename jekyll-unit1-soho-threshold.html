⚗️ Jekyll / Utterson: Elevated Latinate 🧪 Mr Hyde: Somatic Monosyllabic
Dr Henry Jekyll / Mr Utterson (C2 Circumlocutory Restraint)

"It is with profound reluctance that I feel compelled to advert to the nocturnal indiscretions and inexplicable associations that have regrettably compromised my professional standing in the eyes of prudent observers."

Syntactic Markers: Impersonal clefts (It is with...), multi-syllabic Latinate nominalisations (reluctance, indiscretions, associations), epistemic hedging.
Edward Hyde (Visceral Somatic Aggression)

"He snarled aloud into a savage laugh; and the next moment, with the old frenzy of a caged beast, he stamped his foot, shattered the heavy cane against the stones, and bolted into the fog."

Syntactic Markers: Paratactic coordination, Anglo-Saxon somatic verbs (snarled, stamped, shattered, bolted), concrete physical impact.
Museum Words (Passive Decoding) Receptive Only
19th-century London socio-historical terms. Decode them in Victorian fiction, but never deploy them in modern academic essays.
Sawbones [noun, archaic slang] Victorian colloquialism for a surgeon or medical doctor (used by Enfield in Ch. 1). In modern formal prose, use physician, clinician, or medical practitioner.
Link-boy [noun, historical] A boy carrying a flaming torch to guide pedestrians through unlit London fog. Strictly historical.
Brougham [noun, historical] A one-horse closed four-wheeled Victorian carriage. In modern formal reports, use vehicle or conveyance.
Arsenal Words (Active C1/C2 Lexis) High-Yield Production
High-impact evaluative and analytical lexis rewarded in Cambridge C1/C2 essays, proposals, and critical reviews.
Juggernaut [noun] A huge, powerful, and overwhelming force or institution that crushes everything in its path. Collocates: an unstoppable commercial juggernaut, technological juggernaut.
Detestable [adjective] Deserving intense dislike; morally abhorrent. Collocates: a detestable practice, detestable hypocrisy.
Callous [adjective] Showing or having an insensitive and cruel disregard for others. Collocates: callous disregard for human welfare, callous indifference.
Audacity [noun] Willingness to take bold risks; impudent or brazen disrespect. Collocates: the sheer audacity of the proposal, have the audacity to suggest.

2. Syntactic Architecture: Concessive Fronting & Register Contrast

C1/C2 Contrastive Syntax

To capture the tension between respectable Victorian decorum and underlying brutality, C2 writers master concessive fronting, hedged legal reporting, and nominative absolutes:

1. Concessive Adjective Fronting

Fronting a predicative adjective before as or though emphasizes a striking quality while conceding a counter-truth:

• Brutal though the assault was, no bystander dared intervene.
(= Although the assault was brutal...)

2. Concessive 'Much as' Fronting

Much as + Subject + Verb expresses acute internal conflict between desire and duty:

• Much as Utterson wished to trust Jekyll, the forged cheque troubled him.
(= Even though he wished very much...)

3. Epistemic Legal Hedging

Replaces blunt assertions with cautious, defensive professional detachment:

• The solicitor was inclined to conjecture that the document had been coerced.

4. The Concessive Word-Order Trap

Never keep the adjective after the verb when using though/as in fronted concessive inversion:

• ✗ Though was the fog dense, Utterson pressed on.
• ✓ Dense though the fog was, Utterson pressed on.

Register Translation

Colloquial Street Testimony ("Hyde" Somatic Register)
"This ugly little fellow came tearing down the dark street at three in the morning and smashed right into the child at the corner. He didn't even stop—he just trampled straight over her like a monster and paid us off with a cheque signed by a famous doctor!"

"The perpetrator exhibited a callous disregard for pedestrian safety, inflicting unprovoked bodily harm upon a minor before tendering pecuniary restitution via a draft bearing the signature of an eminent medical practitioner."
