Original Novel Extract (1886)
"'All at once, I saw two figures: one a little man who was stumping along eastward at a good walk, and the other a girl of maybe eight or ten who was running as hard as she was able down a cross street. Well, sir, the two ran into one another naturally enough at the corner; and then came the horrible part of the thing; for the man trampled calmly over the child's body and left her screaming on the ground. It sounds nothing to hear, but it was hellish to see. It wasn't like a man; it was like some damned Juggernaut... Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1880s London
19th-century London terms from Chapter 1. Good to recognize when reading Victorian stories, but not used in modern essays.
Sawbones [noun, old slang] 19th-century slang for a doctor or surgeon (used by Enfield in Chapter 1).
Link-boy [noun, historical] A boy who carried a flaming torch through dark or foggy London streets before electric street lighting.
Brougham [noun, historical] A one-horse closed Victorian carriage.
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
Powerful, natural C1/C2 words for essays, reviews, and articles.
Juggernaut [noun] A huge, powerful force or organization that crushes everything in its path. Collocations: an economic juggernaut, a commercial/technological juggernaut.
Callous [adjective] Cruelly insensitive; not caring about hurting others. Collocations: a callous disregard for safety, callous indifference.
Audacity [noun] / Audacious [adj] Boldness, or shocking/rude nerve. Collocations: have the audacity to demand, the sheer audacity of the plan.
Detestable [adjective] Deserving intense dislike; morally unacceptable. Collocations: a detestable practice, detestable behaviour.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Concessive Fronting & Key Collocations

Core • 8 Mins C1/C2 Contrast & Collocation

To write at C1/C2 level, combine strong contrast openers with natural noun collocations:

1. Adjective + though / as + S + V

Start with the adjective to emphasize how extreme something was:

• Brutal though the attack was, Hyde showed no remorse.
(= Although the attack was brutal...)

2. Much as + Subject + Verb

Shows a strong conflict between what someone wants and what they must do:

• Much as Utterson wanted to protect Jekyll, he could not ignore the murder.

3. Key Collocation: Callous disregard

When someone doesn't care at all about others' safety or feelings:

• The stranger showed a callous disregard for the child's safety.

4. Key Collocation: Have the audacity to

When someone is shockingly bold or shameless enough to do something:

• Hyde even had the audacity to ask the bystanders for their names.
