Step 1 · Active Voice First

Academy Session Opener: Speaking Part 2 Long Turn

Every one-hour session at the Belfast Desk begins with the microphone in your hands in the first two minutes. Take 60 seconds to jot four single trigger words down the margin, then speak for two full minutes.

IELTS / C1 Speaking Opener · Cue Card

Describe a difficult skill or subject you mastered through steady practice.

You should say:

  • 1. Past Context: what the skill was and why you decided to learn it
  • 2. Core Obstacle: what made the early stages challenging
  • 3. Turning Point: what method or habit helped things click into place
  • 4. Reflection: and explain how mastering this skill changed your confidence.

C1 Structural Target: Work in one Negative Inversion (Not only did it require...) or one Perfect Participle Clause (Having struggled initially with...).

Step 4 · Gareth's 4-Step Elimination & Deconstruction Strategy

5-Item Academy Placement & Part 4 Diagnostic

Complete the second sentence using between 3 and 6 words, including the given key word unchanged. Click ✂️ Strike Out the Echoes on any item to cross out repeated language and isolate the exact semantic deficit.

Placement Diagnostic Score: 0 / 5 4–5/5: Ready for Track I/III C2 Polish · 2–3/5: Ideal for Track I/II C1 Core · 0–1/5: Start with Phase 1 Deconstruction
DIAGNOSTIC ITEM 01 / 05 · GERUND PREPOSITIONAL COLLOCATION Blueprint: have + no intention of + V-ing

1. The board does not plan to revise the syllabus before next autumn.

INTENTION
The board has the syllabus before next autumn.
Word Count: 0 / 3–6 words Keyword Guard: Waiting for INTENTION
Cambridge Model: no intention of revising (4 words)
4-Step Deconstruction:
1. Echoes Struck: “The board” and “the syllabus before next autumn.”
2. Deficit Isolated: “does not plan to revise” (Present simple negative).
3. Collocation Pattern: The noun intention takes the preposition of + gerund (revising) when preceded by have no.
DIAGNOSTIC ITEM 02 / 05 · PAST SUBJUNCTIVE PREFERENCE Blueprint: would rather + subject + past simple

2. I would prefer it if you did not rely on memorised essay templates.

RATHER
I rely on memorised essay templates.
Word Count: 0 / 3–6 words Keyword Guard: Waiting for RATHER
Cambridge Model: would rather you did not (5 words)
4-Step Deconstruction:
1. Echoes Struck: “I” and “rely on memorised essay templates.”
2. Deficit Isolated: “would prefer it if you did not”.
3. Syntactic Pattern: When the subject changes (`I` → `you`), would rather requires the past subjunctive (you did not + bare infinitive).
DIAGNOSTIC ITEM 03 / 05 · PASSIVE REPORTING VERB Blueprint: Subject + BE + said + to-infinitive

3. Reviewers believe that the archive contains several unpublished manuscripts.

SAID
The archive several unpublished manuscripts.
Word Count: 0 / 3–6 words Keyword Guard: Waiting for SAID
Cambridge Model: is said to contain (4 words)
4-Step Deconstruction:
1. Echoes Struck: “the archive” and “several unpublished manuscripts.”
2. Voice & Tense Shift: Active reporting (“Reviewers believe that... contains”) shifts to Present Passive Reporting with simple infinitive: is said to contain.
DIAGNOSTIC ITEM 04 / 05 · FRONTED NEGATIVE INVERSION Blueprint: Under no circumstances + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb

4. Candidates must never alter the given keyword in Part 4.

CIRCUMSTANCES
Under alter the given keyword in Part 4.
Word Count: 0 / 3–6 words Keyword Guard: Waiting for CIRCUMSTANCES
Cambridge Model: no circumstances must candidates (4 words) (also acceptable: no circumstances should candidates)
4-Step Deconstruction:
1. Echoes Struck: “alter the given keyword in Part 4.”
2. Inversion Trigger: Fronting the negative prepositional phrase Under no circumstances forces subject-auxiliary inversion: must candidates.
DIAGNOSTIC ITEM 05 / 05 · PREPOSITIONAL CONCESSION & NOMINALISATION Blueprint: In spite of + Noun Phrase / the fact that

5. Although the statistics looked daunting, her Task 1 overview remained crystal clear.

SPITE
In statistics, her Task 1 overview remained crystal clear.
Word Count: 0 / 3–6 words Keyword Guard: Waiting for SPITE
Cambridge Model: spite of the daunting (4 words)
4-Step Deconstruction:
1. Echoes Struck: “statistics, her Task 1 overview remained crystal clear.”
2. Nominal Shift: The finite clause “Although the statistics looked daunting” condenses into the prepositional phrase In spite of the daunting + noun (`statistics`).
