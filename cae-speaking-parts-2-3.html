Cambridge C1 Advanced (CAE) · Paper 4 Speaking

1-Minute Speculative Photo Contrast & Collaborative Turn-Taking

Over nineteen years of teaching exam candidates, I have watched plenty of strong IELTS Band 7.5 speakers lose easy marks in Cambridge C1 Advanced Speaking. In IELTS Part 2, you hold the floor alone with a personal story. In CAE Part 2, you have exactly 60 seconds to weave a speculative contrast across two of three photographs without drifting into literal description. Then, in Part 3, you must negotiate ideas live around a 5-point mind-map with a partner rather than taking turns delivering mini-speeches.

Official-Style Part 2 Task · 60 Seconds

"Compare two of the pictures, and say why these people might have chosen to work or create in these environments, and how rewarding each experience might be."

00-20s · Contrast Frame both scenes in one linked sentence. Zero clothing lists.
20-40s · Speculate Q1 Use modal hedging to suggest why each setting suits the task.
40-60s · Evaluate Q2 Weigh up which experience feels more rewarding long term.
Partner Follow-Up Question · 30 Seconds

"Which of these environments would you personally find it easiest to concentrate in?"

30-second rule: Do not echo your partner's entire turn. Pick one setting in your opening ten words, contrast it briefly with one other scene, and land cleanly on a single concrete reason.

Band 5 Cambridge Model Transcript (Photos A & B · 158 Words)

Deliver your own 60-second turn first, then inspect how contrast, speculation, and evaluation interlock cleanly inside one minute.

Colour key: Contrast Frame Speculation Modal Evaluation / Partner Pivot

While both settings involve focused creative work, they operate at opposite ends of the social spectrum. Whereas the first shows two architects leaning over blueprints and a wooden scale model, in stark contrast to that shared commercial table, the writer in the second picture is working completely alone by a sunlit cafe window.

As for their choices, the architects may well be trying to iron out a practical design problem before a client deadline, where immediate face-to-face feedback saves costly mistakes. Judging by the atmosphere in the cafe, however, she looks as though she might have escaped a noisy flat or a rigid office, hoping the gentle hum of background life will keep her moving without interrupting her train of thought.

On balance, I would imagine the studio collaboration feels far more rewarding in the long run, since turning drawings into a real building is a shared milestone, though solitary writing offers a quieter sense of ownership.

30-Second Partner Follow-Up Model (65 Words)

To pick up on that briefly, I can see why an open studio sparks ideas, yet if I had to single one out for pure concentration, I would opt for the window seat in the cafe. What sets the second apart is that you get gentle background movement without anyone tapping your shoulder for a quick opinion, which keeps deep reading and writing intact.

Central Question

What helps a town centre remain genuinely thriving today?

02:00
Spoke 01 of 05

Independent Shops

Opening Angle (15-20s Turn)

Small bookshops, bakeries, and craft retailers give a high street a distinct identity that shoppers cannot replicate online or in identikit retail parks.

Sharp Pushback (Invite Debate)

Independent retail is fragile when commercial rates climb; on its own, a row of boutique shops still empties out completely after five o'clock.

Partner-Ready Discussion Questions

  • Do independent shops actually draw steady weekday footfall, or mostly weekend browsers?
  • How could a council keep local retailers viable without pricing out everyday essentials?

Stage 04 · B2 to C1 Upgrade Clinic

Six Classic CAE Speaking Pitfalls & Band 5 Fixes

Read the B2 trap, say an upgraded version aloud using the structural blueprint, and then check the Cambridge model.

Drill 01 Part 2 · Long Turn

Describing Literal Clothing & Furniture

Formula: While both scenes involve + [Theme], what sets the second apart is + [Contrast]
B2 Pitfall (Drops Marks on Discourse & Lexis) "In the first picture I can see a woman with grey hair in a blue shirt and a man with glasses standing next to a big wooden table with white paper."
C1 Band 5 Upgrade

"While both settings involve hands-on creative work, what sets the architectural practice apart is its deliberate, structured sense of commercial collaboration."

Why it scores: You skip the visual inventory completely and interpret purpose in the first five seconds.

Drill 02 Part 2 · Long Turn

The Two-Separate-Boxes Summary

Formula: Whereas [Photo A] relies on + [Noun], [Photo B], by contrast, thrives on + [Noun]
B2 Pitfall (Describing A, Then Starting Over for B) "Picture one is about architects working on a building together. Now I will talk about picture two. In picture two, a girl is sitting in a cafe with her laptop."
C1 Band 5 Upgrade

"Whereas the architects rely on immediate back-and-forth critique around a physical model, the writer in the cafe, by contrast, appears to thrive on uninterrupted solitude."

Why it scores: Examiners specifically listen for interwoven comparison inside the same clause, not two glued-together descriptions.

Drill 03 Part 2 · Speculation

Stating Guesses as Flat Facts

Formula: Judging by + [Cue], they may well be + [-ing], though it is conceivable that + [Clause]
B2 Pitfall (Missing Modal Speculation) "The people in the pottery studio are very happy because they are close friends who want to sell their mugs and earn money together."
C1 Band 5 Upgrade

"Judging by how relaxed they look, they may well be winding down at a weekend community class, though it is equally conceivable that they share a small cooperative studio."

Why it scores: Offering two plausible hypotheses with modal hedging immediately signals C1 grammatical control.

Drill 04 Part 2 · 30s Follow-Up

Freezing or Echoing on the 30-Second Partner Turn

Formula: To pick up on that briefly, while [Option A] has its charms, I would gravitate towards [Option B] because...
B2 Pitfall (Stalling & Repeating Your Partner) "Erm, yes, I agree with my partner. The cafe is very nice because it is quiet and there are plants and you can drink coffee while working."
C1 Band 5 Upgrade

"To pick up on that briefly, while the cafe clearly has its charms, I would gravitate towards the design studio, simply because bouncing a stubborn problem off a colleague saves hours."

Why it scores: Acknowledges your partner in six words, states a decisive preference, and finishes cleanly inside 30 seconds.

Drill 05 Part 3 · Negotiation

The Flat "I Agree With You" Dead End

Formula: You have raised a fair point about [X], though I wonder whether [Y] matters even more when...
B2 Pitfall (Closing Down Interaction) "Yes, I totally agree with you about public parks. Okay, now let's move on and talk about reliable late transport."
C1 Band 5 Upgrade

"You have raised a fair point about green space making a town welcoming, though I wonder whether late transport matters even more once winter sets in and outdoor squares empty out."

Why it scores: Links your partner's exact idea into the next spoke instead of mechanically ticking off a checklist.

Drill 06 Part 3 · Turn Length

Monologuing for a Full Minute in Part 3

Formula: [1-sentence claim] + [1 brief trade-off] + [Direct hand-off question to partner]
B2 Pitfall (Hogging the Floor Without Hand-Off) "Independent shops are vital because big chains look identical everywhere, and local owners reinvest money, and tourists like buying unique gifts, and high rents are killing small businesses so councils must cut taxes..."
C1 Band 5 Upgrade

"Independent shops give people a real reason to come into town, though high rents make them hard to sustain on their own. Would you rank them ahead of evening venues?"

Why it scores: Crisp 20-second turn with nuance and an authentic invitation that lifts both partners' Interactive Communication scores.
