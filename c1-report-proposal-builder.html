Cambridge C1 Advanced (CAE) · Writing Part 2

C1 Advanced Report vs Proposal: Structure, Headings & Interactive Register Drill

In my nineteen years of teaching—including fifteen years lecturing at Belfast Metropolitan College—no two Part 2 tasks have caused more unnecessary confusion than the Report and the Proposal. Visually, they look like twins: both use a formal register, both use subheadings, and both are written for a superior or peer group. Yet if you write a Report when your examiner asked for a Proposal, your Communicative Achievement mark hits a wall.

The Core Distinction: The Direction of the Clock

Right off the bat, toss out complicated textbook definitions. The difference between a C1 Report and a C1 Proposal comes down to time and persuasion:

  • A Report looks at the PAST or PRESENT to evaluate: An event has already taken place, a programme has just finished, or a facility currently exists. Your job is to give a sober, factual assessment of what worked, what fell short, and finish with one or two practical recommendations for next time.
  • A Proposal looks at the FUTURE to persuade: A council has a £10,000 grant to spend, a university wants to launch a new student wellness initiative, or a company wants to modernise its offices. Your job is to pitch a clear course of action and convince the reader that your plan is the most sensible investment.
Feature C1 Advanced Report C1 Advanced Proposal
Primary Goal Inform, analyse, and evaluate existing facts before suggesting improvements. Persuade a decision-maker to approve a future plan, budget, or course of action.
Dominant Tense & Aspect Past simple, present perfect, and passive reporting ("It was noted that...", "Feedback indicates..."). Hypothetical modals, inverted conditionals, and future passives ("Were the funds to be allocated...", "This would foster...").
Body Paragraph Focus 70% factual evaluation of current/past reality; 30% recommendations at the end. 20% brief summary of current need; 80% forward-looking solutions and expected benefits.
Tone & Voice Detached, analytical, objective, survey-backed. Constructive, persuasive, assured, visionary yet formal.

Section Headings & C1 Phrase Bank

Never write a generic heading like "Body Paragraph 1" or "Conclusion". An examiner scanning forty scripts before lunch rewards headings that reflect the specific context of the prompt.

Report Blueprint

High-Scoring Report Phrases

1. Introduction / Purpose

“The principal aim of this report is to evaluate the effectiveness of... based on feedback gathered from...”

2. Evaluating Strengths & Weaknesses

“While the keynote sessions were widely praised for their depth, dissatisfaction was expressed regarding...”

3. Recommendations

“In light of these findings, what is urgently required is a complete revision of...”

Proposal Blueprint

High-Scoring Proposal Phrases

1. Introduction / Vision

“This proposal outlines how the municipal grant could best be utilised to revitalise...”

2. Pitching Future Measures

“Were the college to invest in dedicated quiet zones, student retention would undoubtedly improve.”

3. Persuasive Conclusion

“Should these measures be implemented, they would yield lasting benefits for the entire institution.”

📊 Retrospective C1 Report Clock: Past / Present Evaluation
Section Heading

Assessment of Current Study Facilities

Formula: It was widely noted that + [Past Clause] / Dissatisfaction was expressed regarding + [Noun Phrase]

“While the ground-floor digital terminals were widely praised by undergraduates during the autumn audit, considerable dissatisfaction was expressed regarding the lack of acoustic insulation on the upper reading gallery.”

Examiner Takeaway: Uses past passive reporting (were widely praised, dissatisfaction was expressed) to present survey evidence with detached authority.
🚀 Forward-Looking C1 Proposal Clock: Future Persuasion & Pitch
Section Heading

Proposed Allocation of the Estates Grant

Formula: It isvital that + [Subject] + [Base Subjunctive] / Were + [Subject] + to + [Infinitive]

“Were the estates committee to allocate £15,000 of the capital grant to acoustic glass partitions, upper-floor noise complaints would be eliminated; it is therefore recommended that installation be scheduled before the spring examinations.”

Examiner Takeaway: Combines an inverted second conditional (Were the committee to allocate...) with a mandative subjunctive passive (that installation be scheduled) to persuade decision-makers.

Item 1 · Passive Reporting Shift Focus: Depersonalising Survey Feedback
Blueprint: A significant proportion of respondents expressed dissatisfaction with + [Noun Phrase]

Flat B2 Candidate Sentence:

“I talked to lots of students after last week's induction tour and they told me the campus map was really confusing.”

Step 1: Which task type does this sentence belong to?

C1 Advanced Report Upgrade:

“A significant proportion of attendees surveyed following last week's induction tour expressed frustration regarding the clarity of the campus orientation map.”

Why this scores C1: It evaluates a completed event (Report) and replaces the chatty "I talked to lots of students" with objective, passive survey authority.

Item 2 · Inverted Conditional Pitch Focus: Persuading a Funding Committee
Blueprint: Were + [Decision-Maker] + to + [Infinitive], [Subject] + would + [Base Verb]

Flat B2 Candidate Sentence:

“If the town council spends the heritage grant on fixing the old clocktower, more tourists will come to our town next summer.”

Step 1: Which task type does this sentence belong to?

C1 Advanced Proposal Upgrade:

“Were the municipal council to allocate the heritage grant to restoring the nineteenth-century clocktower, seasonal visitor numbers would increase substantially.”

Why this scores C1: It pitches a future budget decision (Proposal) using an inverted second conditional (Were the council to allocate...) that sounds composed and persuasive.

Item 3 · Wh- Cleft Recommendation Focus: Remedying an Existing Flaw
Blueprint: What + [Current Situation] + requires / calls for + is + [Nominalised Solution]

Flat B2 Candidate Sentence:

“The college library is way too noisy right now, so the librarian really needs to make a strict silent zone on the top floor.”

Step 1: Which task type does this sentence belong to?

C1 Advanced Report Upgrade:

“Given the current level of disruption in the main reading room, what the library urgently requires is the designation of a strictly enforced silent study zone on the upper floor.”

Why this scores C1: It diagnoses an existing facility issue (Report) and uses a Wh- cleft plus nominalisation (the designation of...) to spotlight the recommendation.

Item 4 · Negative Inversion Persuasion Focus: Highlighting Dual Future Benefits
Blueprint: Not only would + [Proposed Measure] + [Verb 1], but it would also + [Verb 2]

Flat B2 Candidate Sentence:

“Starting a mentorship scheme for new staff next year will help them settle in faster. Also, it will stop people quitting their jobs.”

Step 1: Which task type does this sentence belong to?

C1 Advanced Proposal Upgrade:

“Not only would the introduction of a peer mentorship scheme accelerate onboarding for new recruits, but it would also significantly reduce early staff turnover.”

Why this scores C1: It pitches an unlaunched future initiative (Proposal) and binds two benefits into a single rhythmic period via Not only would...

Item 5 · Concessive Contrast Focus: Balanced Post-Event Assessment
Blueprint: In spite of + [Positive Nominal Phrase], [Passive Limitation Clause]

Flat B2 Candidate Sentence:

“Even though the charity concert raised lots of money last Saturday, the sound system broke down twice during the evening.”

Step 1: Which task type does this sentence belong to?

C1 Advanced Report Upgrade:

“In spite of the impressive funds raised during Saturday's charity concert, the evening was marred by repeated technical failures with the acoustic equipment.”

Why this scores C1: It reviews a past event (Report) with mature concessive framing (In spite of...) and precise passive lexis (was marred by).

Item 6 · Participle Consequence Clause Focus: Projecting Long-Term Impact
Blueprint: Should + [Subject] + [Base Verb], [Main Clause], thereby + [Participle (-ing)]

Flat B2 Candidate Sentence:

“If we subsidise annual train passes for employees, fewer people will drive to the office, and this will cut our company's carbon footprint.”

Step 1: Which task type does this sentence belong to?

C1 Advanced Proposal Upgrade:

“Should the board subsidise annual rail passes for staff, daily commuter traffic would decline markedly, thereby reducing the firm's overall carbon footprint.”

Why this scores C1: It combines an inverted conditional (Should the board subsidise...) with a resulting participle clause (, thereby reducing...) to pitch a future corporate policy (Proposal).

Frequently Asked Questions: C1 Advanced Reports & Proposals

What is the main difference between a Report and a Proposal in C1 Advanced (CAE)? ▼

The core difference is temporal focus and rhetorical purpose. A Report looks backwards or at the present to evaluate a completed event, existing facility, or survey, ending with a brief recommendation section. A Proposal looks forward to persuade a decision-maker (such as a college principal, local council, or company director) how to allocate funds, organise a future initiative, or solve an upcoming challenge.

Should I use headings in both CAE Reports and Proposals? ▼

Yes. Both Reports and Proposals in Cambridge C1 Advanced Part 2 should use a main title and 4 clear, topic-specific section headings. Using headings demonstrates immediate awareness of genre conventions and secures higher marks under the Organisation and Communicative Achievement criteria.

Can I invent survey statistics in a C1 Report? ▼

You can reference general survey feedback, but avoid inventing overly specific percentages like "73.4% of students said...", which sounds artificial. Instead, use authentic C1 reporting quantifiers: "The overwhelming majority of respondents noted...", "A sizable proportion of attendees expressed concern over...", or "Consensus among staff suggests that..."
