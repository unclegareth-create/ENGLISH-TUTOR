From the Belfast Desk: Live C1/C2 Syntax & Part 4 Transformation Workbench

A quick word from my desk before you start: In 19 years of teaching—including 15 years lecturing at Belfast Metropolitan College—I have watched thousands of bright candidates get tied in knots by internet snake oil. Toss out the memorised “Band 9 vocabulary lists” (plethora, conundrum, ubiquitous, paramount) and robotic four-sentence templates (“In a nutshell”, “Every coin has two sides”). Your examiner marks forty scripts before lunch; forced thesaurus words put them to sleep, while clean syntactic variety wakes them right up.

In Cambridge C1/C2 Use of English Part 4—and in high-scoring IELTS writing—three or four well-placed structural shifts do all the heavy lifting. Use my 4-Step Elimination & Deconstruction Strategy below: 1. Strike Out the Echoes to isolate the exact meaning left behind; 2. Inspect Tense Harmony; 3. Check Voice Shift; and 4. Solve the C1 Pattern in 3 to 6 words without ever changing the keyword.

Item 01 · Negative Nominal Refusal

The board definitely will not revise the quarterly forecast before Friday.

Step 2 Tense: Present Simple (Future Refusal) Step 3 Voice: Active → Nominal Phrase 0 / 3–6 words KEY WORD: INTENTION (Unchanged Guard)
The board revising the quarterly forecast before Friday.
Cambridge Split-Mark Scheme (1 mark // 1 mark)
Model: has no intention [1 mark] // of [1 mark] (also accepts have no intention // of · 4 words).
Formula: have no intention + of + V-ing. Notice how revise shifts to the gerund revising after the preposition of.
Item 02 · Past Subjunctive Preference

I think it would be better if you did not mention the audit during lunch.

Step 2 Tense: Present Preference → Past Subjunctive Step 3 Voice: Active → Active Clause 0 / 3–6 words KEY WORD: RATHER (Unchanged Guard)
I mention the audit during lunch.
Cambridge Split-Mark Scheme (1 mark // 1 mark)
Model: would rather [1 mark] // you did not [1 mark] (5 words; contractions like didn’t count as 2 words).
Formula: Subject 1 + would rather + Subject 2 + Past Simple for present/future preference.
Item 03 · Passive Reporting + Perfect Infinitive

Journalists report that the minister resigned over the leaked memo.

Step 2 Tense: Present Report + Past Action → to have + V3 Step 3 Voice: Active → Impersonal Passive 0 / 3–6 words KEY WORD: SAID (Unchanged Guard)
The minister over the leaked memo.
Cambridge Split-Mark Scheme (1 mark // 1 mark)
Model: is said [1 mark] // to have resigned [1 mark] (5 words).
Formula: Subject + BE + said + to have + Past Participle when the reported action happened before the reporting verb.
Item 04 · Fronted Negative Inversion

The auditor never once suspected that the ledger had been altered.

Step 2 Tense: Past Simple → Inverted Auxiliary (did + inf) Step 3 Voice: Active → Inverted Active 0 / 3–6 words KEY WORD: TIME (Unchanged Guard)
At suspect that the ledger had been altered.
Cambridge Split-Mark Scheme (1 mark // 1 mark)
Model: no time [1 mark] // did the auditor [1 mark] (5 words).
Formula: At no time + Auxiliary (did) + Subject + Bare Infinitive.
Item 05 · Concessive Prepositional Shift

Although the harbour experienced gale-force winds, the ferry docked on schedule.

Step 2 Tense: Concessive Clause → Prepositional Phrase Step 3 Voice: Clause → Nominalised Object 0 / 3–6 words KEY WORD: SPITE (Unchanged Guard)
The ferry docked on schedule gale-force winds in the harbour.
Cambridge Split-Mark Scheme (1 mark // 1 mark)
Model: in spite [1 mark] // of (the) [1 mark] (3 or 4 words).
Formula: in spite of + Noun Phrase / V-ing. Never write despite of.
Item 06 · Past Perfect Correlative Inversion

Immediately after the chair closed the session, the delegates began arguing.

Step 2 Tense: Past Sequence → Past Perfect Inversion (had + V3) Step 3 Voice: Active → Inverted Active 0 / 3–6 words KEY WORD: SOONER (Unchanged Guard)
No the session than the delegates began arguing.
Cambridge Split-Mark Scheme (1 mark // 1 mark)
Model: sooner had the chair [1 mark] // closed [1 mark] (5 words).
Formula: No sooner + had + Subject + Past Participle ... than (whereas Hardly / Scarcely pairs with when).
