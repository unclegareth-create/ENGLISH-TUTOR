2. The 4 Intruding Error Patterns (What to Prune)

Pruning Rules

Examine the single contaminating word that causes each common B2/L1 intrusion:

1. Prepositional Intrusion with 'That'

Never place a preposition before a 'that' clause:

insist that (not insist ON that) • explain that (not explain TO that)

"The auditor insisted that all receipts be verified."

2. Concession Over-Extension

'Despite' takes a noun or -ing, never 'of':

despite the rain (not despite OF the rain) • in spite of

"Despite initial delays, the project finished on schedule."

3. Causative Infinitive Contamination

'Have someone DO' takes a bare infinitive, never 'to':

had the engineer inspect (not had the engineer TO inspect)

"We had an independent surveyor assess the damage."

4. Conditional 'Would' Intrusion

Never put 'would' inside the IF condition clause:

If we had known (not If we WOULD have known)

"If the company had known the cost, it would have halted."
