The Belfast Desk Rule: Stop memorising obscure "Band 9" words. Save the final two minutes of Task 1 and Task 2 to hunt these five exact habits—one fast sweep per habit—and your Grammatical Range & Accuracy jumps from 6.5 to 7.0–7.5 without rewriting a single idea.

The 2-Minute Exam Sweep Checklist (5 Fast Passes)
  1. Pass 1 (25s) — Uncountables & Plurals: Scan for infrastructure, staff, damage, evidence, equipment (never plural -s) and check One major + singular.
  2. Pass 2 (25s) — Articles: Add a / an before adjective + singular countable noun (a less habitable environment). Cut the before general plurals (governments, cultural institutions).
  3. Pass 3 (25s) — Topic-Word Spelling: Check repeated words from the prompt (emissions, guarantee, catastrophic, governments, institutions, proportion, steady).
  4. Pass 4 (25s) — Prepositions & Linkers: Task 1: in 1970, from X to Y, fell to zero, late at night, and then. Task 2: need to address, during events, as well as.
  5. Pass 5 (20s) — Tense & Comparisons: Past dates = Past Simple (was / grew). Check has become and than that of.

Speaking Part 2 Bonus: The Past Simple Ignition Switch

One of the most common Speaking Part 2 slips among strong candidates is opening a specific past story with the Present Perfect ("I have visited…"). Lock in Past Simple on Sentence 1 and the whole 2-minute turn runs smoothly.

Specific Past Event Opening = Time Anchor (Last year / Two years ago / When I was at university) + Past Simple (visited / met / decided)

1. The 1-Minute Note Secret

Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin (one per bullet). Never write full sentences—your brain speaks faster from sparks than scripts.

2. The 3-Act Story Arc

Past Context (30s) → Sensory Snapshot / Core Scene (45s) → Reflection / 'So What?' (45s).

3. The Cambridge Anchor

Bullet points are springboards, not an interrogation checklist. If you finish early, deepen your reflection and comparison until the examiner stops you.

4 Quick-Fire Part 2 Opening Switches

Say the corrected opening out loud first, then reveal the model.

Cue Card 1 (A memorable hotel stay):
"I have stayed in a really impressive hotel last summer."

Model: "Last summer, I stayed in a memorable boutique hotel while I was attending a conference."
Why: Last summer locks the time in the finished past → Past Simple (stayed).

Cue Card 2 (A difficult decision you made):
"One of the biggest decisions I have made was when I have moved abroad for work."

Model: "One of the biggest decisions I've ever made was when I moved abroad to take on a new role."
Why: Life-experience frame (ever made) can use Present Perfect, then immediately drop into Past Simple (moved) once you enter the specific event.

Cue Card 3 (A time someone gave you helpful advice):
"Two years ago, my supervisor has given me a piece of advice that changed how I work."

Model: "Two years ago, my supervisor gave me a piece of advice that completely changed how I manage my workload."
Why: Any …ago marker always demands Past Simple (gave).

Cue Card 4 (An exhibition or cultural place you visited):
"I want to talk about a science museum that I have visited during a short city break."

Model: "I'd like to talk about a science exhibition that I visited during a short city break a few months ago."
Why: During a short city break names a finished occasion → Past Simple (visited).
