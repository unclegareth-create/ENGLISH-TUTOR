C1 Syntactic Control Structure over Obscure Lexis

Moving Beyond Flat SVO Syntax

Examiners look for deliberate shifts in sentence focus. Rather than relying on simple Subject-Verb-Object arrangements, master these three explicit structural formulas to highlight key arguments.

Formula A

Wh- Cleft Sentences

Shifts focus onto the predicate or outcome.

[ What + Clause ] + [ BE ] + [ Noun / Infinitive ]
Flat: Tim Hunkin’s biting satire sets these arcades apart.
C1 Cleft: What sets these arcades apart is Tim Hunkin’s biting sense of social satire.
Formula B

Introductory 'It' Cleft

Isolates a single agent, cause, or circumstance.

It + [ BE ] + [ Focus ] + [ that / who ] + [ Clause ]
Flat: The physical tactility captivates visitors.
C1 Cleft: It is the physical tactility of the automata that captivates visitors fatigued by screens.
Formula C

Negative Inversion

Fronts a negative adverbial for rhetorical emphasis.

[ Neg. Adv. ] + [ Aux ] + [ Subject ] + [ Verb ]
Flat: You rarely find arcades that mock corporate greed.
C1 Inversion: Rarely does one encounter an arcade that invites players to mock corporate greed.

Cambridge C1 Advanced Reading and Use of English · Part 4

Key Word Transformations: The Arcade Challenge

Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first, using the keyword given. Do not change the word given. You must use between 3 and 6 words, including the word given.

Gareth's 4-Step Elimination Method

1. Strike Out Echoes

Cross out repeated words in Sentence 2 to isolate the deficit.

2. Tense Harmony

Check whether the original verb is past, present, or modal.

3. Voice Shift

Identify active-to-passive or passive-to-active shifts.

4. Collocation Lock

Fit the keyword into its fixed C1 grammatical frame.

QUESTION 1 OF 4 SELDOM

Original: Visitors hardly ever encounter arcade machines with such provocative themes.

Second sentence: [ ... ] across arcade machines with such provocative themes.

0 words

Cambridge Key: Seldom do visitors come (4 words)

Structural Deconstruction: Fronted negative adverbial inversion with present simple auxiliary: Seldom + do + subject (visitors) + base verb (come) to complete the phrasal verb come across.

QUESTION 2 OF 4 INTENTION

Original: The creator never planned to install digital touchscreens in his workshop.

Second sentence: The creator [ ... ] digital touchscreens in his workshop.

0 words

Cambridge Key: had no intention of installing (5 words)

Structural Deconstruction: Past tense harmony ("never planned" → "had") + fixed C1 noun frame: have no intention of + -ing participle (installing).

QUESTION 3 OF 4 UNTIL

Original: People only realise how intricate the wooden clockwork is when the mechanism starts moving.

Second sentence: Not [ ... ] people realise how intricate the wooden clockwork is.

0 words

Cambridge Key: until the mechanism starts moving do (6 words)

Structural Deconstruction: Fronted negative time clause inversion: Not until [clause] + auxiliary (do) + subject (people) + base verb (realise).

QUESTION 4 OF 4 IT

Original: What makes these satirical contraptions so memorable is their complete lack of digital screens.

Second sentence: [ ... ] lack of digital screens that makes these satirical contraptions so memorable.

0 words

Cambridge Key: It is their complete (4 words)

Structural Deconstruction: Shifting from a Wh-cleft (What makes... is...) to an introductory It-cleft (It is [focus] that...) while preserving present tense harmony.

IELTS Part 3 · Cambridge C1/C2 Discussion Coherence & Sustained Argumentation

The A.R.E.A. Speaking Framework

Examiners mark candidates down when answers trail off into fragmented anecdotes. Build every Part 3 response using the four-tier A.R.E.A. architecture: Assertion → Reason → Example → Alternative.

Examiner Discussion Prompt

"To what extent do you think traditional hands-on crafts and eccentric novelties are losing their place in an increasingly automated world?"

Use the interactive blueprint below to structure a balanced, high-scoring C1 response.

Tier A · Assertion Direct Thesis

State your core stance with immediate nuance and depersonalisation.

Starter: "It is widely argued that artisanal craft is fading, yet evidence suggests..."
Tier R · Reason Underlying Cause

Explain the cultural, psychological, or economic reason driving your thesis.

Starter: "What fuels this renewed appreciation is a collective fatigue with frictionless digital screens..."
Tier E · Example Concrete Evidence

Provide an objective illustration (such as Tim Hunkin's mechanical arcades).

Starter: "A striking illustration is Tim Hunkin’s seaside arcade, where players eagerly queue to interact with physical cogs..."
Tier A · Alternative Nuanced Counterweight

Acknowledge constraints, economic realities, or contrasting outcomes.

Starter: "That being said, unless such independent creators receive cultural subsidies, they inevitably remain niche curiosities..."
