1. Read the Scene & Useful Vocabulary

Jekyll & Hyde • Chapter 9

Read Dr Hastie Lanyon's account of the night Edward Hyde came to his house at midnight in Dr Jekyll's oversized clothes, mixed a chemical potion, and transformed before his eyes:

Original Novel Extract (1886)
"This person wasningerly dressed in clothes far too large for him, which made his figure look almost ludicrous; yet far from moving me to laughter, his presence inspired a curiosity and disgust that I could scarcely control... He measured out a few minims of the red tincture and added one of the powders. The mixture, which was at first of a reddish hue, began, in proportion as the crystals melted, to brighten in colour, to effervesce audibly, and to throw off small fumes of vapour. Suddenly and at the same moment, the ebullition ceased and the compound changed to a dark purple, which faded again more slowly to a watery green. He put the glass to his lips and drank at one gulp. A cry followed; he reeled, staggered, clutched at the table and held on, staring with injected eyes, gasping with open mouth; and as I looked there came, I thought, a change—he seemed to swell—his face became suddenly black and the features seemed to melt and alter—and the next moment, I had sprung to my feet and leaped back against the wall, my arm raised to shield me from that prodigy, my mind submerged in terror. 'O God!' I screamed, and 'O God!' again and again; for there before my eyes—pale and shaken, and half-fainting, and groping before him with his hands, like a man restored from death—there stood Henry Jekyll!" — R.L. Stevenson, Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (1886), Chapter 9: Dr Lanyon's Narrative Original Text

Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
High-value vocabulary for essays and reviews about appearances, honesty, and contrast.
Facade [noun] A false outward appearance that hides a less pleasant reality (or the front wall of a building). Collocations: behind a polite facade, maintain a cheerful facade.
Duplicity [noun] Deceitfulness; pretending to have one set of feelings while acting differently in secret. Collocations: accused of duplicity, political duplicity.
Incongruous [adjective] Strange and out of place in a particular setting. Collocations: look completely incongruous, an incongruous detail.
Repugnance [noun] / Repugnant [adj] Intense disgust or moral revulsion. Collocations: morally repugnant, feel a deep repugnance toward.

2. The Grammar Pattern: "Not Until" & "So + Adjective" Inversion

C1/C2 Sentence Control

Two of the most frequently tested inversions in Cambridge Use of English Part 4 are Not until / Only when and So + Adjective. Here is the exact trick to getting them right every time:

1. Not until / Only when (Wait for the Main Clause!)

When a sentence starts with Not until or Only when + time clause, keep the first clause normal and invert the second clause:

• Not until Hyde drank the potion did Lanyon realise the truth.
(NOT: *Not until did Hyde drink...)

2. So + Adjective + be + Subject + that

Move So + adjective to the front and put was / were / is / are before the subject:

• Normal: His clothes were so incongruous that Lanyon stared.
• Inverted: So incongruous were his clothes that Lanyon stared.

3. Such + be + Noun + that

With a noun (instead of an adjective), start with Such was/is... that:

• Such was Lanyon's shock that he could never recover.
(= Lanyon's shock was so great that...)

4. Far from + -ing

A great C1/C2 opener meaning "Instead of doing X, it did the exact opposite":

• Far from making Lanyon laugh, the visitor's clothes filled him with dread.

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Real-World C1/C2 Application

See how Not until... did..., Far from + -ing, and words like facade and incongruous work naturally in a modern Cambridge C1/C2 Review or Essay:

In Lanyon's Narrative (1886)
"Far from moving me to laughter, his incongruous appearance filled me with disgust. Not until he drank the potion did I realise what lay behind Henry Jekyll's respectable facade."
In a Modern C1/C2 Film/Architecture Review
"Far from spoiling the historic square, the glass museum extension looks surprisingly natural beside the older stone buildings—never once feeling incongruous. Not until you step behind the building's quiet street facade do you realise how spacious the exhibition halls actually are."
