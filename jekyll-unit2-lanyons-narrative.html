"Notwithstanding the patient's febrile agitation and the marked muscular convulsion accompanying his ingestion of the compound, I endeavoured to maintain the dispassionate scrutiny of a medical observer."

Syntactic Markers: Prepositional concession (Notwithstanding...), clinical diagnostics (febrile agitation, dispassionate scrutiny).

Syntactic Markers: Tricolon rhetorical interrogatives, prophetic modality (shall stagger), participle result clause.

2. Syntactic Architecture: Negative & Restrictive Inversion of Disbelief

C1/C2 Emphatic Inversion

When a writer fronts a negative, restrictive, or degree expression to dramatize astonishment or scientific shock, the auxiliary verb must precede the grammatical subject:

1. 'Never' & 'Little' Inversion

Fronting Never or Little (meaning "not at all") triggers immediate auxiliary inversion:

• Never had Lanyon witnessed such a metamorphosis.
• Little did he suspect the true nature of the salt.

2. 'Scarcely / Hardly... when'

Expresses rapid, shocking succession between two past actions using past perfect + when:

• Scarcely had the compound effervesced when its hue turned watery green.

3. 'Only when / Not until' Delay

When Only when or Not until introduces a subordinate clause, inversion occurs in the main clause, not the subordinate clause:

• Not until the potion settled did Lanyon perceive the change.

4. 'So + Adjective + be + Subject'

Fronting a degree adjective with So inverts the copula verb before the subject + that-clause:

• So acute was Lanyon's repugnance that his pulse faltered.
