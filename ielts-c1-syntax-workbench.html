Belfast Desk Red Pen · IELTS Task 2 & C1 Advanced

C1 Structural Power Over Band-9 Thesaurus Snake Oil

If you have ever been told to memorise a list of fifty "Band 9 words" like plethora, conundrum, ubiquitous, or paramount before walking into the exam hall, toss that list in the bin right off the bat.

From my desk to yours: I have spent 19 years in the classroom—15 of them lecturing at Belfast Metropolitan College—marking thousands of IELTS and Cambridge scripts with a red pen and a mug of strong tea. Examiners mark forty essays before lunch. Robotic templates like "Every coin has two sides" or "In a nutshell" put them straight to sleep, and shoehorning archaic vocabulary into a broken sentence just costs you marks for unnatural collocation. What actually wakes an examiner up is clean, restrained syntactic variety: three or four well-placed C1 structural shifts that let your argument speak for itself in the cold light of day.

The "Red Pen" Snake-Oil Detoxifier

Trap #1 · The Thesaurus Pile-Up Band 6.0 → Band 7.5+
The Forced Thesaurus Sentence

"In today's ubiquitous modern era, a plethora of individuals confront a paramount conundrum regarding housing affordability."

Gareth's Red Pen: An era cannot be ubiquitous (that means present everywhere at once), plethora implies an excessive oversupply rather than simply "many", and pairing paramount with conundrum reads like a swallowed dictionary. It unravels before the examiner reaches the verb.

Clean Band 7.5+ / C1 Upgrade (Wh-Cleft + Preparatory 'It')

"What concerns millions of young earners today is the steady erosion of affordable urban housing."

Why it scores: Zero archaic vocabulary. A crisp Wh-cleft (What concerns... is...) combined with natural C1 nominalisation (the steady erosion of...) makes the point plain as day.

Trap #2 · The Robotic Internet Template Band 5.5 → Band 7.5+
The Memorised Cliché Opener

"With the rapid development of society, every coin has two sides, and remote working is definitely a double-edged sword."

Gareth's Red Pen: Examiners see this exact sentence ten times a day. Because it could be pasted into an essay about smartphones, fast food, or space travel without changing a single noun, Cambridge assessors discount it as memorised filler.

Clean Band 7.5+ / C1 Upgrade (Negative Inversion)

"Not only has remote working reshaped commuter habits, but it has also blurred the boundary between domestic life and paid employment."

Why it scores: Instead of announcing "two sides" with a cliché about coins, a Not only... but also... negative inversion demonstrates both sides directly inside the grammar.

Trap #3 · Chatty Personal Pronouns Band 6.0 → Band 7.5+
The Informal First-Person Drift

"I strongly believe that we can all see how cars ruin our city centres, and in a nutshell, governments must ban them."

Gareth's Red Pen: Leaning on I, we, our and pub-chat idioms like in a nutshell drags an academic essay down into a casual blog post. Save I for your thesis statement and conclusion; keep body paragraphs 100% depersonalised.

Clean Band 7.5+ / C1 Upgrade (Passive Reporting + Participle Clause)

"Unrestricted private traffic is widely considered to degrade urban air quality, thereby strengthening the case for pedestrianised city centres."

Why it scores: Elevating the object into the subject slot with is widely considered to removes I/we completely, while , thereby strengthening... links cause and effect in one smooth breath.

Trap #4 · Misused C2 Dictionary Words Band 6.0 → Band 7.5+
The Collocation Trainwreck

"Schools should instigate magnanimous erudition so pupils can ameliorate their job prospects."

Gareth's Red Pen: A proper mess. You instigate a riot or an inquiry, not learning; magnanimous means generous in forgiving an enemy; and erudition is scholarly book-learning. Using normal words inside an It-cleft is ten times more convincing.

Clean Band 7.5+ / C1 Upgrade (Emphatic It-Cleft)

"It is through practical vocational training that secondary schools can best prepare school-leavers for long-term employment."

Why it scores: Every single word is natural, accurate British English, and the It is through X that Y cleft frame spotlights the core argument effortlessly.
