Reporting Passives for Objective Writing

Adapted from Stoker's Novel (1897)
"The strange schooner, driven by the fury of the storm, rushed straight onto the Tate Hill Pier. A searchlight was turned upon her, and there was seen lashed to the wheel a corpse, with drooping head, which swung horribly to and fro at each motion of the ship. No other form could be seen on deck at all... Later, in the dead man's pocket was found a bottle, carefully corked, containing a roll of paper which proved to be an addendum to the ship's log: '19 July.—We are progressing well, yet the men are growing unaccountably anxious. Two nights ago, one of the crew vanished from his watch; he is presumed to have fallen overboard in the dark.'" — Bram Stoker, Dracula (1897), Chapter 7: Cutting from The Dailygraph, 8 August Adapted Extract
Specialist / Historical Words (Just for Reading) Sailing Terms
19th-century sailing terms from the ship's logbook. Useful for understanding the scene, but not needed in general essays.
Bulwark [noun, nautical] The wooden side of a ship above the upper deck. (Note: In modern essays, "a bulwark against inflation/corruption" is sometimes used metaphorically to mean a defensive wall.)
Bight [noun, nautical] A wide curve in a coastline, or a loop of rope.
Coxswain [noun, nautical] The person who steers a ship's boat or a racing rowing crew.
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
High-value vocabulary for formal reports, essays, and articles.
Unaccountably [adverb] In a way that cannot be explained; strangely or mysteriously. Collocations: unaccountably delayed, unaccountably overlooked.
Derelict [adjective] In a very poor condition because of neglect and disuse; abandoned. Collocations: derelict industrial buildings, derelict land.
Disclose [verb] To make secret or new information known publicly. Collocations: disclose financial details, refuse to disclose information.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Reporting Passives

Core • 8 Mins C1/C2 Objective Tone

In formal essays, news articles, and reports, you often want to report what people believe, claim, or suspect without writing "People think that..." or "Someone said that...". Use these two passive patterns:

1. Pattern A: It + passive + that...

Start with It is/was believed, reported, claimed, or observed that...:

• It was observed that the ship was being steered by no living hand.
• It is widely believed that early intervention reduces costs.

2. Pattern B (Present): Subject + passive + to V

Start with the subject, followed by is/are thought, believed, or known to + verb:

• The cargo is thought to consist of fifty wooden crates.
(= People think the cargo consists of...)

3. Pattern B (Past): Subject + passive + to have V3

When the action happened earlier in the past, use to have + past participle:

• The crew are presumed to have perished during the storm.
(= Authorities presume that the crew perished earlier.)

4. Passive Past: to have been + V3

If the earlier action was also passive, use to have been + past participle:

• ✗ The captain is thought to be tied to the wheel days earlier.
• ✓ The captain is thought to have been tied to the wheel days earlier.

Set in a remote coastal town in winter, the novel builds a sinister atmosphere from its very first pages. Hemmed in by steep cliffs and impenetrable sea mist, the villagers are immediately apprehensive when an abandoned cargo ship washes ashore during a storm.
