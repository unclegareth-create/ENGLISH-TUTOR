Cambridge Use of English · Part 3

Word Formation: A System, Not a Vocabulary List

Most students prepare for Use of English Part 3 by reading hundreds of pages of alphabetical word families. Under exam pressure, that passive recognition evaporates. To guarantee high marks on Cambridge B2 First, C1 Advanced, and C2 Proficiency, you need a systematic four-step diagnostic protocol.

The Structure of Part 3

In Part 3, you are given a short authentic text with eight numbered gaps. Next to each gap is a capitalised root word (e.g. DESTROY, CARE, APPROVE). Your objective is to derive the appropriate morphological form that fits both grammatically and semantically into the sentence.

The 4-Step Diagnostic Protocol

Whenever your eyes land on a gap, run this 10-second mental audit before you write a single letter:

Step 1

Determine the Syntactic Slot (Part of Speech)

Inspect the immediate grammatical neighbourhood of the gap:

  • • After possessives, articles, or adjectives? → Noun needed (the sudden [LOSS] of revenue).
  • • Preceding a noun, or following linking verbs (seem, become, remain)? → Adjective needed.
  • • Modifying an adjective, verb, or starting an entire clause? → Adverb needed ([PREDICTABLY], the meeting ran late).
  • • Following modal auxiliaries or subject pronouns? → Verb needed (we must [STRENGTHEN] our ties).
Step 2

Check the Semantic Polarity (Positive vs Negative)

This is where 40% of candidates lose marks. Even if you correctly identify that an adjective is required, does the sentence meaning demand a negative prefix? Look for contextual warning words like failed, unable, despite, regrettably, few, seldom.

Example: “Despite extensive testing, the device proved remarkably [RELY].” → unreliable, NOT reliable!

Step 3

Check Number & Person (Singular vs Plural)

If the gap requires a noun, does it agree with plural verbs or determiners like these, several, numerous? An answer like expectation when the sentence says “their highest ______ were exceeded” receives zero marks. The correct form is expectations.

Step 4

Audit Orthography & Spelling Rules

Check the spelling mechanics:

  • • Dropping the final silent e before vowel suffixes (argue → argument, admire → admirable).
  • • Changing y to i (rely → reliable, day → daily).
  • • Consonant doubling on stressed short vowels (occur → occurrence).

The "Double-Shift" Trap

At C1 and C2 levels, Cambridge frequently introduces root words that demand two simultaneous transformations: both an affix change AND a prefix shift. For example:

Root Common Incomplete Answer Full Exam Transformation (Prefix + Suffix)
APPROVE approval disapproval
SATISFY satisfied dissatisfied / dissatisfaction
CARE carefully carelessly
LEAD leading misleading

Interactive Practice: 4 Classic Exam Challenges

1. Her ______ to admit defeat inspired the whole squad. [REFUSE] See answer

Answer: refusal

Diagnostic: Follows possessive pronoun “Her” → Noun required. Root refuse + noun suffix -al.

2. The company announced a series of ______ measures to cut costs. [PRECEDE] See answer

Answer: unprecedented

Diagnostic: Modifies noun “measures” → Adjective required. Meaning denotes something never seen before → Negative prefix un- + root + suffix -ed.

3. We must ______ the bridge before heavy winter storms arrive. [STRONG] See answer

Answer: strengthen

Diagnostic: Follows modal verb “must” → Base verb required. Root strong → noun strength → verb strengthen.
