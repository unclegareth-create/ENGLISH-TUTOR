The "Flipped Classroom" Methodology

When you have six to eight weeks before test day, standard general English classes cannot move the needle quickly enough. Spending expensive live lesson time silently reading a 900-word passage or listening to an audio track is an inefficient use of your tuition budget.

This syllabus operates on a strict Flipped Mastery Model:

  • Live 1-on-1 Sessions (2 hours / week): Reserved purely for high-stakes active output and real-time correction. We focus on bulletproof paragraph architecture, deconstructing unseen prompts, live line-by-line essay editing, and rapid-fire Speaking Part 3 abstract discourse.
  • Strict At-Home Regimen: Reading and Listening papers are completed strictly at home under exam timer conditions using official Cambridge IELTS Academic Practice Tests (Books 16–19). You score them yourself, analyze your mistake patterns, and bring only unsolved queries to class. Writing Task 1 reports are reviewed asynchronously via annotated Google Docs.

Band 6.0 vs Band 7.0: The Tipping Point

Many students who are stuck at Band 6.0 or 6.5 have plenty of vocabulary. What keeps them from hitting a 7.0 is usually test mechanics and unconscious habits:

Criterion Band 6.0 Plateau Band 7.0 Requirement
Writing Task Response Lists multiple superficial ideas without deep elaboration; position is somewhat unclear or wavers. Addresses all parts of the task with a sustained, clear position. Develops 2 main arguments deeply with logical explanations and concrete examples.
Writing Cohesion Overuses mechanical linkers ("Firstly, Secondly, Furthermore, In a nutshell"); repetitive referencing. Manages paragraph transitions flexibly; uses substitution and referencing without drawing undue attention to linking devices.
Speaking Fluency Speaks at length but shows noticeable hesitation searching for words; answers in Part 3 remain personal anecdotes. Speaks at length without noticeable effort. Moves effortlessly from personal stories to abstract societal analysis using the A.R.E.A. method.
Grammar & Accuracy Attempts complex sentences, but frequent systematic errors occur (e.g. articles, prepositions, verb tense). Produces a wide variety of complex sentence structures; achieves frequent error-free sentences.

The 12-Lesson Course Architecture

Week 1 · Foundations Deconstructing the Rubric

Lesson 1: WT2 Core Structure & Introductions

Deconstruct the 4 grading criteria. Compare Band 6.0 vs 7.0 essays. Master the 4-minute introduction formula (Hook, Paraphrase, Thesis/Outline).

🎯 Output: 3 high-scoring introductions written in < 5 mins each.

Lesson 2: Speaking Part 3 & The A.R.E.A. Framework

Moving from personal narrative to abstract societal discourse. Eliminating pauses using A.R.E.A. (Answer directly → Reason → Example → Alternative).

🎯 Output: 6 fluent, structured responses without trailing off.
Week 2 · Argumentation Developing Depth & Contrast

Lesson 3: WT2 Agree / Disagree Essays

Task Response mastery: building deep single-thread body paragraphs with the I-E-E-R flow (Idea → Explanation → Example → Result).

🎯 Output: 1 complete body paragraph co-written to Band 7.5 cohesion.

Lesson 4: Speaking Part 3 · Comparing & Contrasting

Grammatical range upgrade: contrasting past eras with modern society and future projections ("Whereas in previous decades...").

🎯 Output: Rapid-fire drills contrasting demographics without hesitation.
Week 3 · Nuance Balancing Perspectives

Lesson 5: WT2 "Discuss Both Views" Prompts

Strategic structuring for two-sided essays: managing counter-arguments in Body 1 and transitioning to your preferred argument in Body 2.

🎯 Output: Detailed outlines and bridging transitions for 2 complex prompts.

Lesson 6: Speaking Part 3 · Handling the Unknown

High-level stall and hedge techniques when asked unexpected abstract topics. Modals of deduction and speculative language.

🎯 Output: Zero dead pauses when confronted with unforeseen questions.
Week 4 · Complexity C1 Grammar & Vocabulary

Lesson 7: WT2 Problem & Solution Essays

Cause-and-effect essays with high-yield Band 7 grammar: conditionals, cleft sentences, passive voice, and reduced relative clauses.

🎯 Output: Transforming basic draft sentences into cohesive C1 structures.

Lesson 8: Speaking Part 3 · Upgrading Lexis

Eliminating "crutch" words (good, bad, a lot). Building personal banks of topic-specific C1 collocations and natural paraphrasing.

🎯 Output: Active deployment of 25 high-yield C1 academic collocations.
Week 5 · Pressure Compound Prompts & Stamina

Lesson 9: WT2 Two-Part & Direct Questions

Handling compound questions where candidates frequently miss half the prompt. Rapid 3-minute planning drill for equal weighting.

🎯 Output: Flawless outlining across 4 tricky compound test prompts.

Lesson 10: Speaking Part 3 · 15-Minute Simulation

Continuous 15-minute Part 3 stamina pressure test with zero interruptions to pinpoint the exact moment linguistic fatigue sets in.

🎯 Output: Diagnosed linguistic stamina breakdown point and repair strategies.
Week 6 · Exam Readiness Full Mocks & Final Polish

Lesson 11: WT2 40-Minute Simulation & Live Edit

Student writes an unseen Task 2 essay live under strict 40-minute conditions, followed by immediate line-by-line surgical editing.

🎯 Output: Full Band 7.0 standard essay completed in 40 minutes.

Lesson 12: Full Speaking Mock & Test Routine

Strict 14-minute full Speaking mock (Parts 1, 2, and 3) under official Cambridge timing, complete criterion feedback, and test-morning routine.

🎯 Output: Final verified score benchmark and psychological readiness.

How We Adapt the Plan to Your Profile

During our initial 30-minute Clinical Diagnostic & Roadmap Session (£35), I categorize each student into one of two primary operational profiles:

  • Profile A: The Native-Level but Unstructured Student. They already possess the vocabulary and grammar for a 7.0+, but fail to answer the specific question in Writing, run out of time, or drift off-topic in Speaking. Course emphasis shifts to strict adherence to band descriptors, essay outlining, and time management.
  • Profile B: The 6.0 Ceiling Student. They know the test format thoroughly, but lack the less common lexical items, accurate collocations, and error-free complex sentences required for a 7.0. Course emphasis shifts to high-yield grammar structures (conditionals, clefts, inversions) and topic-specific vocabulary banks.
