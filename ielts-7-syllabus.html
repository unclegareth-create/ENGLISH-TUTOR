Example Scheme of Work: 6-Week Intensive IELTS 7.0 Syllabus

A generic syllabus is an admission of failure. If an IELTS course promises that every student will march through the exact same textbook page-by-page across six weeks, run in the opposite direction.

The Flipped Classroom Principle: No Passive Time-Wasting

If you are paying a tutor to sit silently across from you while you read a 900-word passage about honeybees or listen to an audio track, you are burning your tuition money. When you have six to eight weeks before test day, general English lessons cannot move the needle.

My courses run on an uncompromising flipped-mastery principle:

  • Live 1-on-1 Sessions (2 hours per week): Reserved purely for high-stakes active output and real-time correction. We pull apart unseen prompts, perform live red-pen mark-ups on your paragraphs, and run high-tempo Speaking Part 3 drills until your analytical reasoning becomes second nature.
  • Strict At-Home Regimen: Reading and Listening papers are completed strictly at home under exam timer conditions using official Cambridge IELTS Academic Practice Tests (Books 16–19). You score them yourself, diagnose your error patterns, and bring only your unsolved queries to our desk. Writing Task 1 reports are reviewed asynchronously with detailed annotated feedback.

Band 6.0 vs Band 7.0: Where the Marks Are Won and Lost

Candidates stuck at Band 6.0 or 6.5 rarely have a vocabulary deficit. What keeps them trapped below the line is a cluster of unexamined habits: repeating personal anecdotes in speaking, writing flat, predictable sentences in essays, and leaning on clunky memorised templates:

Criterion Band 6.0 Plateau Band 7.0 Requirement
Writing Task Response Lists multiple superficial ideas without deep elaboration; position is somewhat unclear or wavers. Addresses all parts of the task with a sustained, clear position. Develops 2 main arguments deeply with logical explanations and concrete examples.
Writing Cohesion Overuses mechanical linkers ("Firstly, Secondly, Furthermore, In a nutshell"); repetitive referencing. Manages paragraph transitions flexibly; uses substitution and referencing without drawing undue attention to linking devices.
Speaking Fluency Speaks at length but shows noticeable hesitation searching for words; answers in Part 3 remain personal anecdotes. Speaks at length without noticeable effort. Moves effortlessly from personal stories to abstract societal analysis using the A.R.E.A. method.
Grammar & Accuracy Attempts complex sentences, but frequent systematic errors occur (e.g. articles, prepositions, verb tense). Produces a wide variety of complex sentence structures; achieves frequent error-free sentences.

How I Adapt the Plan to Your Profile

In your initial 30-minute diagnostic session (£35), I sit down with you to find out exactly why your score is stuck. Over the years, I have found that almost every plateaued candidate falls into one of two camps:

  • Profile A: The Fluent but Unstructured Candidate. They speak with natural speed, read complex material at work, and possess a rich vocabulary—yet their writing sits at 6.5. Why? Because they don't answer every part of the prompt, their paragraphs drift into storytelling, or they run out of time. The remedy here is discipline: rigorous paragraph architecture, prompt deconstruction, and strict timing habits.
  • Profile B: The Grammar-Conscious Candidate at the 6.0 Ceiling. They know the test format inside out, but their language is overly cautious and monotonous. Their writing relies on flat verb chains and basic "If" conditionals; their speaking is plagued by hesitant pauses while searching for "perfect" words. The remedy here is structural variety: cleft sentences, negative inversions, nominalisation, and learning to hedge naturally under exam pressure.

1 · Task 2 Depersonalisation Blueprint: It + BE + Adverb + Past Participle + that...

Upgrade this conversational Band 6.0 opener into an authoritative C1 depersonalised claim: "Lots of people think that university education should be free for everyone."

Cambridge C1 Model: “It is widely argued that tertiary education should be publicly funded for all citizens.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: Introductory It + passive reporting (It is widely argued that...) strips out informal personal pronouns and establishes an objective academic tone right off the bat.

2 · Task 2 Passive Reporting Blueprint: Subject + BE + believed/thought + to + Infinitive

Complete the C1 passive reporting sentence: "Ultra-processed convenience foods ______ a primary driver of childhood obesity."

Cambridge C1 Model: “Ultra-processed convenience foods are widely believed to be a primary driver of childhood obesity.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: Subject-fronted passive reporting (are believed to be / are thought to contribute to) demonstrates effortless control of C1 academic hedging.

3 · Task 1 Overview Precision Blueprint: Overall, while [Trend A], [Trend B] experienced a pronounced...

Which Paragraph 2 Overview sentence meets official Band 8.0 Academic register without tabloid exaggeration or raw data clutter?

Cambridge C1 Model: “Overall, while coal consumption contracted steadily throughout the period, renewable output experienced a pronounced upward trajectory.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: An Overview belongs in Paragraph 2, synthesises macro trends using a contrastive while clause, avoids exact numbers (save those for Body 3 & 4), and avoids sensationalist verbs like skyrocketed.

4 · Task 1 Preposition Precision Blueprint: Noun + accounted for + [%] + of + Total Noun

Choose the grammatically accurate Task 1 sentence describing a jump from 20% in 2010 to 35% in 2020:

Cambridge C1 Model: “Online retail rose by 15 percentage points to reach 35% in 2020, accounting for just over a third of total sales.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: Distinguishes between rose by [margin: 15%] and rose to [endpoint: 35%], and uses the participle clause , accounting for... accurately.

5 · Speaking Part 2 Preparation Blueprint: The 1-Minute Note Secret (4 Trigger Keywords)

When the examiner hands you the Part 2 Cue Card and gives you 60 seconds of preparation time, what is the highest-scoring way to use your notepad?

Gareth's 1-Minute Note Secret: 4 Single Trigger Keywords Down the Margin.

Why it scores Band 7.5+: Writing full sentences consumes 45 seconds and causes you to read aloud in a robotic monotone before crashing at the 20-second mark. Four single trigger nouns keep your eyes up, your intonation natural, and your mind focused on storytelling.

7 · C1 Grammatical Range · Clefts Blueprint: What + Clause + BE + Noun Phrase / Clause

Transform this flat sentence into a high-impact C1 Wh- Cleft: "The sheer lack of affordable public transport really frustrates rural commuters."

Cambridge C1 Model: “What frustrates rural commuters most is the sheer lack of affordable public transport.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: A What-cleft (What + Verb Phrase + is + Focused Noun Phrase) acts like a spotlight on your topic sentence or concluding thought without sounding forced.

8 · C1 Grammatical Range · Inversion Blueprint: Not only + Auxiliary + Subject + Base Verb, but... also...

Select the grammatically flawless Negative Inversion for a Band 7.5+ Task 2 body paragraph:

Cambridge C1 Model: “Not only do congestion charges reduce inner-city traffic, but they also generate vital revenue for public transit.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: Placing a negative adverbial (Not only / Rarely / Seldom) at the start of a sentence triggers question-word-order inversion (do + plural subject + bare infinitive). One well-placed inversion per essay is enough to catch the examiner's eye.

9 · C1 Grammatical Range · Participle Clause Blueprint: Main Clause + , thereby + Present Participle (-ing)

Replace the clunky "and this makes..." in this cause-and-effect chain: "Municipal councils can pedestrianise historic town centres, ______ local footfall and reducing air pollution."

Cambridge C1 Model: “Municipal councils can pedestrianise historic town centres, thereby fostering local footfall and reducing air pollution.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: A resultative participle clause (, thereby + -ing) fuses action and consequence into a single fluid C1 sentence without repeating subject pronouns.

10 · Speaking Part 3 A.R.E.A. Hedging Blueprint: Assertion (Hedged) → Reason → Evidence → Alternative

Examiner asks: "Will physical cash completely disappear in the next decade?" Which opening Assertion sets up a Band 8.0 A.R.E.A. response?

Cambridge C1 Model: “That is a tough one to generalise about, but on balance, while urban centres are already virtually cashless, I doubt physical notes will vanish entirely within ten years.”

Why it scores Band 7.5+: Buys thinking time naturally (That's a tough one to generalise about), avoids personal family stories, and sets up a nuanced demographic contrast ready for Reason and Evidence.
