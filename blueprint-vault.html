1. The 4 Causative Formulas (Learn the Pattern)

Formulas & Examples

In advanced English, you rarely say "Someone did X for me". Use these 4 precise structures instead:

1. Causative Passive (Commissioned Service)

You arranged for someone else to do a job:

Subject + have/get + Object + Past Participle (V3)

"We had the security cameras upgraded."

2. Experiencer Passive (Unwanted Mishap)

Something bad happened to you outside your control:

Subject + had + Object + Past Participle (V3)

"The firm had its confidential ledger stolen."

3. Reflexive Agency with 'Get'

Your own actions caused the resulting situation:

Subject + got + myself/himself + Past Participle (V3)

"He argued with the chair and got himself dismissed."

4. Depersonalised Reporting (Formal Register)

Report claims with objective academic distance:

Subject + is/are + believed/alleged + to have + V3

"The treasurer is believed to have siphoned off funds."

2. Financial Vocabulary: Formal Essays vs Spoken Chat

Register Contrast

Do not mix informal idioms into formal Task 2 essays or report writing. Use the correct register for the task:

Category Formal Register (Essays & Reports) Spoken Register (Speaking Exam Only)
Low Income deprived, impoverished, destitute hard up, strapped for cash, on the breadline
High Income affluent, prosperous, well-off loaded, rolling in it, well-heeled
Spending Money allocate funds, invest in, incur costs fork out, shell out, splash out on
Wasting Money squander resources, mismanage capital fritter away, throw money down the drain

3. Practice: Key Word Transformations (3 to 6 Words)

Cambridge Part 4 Format

Complete the second sentence so it means the same as the first. Do not change the keyword. Use between 3 and 6 words:

1. "A master locksmith is replacing our strongroom bolts tomorrow," confirmed the manager.
HAVING 0 WORDS (3–6 REQ)
The manager confirmed that they were tomorrow.
2. A thief stole the diplomat's encrypted laptop while he was boarding the train.
HAD 0 WORDS (3–6 REQ)
The diplomat while boarding the train.
3. Auditors believe that the chief financial officer concealed substantial debt.
BELIEVED 0 WORDS (3–6 REQ)
The chief financial officer substantial debt.
4. Although money was tight, they spent extravagantly on a luxury boardroom desk.
SPLASH 0 WORDS (3–6 REQ)
The partners a luxury boardroom desk.

Cue Card Challenge

  • What options you had to weigh up
