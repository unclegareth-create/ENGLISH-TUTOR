Part 01

15 C1/C2 Grammar Patterns at a Glance

Each lesson in the course isolates one high-value grammar pattern from a classic Gothic novel and shows you how to use it naturally in Cambridge C1/C2 and IELTS Academic writing.

Lesson Grammar Focus Classic Formula Modern Essay / Report Example
Lesson 1
Dracula I		 Participle Clauses Having + past participle, [Subject]...
[Past participle] by..., [Subject]...		 Having reviewed the evidence, the committee approved the reform.
Lesson 2
Dracula II		 Negative Inversion Never / Seldom / Little + aux + S + V
Hardly... when / No sooner... than		 Seldom has a technological shift generated such fierce debate.
Lesson 3
Dracula III		 Impersonal Reporting Passives Subject + is believed/thought + to have + past participle The policy is widely believed to have caused the shortage.
Lesson 4
Frankenstein I		 Inverted & Mixed Conditionals Had + S + past participle, S + would (now) + V
Were it not for + noun...		 Had governments invested earlier, public transport would be far more reliable today.
Lesson 5
Frankenstein II		 Contrast Fronting & Subjunctive Adj + though/as + S + verb...
insist/demand that + S + bare infinitive		 Ambitious though the proposal is, the panel insisted that it be revised.
Lesson 6
Frankenstein III		 Cleft Sentences for Focus It was [X] that...
What [V] was... / All [S] wanted was...		 It is chronic underinvestment, rather than lack of interest, that holds back rural areas.
Lesson 7
Jekyll & Hyde I		 Concessive Clauses & Collocations Much as + S + V...
show a callous disregard for...		 Much as consumers welcome low prices, fast fashion carries a heavy environmental cost.
Lesson 8
Jekyll & Hyde II		 Not until & So + Adj Inversion Not until [X] did + S + V
So [adj] was [S] that...		 Not until the audit was published did the scale of the losses become clear.
Lesson 9
Jekyll & Hyde III		 Time Clefts & Preposition Collocations It was only when [X] that [Y]
extricate oneself from / succumb to		 It was only when inflation peaked that households began cutting essential spending.
Lesson 10
Baskervilles I		 Past Modals of Deduction must / might well / cannot + have + past participle The breach cannot have been caused by a simple software glitch alone.
Lesson 11
Baskervilles II		 Place & Participle Inversion [Place phrase] + stood/lay + Subject
[Participle phrase] + stood + Subject		 At the heart of the controversy lies a fundamental disagreement over privacy.
Lesson 12
Baskervilles III		 But for & Evidence Verbs But for / Had it not been for + noun, S + would have + V But for the emergency grant, several regional hospitals would have closed.
Lesson 13
Wonderland I		 Negative & Restrictive Inversion Never before had S + V3 / Only by + -ing + aux + S + V Only by overhauling municipal energy grids can cities achieve decarbonisation.
Lesson 14
Wonderland II		 Inverted Conditionals & Past Subjunctive Were + S + to + V1 / It is high time + S + past simple It is high time organisations re-evaluated arbitrary meeting schedules.
Lesson 15
Wonderland III		 Passive Reporting & Mandative Subjunctive is alleged to have + V3 / demand that + S + bare infinitive The committee recommends that every unsubstantiated claim be reviewed independently.
Part 02

3-Way Vocabulary Filter: Modern C1/C2, Literary/Stylistic & Period Words

Each lesson sorts vocabulary into three registers: Modern C1/C2 (use actively in essays and speaking), Literary / Stylistic (formal narrative/rhetorical words), and Archaic / Period (recognise when reading 19th-century texts, avoid in modern essays).

Module Use in Modern C1/C2 Writing & Speaking (+ Literary/Stylistic) Recognise When Reading (Archaic / 19th-Century Period Only)
Module I: Dracula apprehensive, impenetrable, sinister, formidable, unaccountably, derelict, disclose (Literary: frowning rocks, countenance) calèche, lofty steeps, perforce, coxswain, bight
Module II: Frankenstein catastrophe, delineate, unquenchable, inexorable, abhorrence, ostracise, repudiate (Literary: spurn, hovel) charnel-house, begone!, thou / thy, succour
Module III: Jekyll & Hyde juggernaut, callous, facade, incongruous, duplicity, pernicious, extricate (Literary: imperious) sawbones, brougham, hansom, cheval-glass
Module IV: Baskervilles plausible, circumstantial, quagmire, treacherous, corroborate, fallacy (Literary: scion, melancholy, audacious) wicket-gate, wagonette, yonder, ulster, dewlap
Module V: Wonderland incongruous, disorienting, arbitrary, futile, unsubstantiated, intimidate (Literary: precipitous, perpetual, preposterous) waistcoat-pocket, antipathies, between whiles, tea-things, stuff and nonsense, herald
