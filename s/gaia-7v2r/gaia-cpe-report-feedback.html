Four Major Wins in This Draft

1. Textbook Negative Inversion

"Not only does Ambrosia boast a wide range of technological devices at students’ disposal..., but it also provides..."

Examiners actively scan C2 reports for emphatic negative inversion. You placed the auxiliary does before the subject effortlessly and paired it with boast (a top-tier C2 verb for institutional facilities).

2. Passive Reporting & Subject Elevation

"...the library is thought to lack spaces where..."

Instead of writing "Students think that the library lacks...", you elevated the subject into an impersonal passive reporting frame (is thought to + infinitive). That is pure Cambridge C2 report register.

3. Resultative Participle & Gerund Subjects

"Including such a tool as part of the library’s equipment means finally accounting for..." • "...thereby making..."

Opening a sentence with a gerund clause (Including such a tool... means accounting for...) and closing the report with a , thereby + -ing resultative participle gives your prose mature syntactic rhythm.

4. Impersonal Institutional Tone & Pacing

"In light of the numerous recommendations..., it would be highly advisable to allocate funds..."

Zero personal "I think" statements, clear subheadings, and survey findings reported with a sense of proportion ("Several...", "83%...", "A third..., albeit a minority"). You clearly understand what a CPE report is for.

Four Surgical Adjustments Before Exam Day

Your sentence structure is already at C2 level. What holds the Language and Communicative Achievement marks back is word choice: a few words that don't mean in English what you want them to, two agreement slips, and a handful of emotive words (incredible, passionately, truly revolutionise) that sit oddly in a neutral report.

Watchpoint 1 · False Friends (Falsi Amici) Lexical Precision
  • Suggested implements → Suggested improvements / Recommended upgrades
    In English, implements (noun) means physical hand tools or farm/kitchen utensils (e.g. agricultural implements), never "things to implement".
  • library's frequenters / attendees → patrons / regular users / members
    Frequenters is a calque of frequentatori (rare in English except for "frequenters of bars"). Attendees is for a one-off event or conference. Use patrons or regular users. (Note: your passive verb "increasingly frequented by students" in para 3 is spot on!)
  • founded in 1860, thus being one of the most ancient... → oldest / most historic
    In formal writing, ancient suggests many centuries (ancient Rome, an ancient manuscript), so it overshoots for 1860. Use oldest. Also remember: historic = famous/important in history; historical = connected with studying the past.
  • overriding issues or lacks to be solved or filled → pressing issues or shortcomings to be addressed
    Lack isn't used as a plural noun (lacks = mancanze/lacune) in this way. Use shortcomings, deficiencies, or gaps in provision.
Watchpoint 2 · Quantifier Verb Agreement Grammar Rule

In Italian, collective singular phrases like un terzo degli studenti or un certo numero di... often take a singular verb. In formal British English, watch these two strict rules:

Rule A: A number of + plural noun + PLURAL verb

A number of attendees has shown interest →
"A number of regular users have expressed interest..."
(Only "The number of users has..." takes a singular verb.)

Rule B: Fractions / % + of + PLURAL noun + PLURAL verb

A third of the interviewed students, albeit a minority, has also advocated →
"A third of the students interviewed, albeit a minority, have also advocated..."

Watchpoint 3 · Academic Collocations & Register C2 Collocations
  • attending their degree in Architecture → pursuing / studying for a degree in Architecture
    You attend lectures or a university, but you pursue or undertake a degree.
  • advocated for the need of projectors → highlighted the need for projectors / advocated the installation of projectors
    The noun need always takes for (the need for projectors), and advocate in formal British English takes a direct object without for.
  • where colleagues may rehearse → where peers / fellow students may rehearse
    Colleagues is strictly for workplace co-workers; students have peers or fellow students.
  • implementation of technological gadgets → acquisition of specialist equipment / upgrading of digital facilities
    Avoid gadgets in formal reports (it sounds like small novelty toys), and remember we implement policies/measures, whereas we acquire or install equipment.
Watchpoint 4 · Name Consistency & Closing Cadence Final Polish
  • Keep Proper Names Identical: Your title says Ambrosiana Library, while the body says Ambrosia Library. Stick to the Ambrosiana Library throughout, and capitalise 3D printer.
  • Landing the Final Tricolon: Look at your closing line:
    "...making Ambrosia Library a quiet, instead of noisy, a cheerful, instead of stressful, and a dynamic, instead of boring, environment."
    Repeating "a ... a ... and a ..." before three adjectives tied to a single noun (environment) tangles the grammar. Move the adjectives after the noun and the pattern lands cleanly:
    "...thereby transforming the Ambrosiana into a place that is quiet rather than noisy, welcoming rather than stressful, and stimulating rather than dull."
  • Keep the Report Neutral: incredible benefits, passionately advocated, truly revolutionise the life of belong in an article or a speech. A report persuades with facts and measured verbs: considerable benefits, called for, make a real difference to.

Report on the self-study facilities at Ambrosiana Library

Introduction:
The principal aim of this report is to evaluate the current study spaces, technological devices and further equipment at the public’s disposal, while suggesting possible improvements to invest in. Several of the library’s frequenters have been interviewed to share their views on the overriding issues or lacks to be solved or filled.

Current provision:
Firstly, although Ambrosia Library was founded in 1860, thus being one of the most ancient libraries in Milan, it does not rely simply on its prestige to attract visitors from all over the country: its advanced technologies and its welcoming rooms have played their part. Not only does Ambrosia boast a wide range of technological devices at students’ disposal, namely computers, graphic calculators and interactive boards, but it also provides frequenters with an increasing number of study rooms, suitable for solo-study sessions as well as group projects.

Suggested implements:
However, interviewees have noted that, in a world where technological change is at its peak, historical and prestigious libraries should adapt their spaces and devices accordingly. A number of attendees has shown interest in the purchase of sound-proof rooms, fundamental to enable students with different needs to have the opportunity of joining the library. Moreover, 83% of the interviewees have mentioned the incredible benefits that a 3d printer would bring to Ambrosia, increasingly frequented by students attending their degree in Architecture and Design. Report on the Self-Study Facilities at the Ambrosiana Library

Introduction
The aim of this report is to evaluate the study spaces, technological devices and other equipment currently available to the public, and to suggest improvements worth investing in. Regular users of the library were interviewed about the most pressing shortcomings that need to be addressed.

Current Provision
Although the Ambrosiana was founded in 1860, making it one of the oldest libraries in Milan, it does not rely on prestige alone to attract visitors from across the country: its modern technology and welcoming rooms have also played their part. Not only does the library boast a wide range of devices for students' use, namely computers, graphic calculators and interactive whiteboards, but it also offers an increasing number of study rooms, suitable for individual study as well as group projects.

Suggested Improvements
However, interviewees felt that, at a time of rapid technological change, even historic and prestigious libraries should adapt their spaces and equipment accordingly. A number of users have expressed interest in soundproof rooms, which would allow students with different needs to make full use of the library. Moreover, 83% of those interviewed mentioned the considerable benefits a 3D printer would bring, given that the library is increasingly used by Architecture and Design students. Including such a tool would mean finally catering for those whose work is not merely theoretical. A third of the students, albeit a minority, also called for projectors, as the library is thought to lack spaces where students can rehearse presentations.

Conclusion
In light of these recommendations, it would be highly advisable to allocate funds to new technological equipment, so as to make the library a more flexible and inclusive space. Soundproof rooms, projectors and 3D printers could make a real difference to a great many students, thereby transforming the Ambrosiana into a place that is quiet rather than noisy, welcoming rather than stressful, and stimulating rather than dull.
