I subscribe to the view that outdoor advertising has become excessive, and I would go so far as to say that exposing vulnerable passers-by to inescapable commercial messages makes the practice ethically questionable.

All in all, outdoor advertising has certainly been transformed by digital innovation, yet the public appears to resent these new, tedious ways of communicating. Although digital screens may offer fresh creative possibilities, in practice they are preventing people from enjoying public spaces.

Four Final Watchpoints to Lock In Before Exam Day

Look closely at these four recurring patterns across your two tasks today—fixing them is straightforward and protects your Content, Language, Organisation, and Communicative Achievement marks.

1. Content Check: Tick Off Every Task Requirement Part 1 & Part 2 Content
  • Part 1 — Match the Exact Scope & Don't Invent Source Claims: Both passages are specifically about outdoor digital advertising (digital screens and billboards replacing street posters), yet your essay never uses the word digital. Conversely, you write "The claim that the increasingly overriding presence of AI and online tools..." as if Text 1 claimed that, when AI isn't in either text. Summarise only what the texts actually say, use their best hooks (e.g. that digital screens only attract a buzz because they are new, and that they represent "creeping commercialisation" targeting children and vulnerable adults), and use your own words rather than lifting phrases like "grab customers' attention".
  • Part 2 — Tick Off All 3 Sub-Questions: Question 4 asks you to (1) describe the situation, (2) explain the reasons for your decision, and (3) assess the future consequences of that decision. You covered (1), skipped (2) (why Mathematical Engineering at Milan specifically?), and kept (3) generic (how university affects anyone, rather than how this degree will affect your future). Always number the sub-questions 1–2–3 on the question paper before you start writing.
2. False Friends, Meaning Slips & Blended Idioms Part 1 Language
  • Instead, the second text highlights... → By contrast, / Conversely,
    In Italian, Invece links any two contrasting ideas. In English, Instead means "in place of an action that was NOT done" ("He didn't go; instead, he stayed home"). To contrast Text 1 and Text 2, use By contrast, or Conversely,.
  • deceivable & nerve-racking → deceptive / misleading & intrusive / irritating
    Deceivable means "gullible" (a person who can be tricked); practices that trick people are deceptive. Nerve-racking means causing acute anxiety or suspense (like waiting for exam results), whereas adverts that bother people are intrusive or irritating.
  • advertisings & exaggerated → advertising (uncountable) & excessive
    Advertising is strictly uncountable (use advertisements / adverts / campaigns for the plural). Where Italian uses esagerato for "too much / over the top", English uses excessive or overblown.
  • I would venture so far as to say → I would go so far as to say (or I would venture to say)
    Keep these two C2 expressions separate rather than blending them together.
3. Prepositions, Articles & Fixed Idioms Language
  • it is far from that → it is far from it
    The fixed English idiom after a comparison is always far from it ("although it may look like a walk in the park, it is far from it").
  • on the short run, unlike some believe → in the short term / run, contrary to what some believe
    Always in the short/long run, and because unlike is a preposition, a clause needs contrary to what some believe.
  • open your eyes on & in the modern society → open your eyes to & in modern society
    We open someone's eyes to something; and society in general takes zero article.
  • After racking my brains myself → After racking my brains
    Because my brains already contains the possessive, adding myself is redundant.
4. Paragraph Structure, Pronouns & British Terms Organisation & Register
  • No Double Summary in Part 1: Keep Paragraph 1 to 1–2 sentences introducing the shared topic and tension; save the specific points of Text 1 and Text 2 for Paragraphs 2 and 3.
  • No Postscript After the Conclusion in Part 2: In your letter, Para 4 sums up and then Para 5 adds your personal decision to study at Milan as an afterthought. Put your Milan decision + reasons in Para 4, and end in Para 5 with the future consequences for you.
  • Pronoun Consistency & British Terms: Addressing magazine readers as "you" works well—just change "How does one decide?" to "How do you decide?" so you don't jump from one to you. Use university (rather than college), science degrees (rather than scientific degrees), enrol (one l), and Yours faithfully, (lowercase f).
