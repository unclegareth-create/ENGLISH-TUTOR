Both texts examine the role of advertising in modern society and how markedly it differs from the past, though they take opposing views on whether that shift has benefited the public.

The first passage argues that advertising plays a crucial role in the present-day world, allowing brands to reach an exceptionally wide audience, and thus demanding both imagination and sensitivity from its creators. Today, however, the trend has reversed and omnipresent, deceptive advertising is widely perceived as unnecessary and frustrating. I subscribe to the view that campaigns have become excessive, and I would go so far as to say that, because algorithms reward speed and volume over craft, modern adverts increasingly lack originality and authenticity, making the practice questionable.

All in all, advertising has certainly been transformed by technology and innovation, yet the public appears to resent these new, tedious ways of communicating. Although digital tools may theoretically benefit creativity, in practice they are preventing people from enjoying advertisements.

Dear Editor,

I am writing in response to your recent column about making important decisions and would like to share my own experience. As an 18-year-old student, I am currently in my final year of secondary school, which means one thing above all: choosing a university.

Thousands of teenagers around the world are eventually compelled to reach a decision concerning their future and, although it may look like a walk in the park from the outside, it is far from it. Four Final Watchpoints to Lock In Before Exam Day

Look closely at these four recurring patterns across your two tasks today—fixing them is straightforward and protects your Language, Organisation, and Communicative Achievement marks.

1. False Friends, Meaning Slips & Blended Idioms Part 1
  • Instead, the second text highlights... → By contrast, / Conversely,
    In Italian, Invece links any two contrasting ideas. In English, Instead means "in place of an action that was NOT done" ("He didn't go; instead, he stayed home"). To contrast Text 1 and Text 2, use By contrast, or Conversely,.
  • deceivable & nerve-racking → deceptive / misleading & intrusive / irritating
    Deceivable means "gullible" (a person who can be tricked); practices that trick people are deceptive. Nerve-racking means causing acute anxiety or suspense (like waiting for exam results), whereas adverts that bother people are intrusive or irritating.
  • advertisings & exaggerated → advertising (uncountable) & excessive
    Advertising is strictly uncountable (use advertisements / adverts / campaigns for the plural). Where Italian uses esagerato for "too much / over the top", English uses excessive or overblown.
  • I would venture so far as to say → I would go so far as to say (or I would venture to say)
    Keep these two C2 expressions separate rather than blending them together.
2. Part 1 Essay Blueprint (No Double Summary) Organisation & Content

In your Part 1 essay, Paragraph 1 summarises Text 1 and Text 2, and then Paragraphs 2 and 3 summarise Text 1 and Text 2 a second time. Notice how the polished model fixes this:

Para 1 (1–2 sentences · ~30w): State the shared topic and the overarching tension between the two texts—do NOT list their specific arguments yet.

Para 2 (~95w): Summarise the two key claims of Text 1 in your own words + evaluate them, explaining why you agree or disagree.

Para 3 (~95w): Open with By contrast, summarise the two key claims of Text 2 + evaluate them with a concrete reason.

Para 4 (~35w): Final synthesis weighing both perspectives together.

3. Prepositions, Articles & Fixed Idioms Language
  • it is far from that → it is far from it
    The fixed English idiom after a comparison is always far from it ("although it may look like a walk in the park, it is far from it").
  • on the short run, unlike some believe → in the short term / run, contrary to what some believe
    Always in the short/long run, and because unlike is a preposition, a clause needs contrary to what some believe.
  • open your eyes on & in the modern society → open their eyes to & in modern society
    We open someone's eyes to something; and society in general takes zero article.
  • After racking my brains myself → After racking my brains
    Because my brains already contains the possessive, adding myself is redundant.
4. Pronoun Consistency, British Terms & Letter Ending Part 2
  • Keep Pronouns Consistent: In Para 2 you write "How does one decide?", and then in Para 3 you switch to "your future career... you are firstly picking". Pick one framing for your general reflection—such as students / they / their (or a student / one)—and stick with it consistently.
  • No Postscript After the Conclusion: In your draft, Para 4 sums up and then Para 5 adds your personal decision to study at the Polytechnic University of Milan as an afterthought. Put your Milan paragraph before your final reflective paragraph, and open the final paragraph with Ultimately, rather than the more essay-like To sum up,.
  • British Academic Terms & Sign-Off: In British English, college usually means sixth-form or further education (ages 16–18), so use university (choosing a university, science degrees rather than scientific degrees, and the Polytechnic University of Milan), spell enrol with one l, and write Yours faithfully, with a lowercase f.
