Five Final Watchpoints to Lock In Before Exam Day

Look closely at these five recurring patterns across your two tasks today—fixing them takes almost zero effort on exam day and protects your Language and Organisation marks immediately.

1. False Friends & Suffix Traps Part 1
  • Instead, the second text highlights... → By contrast, / Conversely,
    In Italian, Invece links any two contrasting ideas. In English, Instead means "in place of an action that was NOT done" ("He didn't go; instead, he stayed home"). To contrast Text 1 and Text 2, always use By contrast, or Conversely,.
  • omnipresent and deceivable practices → deceptive / misleading practices
    Deceivable means "capable of being tricked" (a gullible person). Practices that trick consumers are deceptive or misleading.
  • advertisings have become exaggerated → advertising (uncountable) / excessive
    Advertising (the industry/activity) is strictly uncountable (never advertisings); use advertisements / adverts / campaigns for the plural. And where Italian uses esagerato for "too much / over the top", English uses excessive, intrusive, or overblown.
2. Part 1 Essay Blueprint (No Double Summary) Organisation

In your Part 1 essay, Paragraph 1 summarises Text 1 and Text 2, and then Paragraphs 2 and 3 summarise Text 1 and Text 2 all over again. That costs Organisation marks for repetition. Use this clean 4-paragraph blueprint on the day:

Para 1 (2 sentences · ~40w): Introduce the shared theme + the general divide between the two texts (do NOT summarise the specific points yet).

Para 2 (~95w): Summarise the two key claims of Text 1 in your own words + evaluate them with your own view.

Para 3 (~95w): Open with By contrast, summarise the two key claims of Text 2 + evaluate them.

Para 4 (~35w): Final synthesis weighing both perspectives together.

3. Prepositions & Fixed Idioms Part 2
  • on the short run → in the short run / in the short term
    Always takes in (in the short run / in the long run).
  • unlike some believe → contrary to what some believe / unlike what many assume
    Unlike is a preposition, so it cannot be followed directly by a subject + verb clause without what.
  • open your eyes on the importance → open our eyes to the importance
    The idiom is always open someone's eyes to something.
  • in the modern society → in modern society
    When society means society in general (la società moderna), English never uses the.
4. Letter to the Editor Conventions Communicative Achievement
  • Keep Pronouns Consistent: In a Letter to the Editor, you are addressing the Editor and the magazine's readership—not lecturing an 18-year-old directly as "you". Where you used "How does one decide?" in Para 2, stay in that register: use one / a student / young people / we rather than switching to "your future career" and "you are firstly picking".
  • No Essay Signposts or Postscripts: Avoid "To sum up," in a letter (use Ultimately, or All things considered,), and never tack a 1-sentence personal story onto the very end after your conclusion. Tell your Milan story first, then close with your reflection.
  • British Sign-Off & Spelling: Capitalise only the first word of the sign-off (Yours faithfully,), and use British enrol (single l; enrolled/enrolling double the l).
