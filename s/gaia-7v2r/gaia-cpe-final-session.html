✍️ 1. The 1-Minute Note Secret

Write only 4 trigger words down the margin: one for the question, one for each idea. Never full sentences. In the exam you will start almost at once, so treat this minute as training for an instant habit.

  • Act 1 · Context (30s): your stance and a personal or cultural backdrop.
  • Act 2 · Core Scene (45s): one vivid, specific example you can see, hear, or taste.
  • Act 3 · Reflection (45s): the "so what?": why it matters, and what is at stake.

Part 3 · Organising Your 2-Minute Speech

C2 Sequencing & Discourse Signposts

At C2, examiners listen for natural signposts that guide the listener through your 2-minute monologue—never mechanical lists like "Firstly, secondly, in conclusion".

1 · Opening & Framing (0:00–0:30)
At first glance, ... yet on closer inspection...
  • • "When considering the question of..., one has to distinguish between..."
  • • "To my mind, the crux of the matter lies in..."
  • • "Where I would start is with the idea that..."
2 · Pivoting & Sequencing (0:30–1:25)
Closely bound up with this is + Noun Phrase
  • • "Turning first to the question of..."
  • • "Closely bound up with this is the wider issue of..."
  • • "Looked at from another angle, however,..."
  • • "That said, we cannot overlook the fact that..."
3 · Landing the 'So What?' (1:25–2:00)
Ultimately, what this comes down to is...
  • • "Ultimately, then, what this comes down to is..."
  • • "In the final analysis, unless we..."
  • • "Taken together, all of this points to one conclusion:..."
Live Sequencing Workout Formula: Turning first to... → Closely bound up with this is... → Ultimately, what this comes down to is...

Baseline: First, traditions help families bond. Second, regional food keeps local identity alive. Finally, globalisation shouldn't erase everything.

Cambridge Model: "Turning first to family celebrations, these shared rituals anchor us across generations. Closely bound up with this is regional food and craft, which give a town its unmistakable character. Ultimately, what this comes down to is keeping our roots alive without turning the past into a museum."

Your C2 Blueprints, From Memory

Say the formula aloud, then transform the baseline before you reveal.

1 · Cleft Sentences It is + Focus + that + Clause

Baseline: Constant comparison on social media erodes young people's self-esteem.

Focus on the cause.

"It is constant comparison on social media that erodes young people's self-esteem."
2 · Passive Reporting Subj + BE + V3 + to have + V3

Baseline: Experts estimate that the river has lost half of its fish population.

Begin: "The river..."

"The river is estimated to have lost half of its fish population."
3 · Participle Clauses Having + V3, + Main Clause

Baseline: She had read the reviews, so she was reluctant to see the film.

Front the prior action.

"Having read the reviews, she was reluctant to see the film."
Rapid Retrieval · 2 of 2

Inversion, Concession, Conditionals

Three well-placed shifts in an essay will do the work. Restraint reads as confidence.

4 · Emphatic Inversion Seldom / Rarely + Aux + Subj + Verb

Baseline: We seldom consider the hidden cost of convenience.

Begin: "Seldom..."

"Seldom do we consider the hidden cost of convenience."
5 · While Concession While + Concession, + Counter-Claim

Baseline: Tourism brings in revenue. It also puts pressure on local infrastructure.

One balanced sentence.

"While tourism undoubtedly brings in revenue, it places considerable strain on local infrastructure."
6 · Advanced Conditionals Had + Subj + V3, + would + be + -ing (now)

Baseline: I didn't accept the scholarship, so I'm not living in Edinburgh now.

Inverted mixed conditional.

"Had I accepted the scholarship, I would be living in Edinburgh now."
Reading & Use of English · Part 4

Key Word Transformations: The Rules of the Game

Six items in the exam, two marks each. Your answer is 3–8 words, including the key word. The key word never changes its form. Contractions count as two words.

STEP 1

Strike Out the Echoes

Cross out what Sentence 2 already gives you. What remains is the exact meaning you must rebuild.

STEP 2

Tense Harmony

Name the tense of Sentence 1. It dictates the tense and aspect inside your gap.

STEP 3

Voice Shift

Has active become passive, or the reverse? Check who does what to whom.

STEP 4

Collocation / Pattern

Fit the missing meaning into the fixed phrase or structure the key word demands.

Worked Example

I'd prefer you not to mention this to anyone.

RATHER

I mention this to anyone.

1 · Echoes: I 'd prefer you not to mention this to anyone → missing: prefer + you + not.

2 · Tense: present preference about another person's action.

3 · Voice: no shift; the subject is still "I".

4 · Pattern: would rather + Subj + past simple

Answer: I would RATHER you did not mention this to anyone. (5 words · "didn't" also = 5)
