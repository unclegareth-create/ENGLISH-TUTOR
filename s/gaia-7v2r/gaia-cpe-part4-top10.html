What Past Papers Actually Test in C2 Part 4

An audit of 87 official & practice Part 4 items (including 8 full tests from Cambridge English Proficiency past-paper books + Official Sample Paper 1) shows a clear truth: over 60% of C2 transformations test Nominalisation (Verb/Adjective → Noun + Preposition) and Fixed Idiomatic Collocations—often layered over a passive or grammar shift.

1 · Strike Out the Echoes
Cross out every word from Sentence 1 that is already accounted for in Sentence 2. Isolate the exact missing meaning.
2 · Check for Noun Shift
At C2, verbs & adjectives regularly become nouns: resemble → bear no resemblance to; clear → a lack of clarity.
3 · Check Prepositions
The second mark almost always depends on the exact preposition + gerund: prospect of being, restricted to those who.
4 · Tense & Passive Layer
Even when the key word is an idiom or phrasal verb, check whether Sentence 2 has flipped into the passive.
📊 Frequency Across Official Cambridge Papers
  • #1 Nominalisation & Noun + Preposition (~35–49%): no intention of, little prospect of, bore no resemblance to, came as a surprise to
  • #2 Fixed Idioms & Expressions (~25–40%): taken into account, at a complete loss, to the best of my knowledge, only time will tell
  • #3 Phrasal Verbs & Dependent Prepositions (~16%): come up with, put off until, taken in by, restricted to, dismissed as
  • #4 C2 Grammar Shifts (~25%): But for + noun, Providing there is no…, At no time was there…, On no account are…, It was not until… that, is rumoured to have sustained
🎯 How the 2 Marks Work (The | Split)
Every Part 4 answer is divided into two halves worth 1 mark each (shown as | in the official key). Even if you aren't 100% sure of one preposition, write the rest of the structure cleanly—you still collect 1 of the 2 marks.
Pattern 1 of 10 • #1 Most Tested C2 Family (Part A)

Nominalisation: Negative Noun Frames (no / little + noun + prep)

Blueprintbear no resemblance to  |  show no sign of + noun  |  have no intention of + -ing
Cambridge loves turning a negative verb or adjective (didn't look like, didn't seem nervous, doesn't intend) into a verb + no + abstract noun + preposition.
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 1 Q29
My father said that the portrait did not look like him.
RESEMBLANCE
My father said that the portrait him.
0 words
My father said that the portrait bore no | resemblance to him. (4 words)
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 2 Q26
Katy didn't seem nervous, even though she was making her speech without notes.
SIGN
Katy even though she was making her speech without notes.
0 words
Katy showed no sign | of nervousness even though she was making her speech without notes. (5 words · gave no sign also accepted)
Pattern 2 of 10 • #1 Most Tested C2 Family (Part B)

Nominalisation: Probability, Demand & Obligation

Blueprintsee little prospect of + -ing  |  under no obligation to + V  |  little demand for
Watch the preposition after probability nouns: little prospect of (being able to), what are Tom's chances of + -ing, every likelihood that + clause.
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 4 Q27
Sam doesn't think he'll be able to come with us at the weekend.
PROSPECT
Sam sees to come with us at the weekend.
0 words
Sam sees little prospect | of being able to come with us at the weekend. (5 words · of him/his being able = 6)
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 3 Q26
The owner of the shop said that we didn't have to buy anything; we could just look round.
OBLIGATION
The owner of the shop said that we a purchase; we could just look round.
0 words
…said that we were under no | obligation to make a purchase. (6 words · Notice buy → make a purchase!)
Pattern 3 of 10 • Nominalisation (Part C)

Clause-to-Noun Transformations & Abstract Nouns

Blueprint[Person's] + noun + came as a surprise to  |  There was a lack of + abstract noun
When Sentence 1 has a clause like "Nobody expected Natalia to resign" or "was not very clear", turn the verb/adjective into a noun: resignation, decision to retire, clarity.
Official Sample Paper 1 · Q28
Nobody expected Natalia to resign.
CAME
Natalia's everyone.
0 words
Natalia's resignation came | as a surprise to everyone. (6 words · decision to resign came as a surprise to = 8)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 1 Q27
What the lecturer said was not very clear at times.
LACK
There in what the lecturer said at times.
0 words
There was a lack of | clarity in what the lecturer said at times. (5 words)
Pattern 4 of 10 • #2 Most Tested C2 Family (Part A)

Fixed Idioms & Prepositional Phrases (Often Passive)

Blueprintbe taken into account  |  be at a (complete) loss to understand  |  be put in charge of
Notice how account and charge are tested inside a passive frame when the object moves to the front of Sentence 2.
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 1 Q25
I hope the committee will consider this new information when they meet next week.
ACCOUNT
I hope this new information when the committee meet next week.
0 words
I hope this new information will be taken | into account when the committee meet next week. (5 words)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 4 Q29
I have no idea why my email bounced back.
LOSS
I why my email bounced back.
0 words
I am at a loss | to understand why my email bounced back. (6 words · at a complete loss to know also accepted)
Pattern 5 of 10 • #2 Most Tested C2 Family (Part B)

High-Frequency C2 Set Expressions

Blueprintto the best of one's knowledge  |  have no choice but to + V  |  take no notice of
Other official C2 favourites in this group: only time will tell, there is no telling how long, at the height of his success.
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 4 Q30
The professor said that, as far as he knows, the results of the research are accurate.
BEST
The professor said that the results of the research are accurate.
0 words
The professor said that to the best | of his knowledge the results of the research are accurate. (6 words)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 2 Q29
Although she didn't agree with the management's decision, Chloe was forced to accept it.
CHOICE
Much as she disagreed with it, Chloe the management's decision.
0 words
Much as she disagreed with it, Chloe had no choice | but to accept the management's decision. (6 words · was left with no choice other than to accept = 8)
Pattern 6 of 10 • Phrasal Verbs in C2 Frames

Phrasal Verbs Layered With Passives or Nouns

Blueprinthave no difficulty (in) coming up with  |  let yourself be taken in by  |  be put off until
At C2, a phrasal verb is rarely tested alone—it is usually tucked inside a passive (be taken in by, be singled out for, be put off until) or after a noun (difficulty coming up with).
Official Past Paper · CPE 2 Test 1 Q26
James did not find it difficult to answer the interviewer's questions.
COMING
James had no the interviewer's questions.
0 words
James had no difficulty coming up | with answers to the interviewer's questions. (6 words · difficulty in coming up with the answers to = 8)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 3 Q25
Don't let Sarah's carefree attitude deceive you; she's an extremely conscientious worker.
TAKEN
Don't let Sarah's carefree attitude; she's an extremely conscientious worker.
0 words
Don't let yourself | be taken in by Sarah's carefree attitude. (5 words)
Pattern 7 of 10 • Dependent Prepositions & Verb Patterns

Verb & Adjective + Dependent Preposition Patterns

Blueprintbe restricted to + noun/those who  |  have any objection to (my) + -ing  |  be dismissed as
Trap alert: in have any objection to, to is a preposition, so it must be followed by an -ing form (to my watching), never an infinitive.
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 2 Q26
Visitors can only enter the exhibition if they have booked online.
RESTRICTED
Entrance have booked online.
0 words
Entrance to the exhibition is restricted to | those who have booked online. (8 words · visitors/people who = 8)
Official Sample Paper 1 · Example 0
Do you mind if I watch you while you paint?
OBJECTION
Do you you while you paint?
0 words
Do you have any objection | to my watching you while you paint? (6 words)
Pattern 8 of 10 • #1 Most Tested Grammar Pattern

C2 Conditionals: But for + Noun Phrase & Providing

BlueprintBut for + [possessive + noun phrase], S + would have + V3  |  Providing (that) there is no + noun
Notice how C2 combines a conditional with nominalisation: "If Stevie hadn't acted promptly" becomes "But for Stevie's prompt action in…"
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 2 Q27
If Stevie hadn't acted promptly to put out the fire, there might have been more damage to the kitchen.
BUT
out the fire, there might have been more damage to the kitchen.
0 words
But for Stevie's prompt action | in putting out the fire, there might have been more damage. (7 words)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 4 Q25
Unless Sam's plans change over the weekend, we'll leave early on Monday morning.
NO
Providing over the weekend, we'll leave early on Monday morning.
0 words
Providing there is no change | to Sam's plans over the weekend… (7 words · in Sam's plans also accepted)
Pattern 9 of 10 • Negative & Limiting Inversion

Negative & Limiting Inversion at C2

BlueprintAt no time + was there + noun  |  On no account + are + S + allowed  |  Only as a last resort + does + S
When a negative or limiting phrase opens the sentence (At no time, On no account, Only in the last resort), invert the auxiliary and subject—and watch for a noun or passive shift at the same time.
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 4 Q27
The role played by the PR company in securing the government contract was never acknowledged officially.
OFFICIAL
At no time the role the PR company played in securing the government contract.
0 words
At no time was there any official | acknowledgement of the role the PR company played. (6 words)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 4 Q28
Passengers are absolutely forbidden to cross the railway track.
ACCOUNT
On to cross the railway track.
0 words
On no account | are passengers allowed to cross the railway track. (5 words · permitted also accepted)
Pattern 10 of 10 • Clefts & Passive Reporting

Cleft Sentences & Passive Reporting + Collocation

BlueprintIt was not until [clause] that…  |  The last thing I want is for…  |  is rumoured to have sustained losses of
In CPE, passive reporting rarely tests to have + V3 alone—it also tests a formal collocation like sustain a loss / losses of.
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 1 Q28
It was only when it got dark that Paolo decided to make his way back home.
FELL
It was not that Paolo decided to make his way back home.
0 words
It was not until | darkness fell that Paolo decided to make his way back home. (3 words)
Official Past Paper · CPE Test 3 Q28
There is a rumour that the company lost over $20 million during the price war.
SUSTAINED
The company is rumoured over $20 million during the price war.
0 words
The company is rumoured to have sustained | losses of over $20 million during the price war. (5 words · a loss of = 6)
