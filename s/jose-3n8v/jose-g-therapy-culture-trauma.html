Stage 02 // Receptive Reading

Read the briefing below and answer the comprehension question.

📰 Psychology & Culture Digest
The Rise of 'Therapy-Speak': Are We Overusing Psychiatric Words?
Open Original Article ↗

Over the last few years, psychological words have moved from the doctor's office into our everyday chats, text messages, and workplace emails. Words that used to have strict medical definitions—like 'trauma', 'narcissist', 'gaslighting', 'safe space', and 'boundary'—are now used casually by everyone.

While talking openly about mental health has helped break old stigmas, many therapists are worried about what is called 'therapy-speak'. When someone uses a clinical term like 'trauma response' to explain simply being rude or unpunctual, or calls a regular argument 'emotional abuse', it can confuse normal human friction with real clinical harm.

In a culture where having emotional wounds can sometimes feel like a badge of honour on social media, experts remind us that the goal of therapy is healing and resilience—not staying stuck in the role of a permanent victim.

1. According to the text, what is the main problem when people use serious psychiatric words for everyday disagreements?
Model Summary: It blurs the line between real clinical conditions and normal human stress, making it easy for people to use emotional labels as an excuse rather than learning to resolve normal conflicts.

Stage 06 // Communicative Production

Executive Debate • Point & Counterpoint

Select your initial stance on each proposition and defend it. Then switch to ⚡ Devil's Advocate and argue the opposite view.

PROPOSITION 01
“Calling everyone you disagree with 'toxic' or a 'narcissist' makes it harder to learn real communication skills.”
Executive Follow-Up Angle: Have you seen people use these labels to end an argument early, instead of explaining what actually upset them?
PROPOSITION 02
“There is a big difference between 'holding space' to listen to a friend and becoming their unpaid full-time therapist.”
Executive Follow-Up Angle: What is your best phrase or strategy for gently stepping back when a friend expects too much emotional support?
PROPOSITION 03
“Social media accounts that offer 30-second mental health diagnoses do more harm than good.”
Executive Follow-Up Angle: Do you find that clients who follow psychology influencers online are more curious, or do they hold too tightly to self-made labels?

• Preparatory 'It': "It is widely argued that..." / "It would be unreasonable to expect..."
• Objective Stance: "Evidence indicates that..." / "Critics contend that..."
• Evaluative Counter: "While on the surface it appears..., what actually determines..."
• Concessive Balance: "Compelling though that rationale may seem, in practice..."
