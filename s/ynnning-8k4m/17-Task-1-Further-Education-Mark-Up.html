Four Habits to Fix for Your Band 7.5 Cushion

These four mechanical adjustments will eliminate every red mark across your Task 1 reports.

Habit 1 · Academic Numbers vs Informal 'k'

Rule: In academic writing, never use internet or text shorthand like 1000k, 850k, or 120k. Write figures out with commas or words.

  • 1000k → 1,000,000 or one million
  • 850k → 850,000
  • 1,100k → 1,100,000 or 1.1 million
  • 120k / 160k / 250k → 120,000 / 160,000 / 250,000

Writing the full numbers shows formal academic register and prevents Task 1 vocabulary penalties.

Habit 2 · Prepositions of Time: 'in' vs 'by'

Rule: Use in + [year] for a specific point on the chart. Reserve by + [year] for deadlines or cumulative endpoints.

  • started with 1000k by 1970 → started at 1,000,000 in 1970
  • dipped to 850k by 1980 → dipped to 850,000 in 1980
  • arrived at 1,100k at 1990 → reached 1,100,000 in 1990
  • Correct use of 'by': "overtaking their male counterparts by 1990" (cumulative outcome — this was correct!).
Habit 3 · Tense Discipline for Past Historical Data

Rule: When the chart dates are in the past (1970, 1980, 1990), use the Past Simple throughout. Avoid the present tense or present perfect.

  • S3: have grown → grew significantly over the period
  • S6: is only a fraction → was only a fraction

Only the introductory sentence describing what the chart currently displays takes present simple ("The chart shows...").

Habit 4 · Clause Coordination & Article 'A'

Rule: Don't leave verbs hanging after "There were" without a connecting subject, and remember Habit 1 from Essay #5 (a + adjective + singular noun).

  • S2: attracting a substantially larger number (Habit 1 from Essay #5)
  • S7: There were 120k male participants by 1970, steadily increased… → There were 120,000 male participants in 1970, a figure that steadily increased…

In S7, "steadily increased" lacked a grammatical subject. Adding "a figure that" turns it into a smooth C1 relative clause.

Style & Vocabulary Notes (Not Pure Errors)
  • Proportions vs Numbers: In S3, you wrote "The proportions of female students... overtaking the share". The chart shows raw headcounts in thousands, not percentages. Calling them "numbers" is more accurate for Task Achievement.
  • Gender nouns: In S6, you wrote "for both male and female". Male and female are adjectives here; write "for both men and women" or "for both male and female students".
  • Spelling: S5 had a small slip: stready → steady.
