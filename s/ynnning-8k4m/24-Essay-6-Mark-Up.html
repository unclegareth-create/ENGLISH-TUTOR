Four Habits from Essay #6: Your 2-Minute Check

None of these are gaps in your English—they are proofreading slips. Catching them turns 2 / 13 error-free sentences into 13 / 13.

Habit 1 · Topic-Word Spelling

Rule: Check high-frequency IELTS words once at the end—especially double letters and silent letters.

  • S4 & S13: emmision / emssions → emissions (one m, two s's)
  • S6: guarentee → guarantee (gua-, not gue-)
  • S8: catastropic → catastrophic (-phic, just like catastrophe from Essay #5)
Habit 2 · Plurals, Agreement & Uncountables

Formula: One + singular | infrastructure (uncountable) | carbon emissions (plural)

  • S2: Carbon emission … has → Carbon emissions … have
  • S5: One major disadvantages → One major disadvantage
  • S7: such as earthquake → such as earthquakes (general plural example)
  • S8: infrastructures → infrastructure (recurring from Essay #4—always uncountable!)
Habit 3 · Prepositions & Missing Link Words

Formula: need to + verb | during + event | as well as + -ing

  • S1: the need of addressing → the need to address
  • S2: attributed as → recognised as (we attribute an effect to a cause, not a cause as a driver)
  • S7: under unexpected events → during unexpected events
  • S13: as well mitigating → as well as mitigating
Habit 4 · Comparison Logic & Verb Forms

Formula: Compare like with like (the amount … than that of …); check past participles.

  • S1: has became → has become (become – became – become)
  • S3: the most and only realistic option → the most realistic, or even the only, option
  • S10: than many other green alternatives → than that of many other green alternatives
  • S11: has also → also has (adverb before main verb has)
Style Notes (Not Errors)
  • In S9 you wrote US spelling favor. In IELTS, either UK or US spelling is fine as long as you stay consistent across the whole essay (you used energy and no conflicting UK spellings, though favour matches your other essays).
  • In S6, decompose and seamless containment are understandable, though break down / neutralise and complete containment are more natural collocations.
  • In S8, suffer accidents or experience incidents sounds slightly more natural than cause incidents when the subject is infrastructure.
