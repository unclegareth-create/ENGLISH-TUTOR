📊 Part B Launch • Official IELTS Academic Writing Task 1

What the Exam Contains: The 6 Task 1 Question Types

Time: 20 Minutes • Length: Minimum 150 words (Target: 165–190 words) • Weight: 33% of Writing mark. As IELTS.org states: "It is an information-transfer task, which relates narrowly to the factual content of a diagram, graph, table, chart or map—not to speculative explanations."

1. Dynamic Charts (Over Time)
Line graphs, bar charts, tables, or pie charts across multiple years or hours of the day.
Focus: Trends (rose, peaked, declined) + comparisons & crossover points.
2. Static Charts (One Snapshot)
Bar charts, tables, or pie charts comparing categories at one point in time.
Focus: Highest/lowest rankings, multiples (twice as high as), and proportions.
3. Mixed / Multiple Charts
Two distinct charts on the same page (e.g., a Bar Chart + a Pie Chart or Table).
Focus: 1 overview sentence per chart; Body 1 = Chart 1, Body 2 = Chart 2.
4. Process Diagrams (Task 1C)
How something is made (e.g., Brick Manufacturing) or a natural cycle.
Focus: Number of steps in Overview; Present Simple Passive + sequencers.
5. Maps (Before & After)
Two plans of a town, museum floor, or island across two dates.
Focus: Compass directions (to the north-east) + passive changes (was expanded).
6. Tables
Rows and columns of figures (can show change over time or a static snapshot).
Focus: Selecting main patterns and outliers rather than listing every cell.

The 4-Paragraph Blueprint & Official Overview Descriptors

Look at the official British Council Task Achievement table (from Writing the Overview, Page 2):

Band Official British Council / Cambridge Task Achievement Descriptor
Band 7+ "It presents a clear overview, the data are appropriately categorised, and main trends or differences are identified."
Band 6 "A relevant overview is attempted." (e.g., incomplete or mixed up with raw figures/details).
Band 5 "There may be a tendency to focus on details (without referring to the bigger picture)."
Para 1 · Introduction
1 sentence (20–30w): Paraphrase what the graph shows in your own words.
Para 2 · Overview ⭐
2 sentences (35–45w): Start with Overall, or In general, — state the main trends with NO specific numbers.
Para 3 · Body Group A
3–4 sentences (50–60w): Category 1 details with exact figures & times.
Para 4 · Body Group B
3–4 sentences (50–60w): Category 2 details + direct comparisons/crossovers.
📈 Live Case Study • Official IELTS Academic Sample Task 1B

Official IELTS Task 1B: Radio & TV Audiences Throughout the Day

Official Prompt: "The graph below shows radio and television audiences throughout the day in 1992. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant."

Radio and television audiences in UK, October – December 1992
(% of UK population over 4 years old)
🔍 What the Official IELTS Examiner Looked For

On Page 11 of the IELTS Sample Booklet, Candidate A scored Band 6.0 because they listed numbers hour-by-hour without an overview.

Verbatim Examiner Comment:
"In order to gain a higher mark for content, the candidate would be expected to summarise the most important features of the graph in an overview, for example, 'audiences for radio peak in the morning, and for TV, in the evening'."

How to Group the Body Paragraphs:
• Body 1 (Radio): Morning surge to `~27%` at `8:00–8:30 AM`, gradual decline all afternoon/evening.
• Body 2 (Television): Low morning (`<10%`), overtakes radio around `1:00 PM` (`~15%`), surges to `~45%` peak at `8:00 PM`, plummets after `10:00 PM`.

✍️ Step-by-Step Build • Paragraphs 1 & 2 (Radio & TV Audiences)

Writing the Introduction & Overview for Sample Task 1B

British Council Overview Formula Overall, it is clear that [Category A trend/peak], whereas [Category B opposite trend/peak], with [Main Comparison].
Paragraph 1 · Complete Introduction
Examiner Tip (Page 12): Include the specific population group (over 4 years old) and timeframe (October to December 1992) from the chart labels!
"The line graph illustrates the proportion of the UK population aged over four who listened to the radio or watched television throughout a typical 24-hour period between October and December 1992."
Paragraph 2 · The Band 8.0 Overview (Zero `%`!)
Includes: (1) Radio morning peak vs. TV evening peak, and (2) TV attracting a larger maximum share overall.
"Overall, radio and television followed contrasting daily patterns: while radio audiences peaked in the morning, television viewing reached its highest point in the evening. Furthermore, television attracted a substantially larger maximum share of the population than radio."
✏️ Live British Council Overview Check (From Official Worksheet 3)
1. Which two discourse markers signal that Paragraph 2 is an overview rather than detail?
2. Should you ever put specific percentages (e.g., 27% or 45%) inside your Overview?
✓ 1. Signposting Markers: Overall, / In general, / Generally, / From an overall perspective,
✓ 2. Official British Council Answer: "No. The information in the overview should be general. You will write other paragraphs with more specific details and numbers."
📐 Body Paragraph Organisation • Grouping by Line (Not Hour-by-Hour)

Paragraphs 3 & 4: Complete Band 8.0 Body Paragraphs

Notice how grouping Radio in Body 1 and Television + the 1:00 PM Crossover in Body 2 makes the report crystal clear and keeps every sentence 100% error-free:

Paragraph 3 · Body 1: Radio Trajectory
"At 6:00 am, approximately 7% of the population tuned in to the radio, compared with almost no television viewers. Radio listening then rose sharply to peak at just under 30% around 8:30 am. Thereafter, the figure declined steadily across the remainder of the day, apart from a brief plateau at roughly 14% in the late afternoon, before falling below 5% after midnight."
✓ Spotless Accuracy: Past simple verbs throughout (tuned, rose, declined) + participle clause (..., before falling below 5%).
Paragraph 4 · Body 2: Television & The Crossover
"By contrast, television audiences remained below 10% throughout the morning before climbing to 15% at noon, overtaking radio listening shortly after 1:00 pm. Viewing figures then surged dramatically from 4:00 pm onwards, reaching a peak of roughly 45% at 8:00 pm. Attendance remained high until 10:00 pm, after which it dropped steeply to under 5% by 2:00 am."
✓ Spotless Accuracy: Highlights the `1:00 pm` crossover, the `45%` peak at `8:00 pm`, and the steep midnight drop.
