1 Passive Reporting & Concession Thesis
Introductory 'It'
✗ "Globalisation has changed how people work and live compared to the past generations. Many young adults nowadays..."
✓ "It is widely acknowledged that globalisation has fundamentally reshaped living and working patterns across generations..."
⭐ What You Did Well: You immediately set an academic tone and avoided personal stories. Your stance that advantages outweigh disadvantages is clearly stated.
🛠️ C1 Structural Move: Introductory 'It' + Passive Reporting ("It is widely acknowledged that...") followed by a Concession Clause ("While this trend creates undeniable challenges..., I firmly believe that the advantages substantially outweigh the disadvantages.").
Why it locks in 7.5: Rather than using a conversational opening, starting with "It is widely acknowledged that" instantly signals high-level grammatical control without risking difficult vocabulary.
2 Morphological Precision & Clear Topic Sentence
Accurate Suffix
✗ "The downsides of this trend are two folds."
✓ "The drawbacks of this outward migration are twofold."
⭐ What You Did Well: Clean paragraph boundary indicating that you are exploring the drawbacks first.
🛠️ The Quick Fix: "Twofold" is a compound adjective/adverb that never takes an plural '-s' (*twofold, threefold*). Also, replacing the generic word *"trend"* with *"outward migration"* keeps your vocabulary natural and precise.
3 Dummy 'It' Subject & Quantifier Agreement
Preparatory 'It'
✗ "First, having less productive young adults means that it is harder for small regional towns to remain economically competitive."
✓ "First, it is increasingly difficult for small regional towns to remain economically competitive when they lose talented young adults."
⭐ What You Did Well: Your economic reasoning is very strong—you correctly observed that small towns lose competitiveness when youth relocate.
🛠️ C1 Structural Move: Preparatory 'It' as Dummy Subject ("It is increasingly difficult for small towns to remain competitive...").
Why it locks in 7.5: Your draft had *"having less productive young adults"*, which grammatically requires *"fewer"* and accidentally sounded like remaining adults aren't productive! Using "it is increasingly difficult for small towns to... when they lose talented young adults" is clean, natural, and error-free.
4 Negative Inversion for Sophisticated Cohesion
Inversion
✗ "For example, if students with better grades or employees with more ambitious professional goals choose to leave the town, companies in small towns will be left with a less competitive batch of employment candidates."
✓ "When ambitious graduates and skilled workers leave, regional businesses are left with a depleted talent pool, making it harder to attract investment."
⭐ What You Did Well: Excellent concrete example supporting your economic argument.
🛠️ C1 Structural Move: Fronted Time Clause + Non-finite Result Clause ("When ambitious graduates leave, businesses are left with..., making it harder to attract investment.").
Why it locks in 7.5: Replaces conversational wording (*"students with better grades"*, *"batch of candidates"*) with natural, polished phrasing and a clean participial result (*", making it..."*).
5 'It'-Cleft Sentence for Dramatic Focus
Cleft Sentence
✗ "Second, there are also non-trivial societal impacts. Because of the geographical separation, there will be less time spent across generations. This might lead to loneliness..."
✓ "Furthermore, there are significant social consequences. It is this geographical separation that often weakens family bonds, leading to loneliness and reduced psychological resilience for both parents and young adults."
⭐ What You Did Well: Brilliant vocabulary choice with "psychological resilience"! That was one of the best phrases in your entire essay.
🛠️ C1 Structural Move: 'It'-Cleft Sentence ("It is this geographical separation that often weakens family bonds, leading to...").
Why it locks in 7.5: Instead of three short, choppy sentences (*"Second...", "Because of...", "This might..."*), a C1 cleft sentence highlights the cause with supreme grammatical elegance.
6 'Wh-' Cleft Sentence for Conceptual Impact
Wh- Cleft
✗ "However, there are also numerous advantages to this trend. One of the most attractive advantages is the degree of freedom given to young adults to design their lives..."
✓ "On the other hand, the advantages of urban migration are widely recognized. What makes moving to a major city particularly attractive is the freedom it affords young adults to shape their own futures."
⭐ What You Did Well: Clear topic sentence pivoting to the advantages, highlighting the personal growth and freedom of young adults.
🛠️ C1 Structural Move: Wh- Cleft Structure ("What makes moving to a major city particularly attractive is the freedom it affords...").
Why it locks in 7.5: The "What [verb] is..." cleft is one of the easiest, most impressive C1 structures to deploy in IELTS essays. It shows the examiner deliberate syntactic control.
7 Negative Inversion with Correlative Conjunction
Inversion
✗ "They can also meet peers from diverse backgrounds and inspire each other. Such interactions can broaden their horizons. There are also more career opportunities..."
✓ "Not only can students interact with peers from diverse backgrounds, which broadens their horizons, but they also gain access to specialized career opportunities that are simply unavailable in smaller towns."
⭐ What You Did Well: Excellent use of "broaden their horizons" and touching upon both networking and career options.
🛠️ C1 Structural Move: Negative Inversion (Not only + auxiliary verb + subject): "Not only can students interact..., but they also gain access to...".
Why it locks in 7.5: In your draft, two sentences began with *"They can also..."* and *"There are also..."*. Replacing this repetition with a textbook negative inversion is an immediate Band 7.5+ signal to the examiner.
8 Perfect Participle Clause & Cleft Clincher
Participle & Cleft
✗ "To sum up, the trend of migrating to metropolitan cities can lead to economical and societal challenges. Nevertheless, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages. In the end, having the experience working or studying elsewhere doesn’t eliminate the possibility of returning to smaller towns at a later stage of life. The opportunities given to the young adults can make a pivotal impact on their life and career trajectories."
✓ "To conclude, although rural depopulation poses distinct economic and social hurdles, these are comfortably outweighed by the benefits of urban life. Crucially, moving away does not mean a permanent departure; having gained valuable experience in a major city, individuals can always return to smaller towns later in life. Ultimately, it is these metropolitan opportunities that make a pivotal impact on their career trajectories."
⭐ What You Did Well: Your point about return migration is pure gold! You proved why the benefits outweigh the costs.
🛠️ C1 Structural Moves: (1) Fix false friend & contractions: economic (not economical) & does not (never doesn't).
(2) Perfect Participle Clause: "having gained valuable experience in a major city, individuals can always return...".
(3) Cleft Clincher: "it is these metropolitan opportunities that make a pivotal impact...".
Why it locks in 7.5: This conclusion delivers a sophisticated, error-free finish that guarantees your score.
⚡ 3 Non-Negotiable C1 Grammar Drills for You, Ynnning
1. Negative Inversion: "Not only [auxiliary] + [subject]..."
✗ They can also meet peers, and they can also find jobs.
✓ Not only can they collaborate with peers, but they also gain access to career opportunities.
Examiner impact: Using just ONE inversion in an essay immediately signals C1 grammatical range without risking vocabulary errors.
2. Cleft Sentences for Focus: "What makes... is..." / "It is... that..."
✗ Moving gives young people freedom to design their lives.
✓ What makes moving to a major city so attractive is the freedom it affords young adults.
Examiner impact: Cleft sentences show the examiner you know how to highlight important ideas with grammatical variety.

✗ economical challenges / two folds / doesn't
✓ economic challenges / twofold / does not
