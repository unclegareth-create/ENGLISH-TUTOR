🎯 The Task 2 Prompts (Choose Option A or Option B)

Select either Option A or Option B for your primary submission (or attempt both for accelerated transformation!). Both prompts are calibrated to showcase your newly mastered C1 grammatical levers.

Option A · Education & Public Policy Discuss Both Views
"Some people believe that university education should be free for all students, while others argue that individuals should pay for their own tertiary studies.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion."
💡 C1 Levers Focus: Ideal for deploying Wh-cleft sentences ("What makes higher education pivotal is...") and preparatory 'It' ("It is widely maintained that...") to balance state funding against individual returns.
Option B · Technology & Automation Outweigh / Impact
"In many parts of the world, automated systems and artificial intelligence are rapidly replacing human workers across various industries.

Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?"
💡 C1 Levers Focus: Seamlessly applies our Master Concession Thesis alongside negative inversion ("Not only does automation accelerate output...") and participle clauses ("..., thereby creating higher-value sectors").

🛡️ The 5 Non-Negotiable Band 7.5+ Guardrails

1. The 260–290 Word Target
Write between 260 and 290 words (hard ceiling: 300 words). Strict brevity forces high grammatical density and eliminates superficial rambling.
2. Exactly 4 Paragraphs
Introduction (45–55w) • Body 1 (90–100w) • Body 2 (100–110w) • Conclusion (40–50w). Zero single-sentence paragraphs.
3. Mandatory C1 Grammar Targets
You must explicitly include:
1 Inversion ("Not only... but [also]...")
1 Cleft ("What... is..." or "It is... that...")
1 Participle ("..., thereby..." / "Having...")
4. Concession + Outweigh Thesis
Deploy Gareth's formula in Paragraph 1: "While it is undeniable that [Concession], I firmly maintain that [Assertion] substantially eclipses [Drawbacks]."
5. Register & Depersonalisation
Zero informal contractions (spell out does not, cannot, will not). Zero personal memoirs (I, me, my, we) outside your thesis statement.

📋 Mandatory 5-Minute Pre-Writing Skeleton Plan

Review the 4-box architectural blueprints below before drafting. Switch tabs to see the breakdown for either prompt:

Box 1: Introduction ~50 words
  • Paraphrase: Debate surrounding state funding versus individual financial contribution for tertiary degrees.
  • Concession & Stance Thesis: While universal state funding guarantees social mobility, individuals derive substantial private financial returns, making a subsidized cost-sharing model far more sustainable.
Box 2: Body 1 (Free Higher Education) ~95 words
  • [I] Topic Sentence (Cleft): What makes universal access crucial is its capacity to democratize socioeconomic opportunity regardless of familial wealth.
  • [E] Mechanism: Tuition fees construct insurmountable barriers for underprivileged students, entrenching generational disparity.
  • [E] Societal Example: Nordic economies where publicly funded education generates an egalitarian, highly skilled civic workforce.
  • [R] Evaluative Link: Thus fostering long-term social cohesion and broad civic productivity.
Box 3: Body 2 (Tuition / Individual Cost) ~105 words
  • [I] Topic Sentence (Inversion): Not only does individual tuition ease fiscal pressure on public treasuries, but it also incentivises academic commitment among students.
  • [E] Mechanism: Completely free university places often stretch state budgets, thereby compromising campus infrastructure and research facilities.
  • [E] Societal Example (Participle): Having invested capital into their credentials, graduates frequently pursue high-yield career paths with heightened determination.
  • [R] Evaluative Clincher: Consequently ensuring robust institutional quality and individual accountability.
Box 4: Conclusion ~45 words
  • Restatement & Synthesis: In conclusion, although state-funded university systems enhance accessibility, the private financial dividends gained by graduates make a hybrid co-payment structure far more equitable and economically viable.

  • Concession & Outweigh Thesis: While automated workflows precipitate undeniable friction in manual job markets, the transformative productivity gains and new economic sectors firmly outweigh these disruptions.
Box 2: Body 1 (Drawbacks & Mitigation) ~95 words
  • [I] Concession (Introductory 'It'): It is widely acknowledged that rapid technological displacement creates acute unemployment among lower-skilled workers.
  • [E] Mechanism: Algorithmic automation renders repetitive manual tasks obsolete, inducing painful structural unemployment.
  • [E] Societal Example: Factory production lines and back-office clerical jobs being absorbed by automated software.
  • [R] Mitigation: However, these hurdles can be mitigated if public institutions invest actively in lifelong digital upskilling.
Box 3: Body 2 (Stronger Advantages) ~105 words
  • [I] Core Assertion (Inversion): Not only does artificial intelligence drastically eliminate human operational error, but it also catalyzes the emergence of entirely new industrial sectors.
  • [E] Mechanism: Automated efficiency reduces manufacturing costs, freeing human cognitive capital for creative, analytical, and strategic domains.
  • [E] Societal Example (Participle): Modern data science and green engineering, thereby creating lucrative careers that did not exist two decades ago.
  • [R] Evaluative Clincher: Elevating overall human standard of living and global productivity.
Box 4: Conclusion ~45 words
  • Restatement & Synthesis: In conclusion, while workforce automation causes temporary socioeconomic adjustment challenges, its unprecedented efficiency and capacity to generate advanced employment ensure its benefits decisively eclipse the drawbacks.
