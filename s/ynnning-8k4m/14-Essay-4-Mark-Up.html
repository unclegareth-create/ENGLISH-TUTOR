All Five Structures, Used Naturally

1. Introductory 'It'

"It is commonly debated whether…" · "Nevertheless, it is unclear if…"

2. Participle Clause (, thereby + -ing)

"…thereby outweighing its disadvantages…" · "…thereby resulting in more returning guests."

3. Wh-Cleft Topic Sentence

"What makes self-funded institutions excel is their efficiency."

4. Negative Inversion

"Not only would the owners and staff have clearer incentives…, but they would also have more autonomy…"

The auxiliary order (would + subject + have) is perfect.

5. Concession + Nominalisation

"While having a sense of autonomy helps to boost…" · "The democratisation of access to knowledge is critical to the development of a healthy society."

Four Habits: Your 2-Minute Final Check

Save two minutes at the end of Task 2 and read your essay four times, once for each habit. Each read looks for one thing only.

Habit 1 · Uncountable Nouns

Formula: uncountable noun + NO -s + singular verb

  • staffs → staff (or staff members if you need a plural)
  • infrastructures → infrastructure
  • more social responsibilities → greater social responsibility

Watch list for IELTS: advice, equipment, evidence, feedback, funding, information, infrastructure, knowledge, research, staff.

Habit 2 · Adjective Before a Noun

Formula: adjective (-al) + noun

  • culture institutions → cultural institutions

You wrote cultural institutions correctly in three places and slipped twice when writing fast. It's a speed slip, not a gap in knowledge, so your final check will catch it.

Habit 3 · No 'the' for General Ideas

Formula: general plural / uncountable noun = NO 'the'

  • exploring their interests in the literature
  • should be funded by the governments
  • innovation of the cultural institutions

Ask: "Do I mean these specific ones, or this thing in general?" In general → no the.

Habit 4 · Spelling the Key Words

Rule: words from the question must be spelt perfectly every time.

  • goverments → governments
  • insitutions → institutions
  • excell → excel
  • to limited → too limited (to = direction/infinitive, too = excessively)
