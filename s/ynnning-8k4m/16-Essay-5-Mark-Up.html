Four Habits from Essay #5: Your 2-Minute Check

Habit 1 is the one that moves your score. On its own it takes you from 9 to 11 error-free sentences. Add the hyphen from Habit 4 and you reach 12.

Habit 1 · 'A / An' Before Adjective + Singular Noun

Formula: a / an + [Adjective] + [Singular Countable Noun]

  • S7: offers a certain level of robustness
  • S8: Having a less habitable environment
  • S9: An appropriate climate, drinkable water and clean air…

You got this right in S15 ("provide a habitable environment"). Whenever a singular noun like environment, climate or level has an adjective in front of it, include a / an.

Habit 2 · Countable vs Uncountable (S11)

Formula: a number of + plural | damage (no -s, no 'countless')

  • an increasing amount of wild fires → an increasing number of wildfires (wildfires are countable, so use number of; amount of is for uncountable nouns like pollution).
  • countless damages → widespread damage (damage meaning physical harm is uncountable; plural damages means money paid by a court).
Habit 3 · Word Choice & Collocation

Formula: pose + challenges / threats / risks · endangered species

  • S2: raise pose a range of challenges (we raise questions, concerns and awareness; we pose challenges).
  • S4: the distinguishing species → the endangered species (distinguishing means making something stand out; endangered means at risk of extinction).
Habit 4 · Spelling & Hyphens

Rule: compound adjectives before a noun take a hyphen; check one-word compounds.

  • S8: large scale → large-scale problems
  • S11: wild fires → wildfires (one word)
  • S11: catastrophies → catastrophes (ends in -phes)
Style Notes (Not Errors)
