Band 7.5+ Reference Model (157 words)

157 words • 18w / 35w / 62w / 42w

The diagram illustrates how electricity is generated and stored in a hydroelectric power station over a 24-hour cycle.

Overall, this is a cyclical process divided into two main phases: water flows downwards from a high-level reservoir to generate electricity during the day, and is pumped back up from a low-level reservoir at night.

During the day, the intake beneath the dam opens, allowing river water stored in the high-level reservoir to flow downwards into the power station. Here, the force of the water spins the reversible turbines, which drive a generator to produce electricity. This electricity is then transmitted via power lines to the national grid, while the used water drains into a low-level reservoir.

At night, the intake closes to stop further water entering from the river. Instead, the reversible turbines operate in reverse, pumping water from the low-level reservoir back up to the high-level reservoir so that the cycle can begin again the next morning.

Notice how plain and natural the vocabulary is: adding the open/closed intake and the drain into the low-level reservoir brings the word count cleanly to 157 words with just three C1 structures (allowing ... to flow, non-defining which drive, and the participle clause pumping water ...).
