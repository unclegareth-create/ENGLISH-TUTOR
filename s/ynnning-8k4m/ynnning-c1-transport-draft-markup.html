4. Core C1 Grammar Patterns

Aim to include three or four of these patterns naturally across your essay:

Pattern Structure In Your Draft How to Use It
Introductory / Preparatory 'It' It + BE + Adverb + Past Participle / Adjective + that-clause ✓ 1 Found (it is widely argued that) Replace personal 'I think that...' or flat 'Many people say...' with 'It is widely acknowledged that...'.
Passive Reporting Verbs Subject + BE + believed / thought / said / expected + to + Infinitive ✓ 1 Found (are widely believed to reduce) Shift active generalisations ('Experts believe this causes...') into sleek passive reporting ('This is widely believed to cause...').
Cleft Sentences (Wh- & It-Clefts) What + Subject + Verb + BE + Noun/Clause | It is [X] that [Y] ✓ 2 Found (it is widely argued that expanding) Use a Wh-cleft at the start of a body paragraph to spotlight your core argument: 'What drives this shift is not cost, but convenience.'
Negative & Restrictive Inversions Negative Adverbial + Auxiliary + Subject + Main Verb ✓ 1 Found (Not only does) Place ONE well-judged negative inversion in your essay ('Not only does this policy burden commuters, but it also...'). Never force more than one.
Resultative Participle Clauses Main Clause + , + thereby / thus + Present Participle (-ing) ✓ 1 Found (, thereby fostering) Merge choppy cause-and-effect sentences ('This cuts funding. As a result, it weakens schools.') into a resultative participle clause (', thereby weakening local schools.').

Workout 1 · Participial Frame & Passive Reporting Shift

Sentence S1 · Preposed Participle + Passive Reporting
Pattern: Having + [Past Participle]..., [Subject] + is / are widely believed to + [Infinitive]
"Nowadays, urban congestion is a hot topic that people discuss about in every major city, and I think every coin has two sides when governments consider road tolls."

Open with a participial frame ('Having weighed the evidence,' or 'Given these pressures,') and shift the main clause into a Passive Reporting structure ('...is widely believed to...'):

Cambridge C1 Model: Having weighed the broader evidence, analysts widely consider this shift to determine how urban congestion is a hot topic that people discuss in every major city, and I think every coin has two sides when governments consider road tolls.

Workout 2 · The Spotlight Wh-Cleft Pivot

Sentence S3 · Cleft Sentences (Wh- & It-Clefts)
Pattern: What + [Subject + Verb] + is / remains + [Core Factor / That-Clause]
"On the one hand, recent researches and informations show that punitive congestion charges burden low-income commuters, however, many councils still rely on them."

Spotlight the core mechanism of this sentence from your draft using a 'What... is...' cleft structure (or 'It is precisely... that...'):

Cambridge C1 Model: What warrants closer scrutiny is the extent to which recent empirical research and information show that punitive congestion charges burden low-income commuters; however, many councils still rely on them.

Workout 3 · Concessive Subordinator & Preparatory 'It'

Sentence S9 · Concessive Subordinator + Preparatory 'It'
Pattern: Whilst / Although + [Concession Clause], it is widely acknowledged that + [Main Clause]
"In a nutshell, solving this urban conundrum shouldn't comprise of punitive taxes alone."

Recast this sentence from your draft using a concessive subordinator ('Whilst...' or 'Although...') paired with an impersonal Preparatory 'It' frame ('...it is widely acknowledged that...'):

Cambridge C1 Model: Whilst opinions on implementation vary, it is widely acknowledged that solving this urban policy challenge shouldn't comprise punitive taxes alone.

Workout 4 · Restrictive Inversion & Resultative Participle

Sentence S10 · Negative Inversion + Participle Clause
Pattern: Rarely / Seldom / Not only + [Aux + Subject + Verb]..., thereby + [Present Participle -ing]
"Ultimately, balanced transport policies give citizens a plethora of travel choices and broaden our horizons over the long term."

Elevate this sentence from your draft using a Restrictive Inversion ('Rarely do...', 'Seldom has...', or 'Not only does...') paired with a trailing Resultative Participle Clause (', thereby fostering / enabling...'):

Cambridge C1 Model: Rarely do short-term fixes resolve the underlying issue when ultimately, balanced transport policies give citizens a numerous travel choices and broaden our horizons over the long term, thereby requiring sustained structural reform.
