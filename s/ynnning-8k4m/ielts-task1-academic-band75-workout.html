The 4 Essential Data Anchors (Never List Hour-by-Hour!)

Listing every single two-hour gap locks the score at Band 6.0 (Examiner note: "lacks synthetic grouping"). Band 7.5 selects only these 4 structural milestones:

1. The Morning Surge (Radio Peak):
Radio climbs rapidly from 7% at 6:00 to an all-day peak of roughly 27% around 8:30, while TV remains under 8%.
2. The Midday Crossover (13:00):
Both audiences converge at approximately 15% at 1:00 PM; after this point, television takes the lead permanently.
3. The Prime-Time Climax (TV Peak):
Television viewing surges dramatically between 16:00 and 20:00, cresting at approximately 45%, while radio steadily wanes below 10%.
4. The Night-time Convergence (Midnight):
Both lines plummet sharply past 22:00, bottoming out between 1% and 3% in the early hours of the morning.
🎯 Task Achievement • Band 7.5 Overview Architecture

The British Council 2-Sentence Overview Blueprint

An Overview must present the overarching trend and the principal comparison without quoting exact figures or percentages.

The Band 7.5+ Overview Formula: Overall, while [Medium A] reached its highest engagement during [Timeframe A], [Medium B] peaked in the [Timeframe B]. In addition, [Medium B] commanded a significantly higher maximum share of [Population] than [Medium A].
Sentence 1: The Diurnal Divergence (Morning vs Evening)

Contrast the prime hours of both mediums in a single fronted subordinate clause.

"Overall, it is immediately apparent that radio and television followed contrasting daily trajectories: while radio audiences peaked in the early morning, television viewing reached its pinnacle during prime-time evening hours."
Sentence 2: The Macro Comparison (Volume / Peak Share)

Identify which medium dominated overall engagement without citing any exact percentage.

"Furthermore, television attracted a considerably larger peak proportion of the population than radio, commanding the vast majority of media engagement from the early afternoon onward."
Examiner Red-Line Rule:

Never insert figures like 27% or 45% into your overview. Including raw data in Paragraph 2 demotes it to a body paragraph and leaves your response without an authentic overview under the Task Achievement rubric.

Transforming Flat Band 6.0 Sentences into Band 7.5+ Structures

Type your upgraded sentence into each workbench box, then click Reveal Model to compare your structure against the Cambridge C1 blueprint.

Drill 1: Fronted Contrast Clause (While / Whereas)
Target: Replace two separate simple sentences with a single balanced contrast.
Introductory 'While'
Original Flat Sentence: "Radio had high listeners in the morning. Television had very low viewers at the same time."
Band 7.5 Model: While radio listenership surged during the morning hours, television viewership remained minimal until the early afternoon.
Structure Note: Uses fronted subordinate clause (While...), replaced colloquial "had high listeners" with nominalised listenership surged, and paired with viewership remained minimal.
Drill 2: Participle Clause for Culmination (..., reaching / peaking at...)
Target: Chain a trend and its final peak using a present participle clause.
Participle Clause
Original Flat Sentence: "Television viewing increased sharply after 16:00 and then it reached a peak of 45% at 20:00."
Band 7.5 Model: Television viewing climbed steeply after 16:00, eventually peaking at roughly 45% around 20:00.
Structure Note: Eliminates repetitive coordinate conjunction ("and then it reached"), replacing it with an adverbial participle construction (..., eventually peaking at...).
Drill 3: Passive Reporting & Data Approximation
Target: Depersonalised academic passive with precise qualifiers (approximately / roughly).
Passive Reporting
Original Flat Sentence: "We can see that around 27% of people listened to radio at 8:30 in the morning."
Band 7.5 Model: Approximately 27% of the UK population was recorded listening to the radio at 8:30, representing the zenith for this medium.
Structure Note: Completely strips first-person informal frame ("We can see that"), using passive verb was recorded listening followed by appositive participle phrase representing the zenith.
Drill 4: Negative Inversion for Dramatic Contrast
Target: Demonstrate high-level C1 syntactic control using a fronted negative limiter.
Negative Inversion
Original Flat Sentence: "Television viewing did not exceed radio listening at any point before midday."
Band 7.5 Model: At no point prior to midday did television viewing surpass that of radio.
Structure Note: Negative Adverbial (At no point) + Auxiliary (did) + Subject (television viewing) + Base Verb (surpass). Includes demonstrative pronoun that of to avoid repeating "listening".
Drill 5: Cleft Sentence for Focal Emphasis
Target: Emphasise the crossover timing using an introductory 'It was' cleft.
Cleft Construction
Original Flat Sentence: "The two mediums equalled each other at 13:00 and then TV took over."
Band 7.5 Model: It was at approximately 13:00 that audience shares converged at 15%, marking the threshold after which television dominated.
Structure Note: It + BE (was) + Prepositional Phrase + that-clause. Elevates basic time reporting into a sharp analytical milestone.

Complete Band 8.0 Model: Radio & Television Audiences

Carefully observe the 4-paragraph balance: Introduction → Overview → Morning/Midday Breakdown → Evening Primetime Climax.

Paragraph 1: Introduction (Paraphrase) The line graph illustrates the percentage of the United Kingdom population aged four and over who engaged with radio and television across a 24-hour cycle between October and December 1992.
Paragraph 2: The Macro Overview (Zero Data Figures) Overall, it is clear that the two mediums exhibited divergent diurnal patterns: while radio audiences peaked during the morning commute, television viewership culminated in the evening. Furthermore, television commanded a substantially higher peak audience share than radio, remaining the dominant medium for the majority of the day.
Paragraph 3: Body 1 (Radio Progression & Midday Crossover) Regarding radio, listenership commenced at approximately 7% at 6:00 before surging dramatically to an all-day zenith of around 27% at 8:30. Thereafter, the proportion declined steadily throughout the late morning and afternoon, settling between 10% and 15%. Television, having begun near zero, increased progressively to intersect with radio at 13:00, where both stood at roughly 15%.
Paragraph 4: Body 2 (Television Surge & Night-Time Decline) From midday onward, television viewing climbed steeply, particularly between 16:00 and 20:00, when it reached a distinct peak of 45%. In sharp contrast, radio listenership waned below 10% during these evening hours. Following this prime-time high, television viewership dropped sharply after 22:00, with both mediums converging near zero percent by 4:00.
