Lesson 7: Speaking Test & Process Diagrams (Passives & Sequencing)

Part 1: Familiar Topics — Neighbours

Keep answers brisk and natural (2–3 sentences each): give a direct answer plus a quick reason or example.

Question 1

How often do you see your neighbours? [Why/Why not?]

Question 2

Do you invite your neighbours to your home? [Why/Why not?]

Question 3

Do you think you are a good neighbour? [Why/Why not?]

Question 4

Has a neighbour ever helped you? [Why/Why not?]

IELTS Speaking Part 2 Cue Card

Describe a very difficult task that you succeeded in doing as part of your work or studies.

You should say:

  • what task you did
  • why this task was very difficult
  • how you worked on this task

and explain how you felt when you had successfully completed this task.

Structuring Rules

1. The 1-Minute Note Secret: Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin (one per bullet)—never full sentences.

2. The 3-Act Story Arc: Past Context (30s) → Core Challenge & Steps (45s) → Reflection / “So What?” (45s).

3. The Cambridge Anchor: Bullet points are springboards, not an interrogation checklist. If you finish early, deepen your reflection until halted.

Natural Spoken Signposting

1. Opening: “The task I’d like to talk about was...”

2. Why it was difficult: “What made it particularly tough was...”

3. How you tackled it: “As for how I went about solving it...”

4. How you felt: “Looking back, once I finally got it over the line, I felt...”

Part 3: Discussion Topics

Develop extended abstract answers with A.R.E.A. (Answer • Reason • Example • Alternative).

Topic A: Difficult Jobs
  • What are the most difficult jobs that people do?
  • Why do you think some people choose to do difficult jobs?
  • Do you agree or disagree that all jobs are difficult sometimes?
Topic B: Personal and Career Success
  • How important is it for everyone to have a goal in their personal life?
  • Is it always necessary to work hard in order to achieve career success?
  • Do you think that successful people are always happy people?

The 3-Part Process Overview Formula

Structural Blueprint (Paragraph 2)

Overall, [the process] is a [linear / cyclical] [number]-stage sequence that begins with [initial input/stage] and culminates in [final product/outcome].

Never list every single step in Paragraph 2. State the type (linear vs. cyclical), the total number of main stages, and the start → end points.

Live Workout: Write the Overview

1. Chocolate Production (10 stages): Starts with ripe red cacao pods harvested from trees → ends with liquid chocolate ready for moulding.

"Overall, the production of chocolate is a ten-stage linear process that begins with harvesting ripe cacao pods and concludes with pressing the roasted nibs into liquid chocolate."

2. Glass Bottle Recycling (3 main phases): Used bottles collected → crushed, melted, and reshaped → delivered back to supermarkets to repeat.

"Overall, glass recycling is a continuous three-phase cycle in which used bottles are collected, remanufactured into new containers, and returned to retail outlets for reuse."

Passive Voice vs. Active Voice in Process Diagrams

Man-Made / Industrial Steps → Passive

Subject + is / are + Past Participle (V3)

Use Present Simple Passive when people or machines act on a material: "The clay is dug from the ground and is passed through a metal grid."

Natural / Biological Steps → Active

Subject + Present Simple (V / V-s)

Never force passive onto natural phenomena: "Water evaporates from the ocean and condenses into clouds." (Never "is evaporated".)

Live Workout: Choose Active or Passive & Fix

1. "Workers sort the plastic bottles by colour, and then they wash them."

"The plastic bottles are sorted by colour and washed." (Omit unnecessary agents like workers.)

2. "The salmon eggs are hatched in the upper river before the young fish are swum to the sea."

"The salmon eggs hatch in the upper river before the young fish swim to the sea." (Natural biological cycle → Active voice.)

3. "A machine cuts the clay into brick shapes using a wire cutter."

"The clay is cut into brick shapes by a wire cutter." (Use by for the instrument/machine if helpful.)

C1 Sequencing: Replacing "First... Then... Next..."

Three High-Scoring Linking Formulas
  • Time Clause (`Once` + V3): Once [V3 / passive], [next stage + passive]. → "Once sorted, the clay is mixed with sand and water."
  • Relative Clause (`where` + passive): ...placed in a [location], where [S + is/are + V3]. → "...placed in a drying oven, where they remain for 24 to 48 hours."
  • Preposition + `-ing` (`Before` / `After`): Before/After being [V3], [S + is/are + V3]. → "After being cooled for up to 72 hours, the bricks are packaged."

Live Workout: Combine Two Choppy Steps into One C1 Sentence

1. "First, the beans are dried in the sun. Next, they are packed into sacks."

"Once dried in the sun, the beans are packed into sacks."

2. "The bricks are moved to a kiln. In the kiln, they are heated at 200°C to 980°C."

"The bricks are moved to a kiln, where they are heated at temperatures ranging from 200°C to 980°C."

3. "The bricks are cooled in a chamber for 48–72 hours. Then they are packaged for delivery."

"After being cooled in a chamber for 48 to 72 hours, the finished bricks are packaged for delivery."
