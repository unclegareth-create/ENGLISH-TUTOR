Step 2 • C1 Grammatical Variety Over Obscure Lexis

The 5 C1 Structural Devices for IELTS Writing Task 2

Three or four well-placed C1 structural shifts in a 270-word essay guarantee a Band 7.5+ in Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Practise each blueprint below and click 👁️ Reveal Cambridge Model.

Device 1 • Introductory / Preparatory 'It' 100% Depersonalised Opening
It + is + [Adverb: widely / increasingly / often] + [Past Participle: argued / recognised / observed] + that + Clause

Upgrade This Band 6.5 Sentence: "I think that universities should teach practical workplace skills alongside academic theory."

Band 7.5+ C1 Model: It is widely argued that universities should cultivate practical workplace skills alongside academic theory.
Device 2 • Passive Reporting Verbs (Subject Elevation) Academic Distance
Subject + is / are + [widely / generally] + believed / thought / known + to + Base Verb (or: to have + V3)

Upgrade This Band 6.5 Sentence: "Many people believe that affordable public transport reduces urban inequality."

Band 7.5+ C1 Model: Affordable public transport is widely believed to reduce urban inequality.
Device 3 • Cleft Sentences (Wh- Cleft & It- Cleft) Topic Sentence Spotlight
What + Clause + is + Noun / that-clause | It is + [Focused Noun] + that + Relative Clause

Upgrade This Band 6.5 Sentence: "Consistent daily practice rather than natural talent determines long-term academic success."

Wh- Cleft Model: What determines long-term academic success is consistent daily practice rather than natural talent.
It- Cleft Model: It is consistent daily practice, rather than natural talent, that determines long-term academic success.
Device 4 • Negative Inversion (Use Once Per Essay) Auxiliary Before Subject
Not only + do / does / did + Subject + Base Verb, but + Subject + also + Verb

Upgrade This Band 6.5 Sentence: "Subsidised rail networks ease motorway congestion and they also lower household commuting costs."

Band 7.5+ C1 Model: Not only do subsidised rail networks ease motorway congestion, but they also lower household commuting costs.
Device 5 • Resultative Participle Clauses (..., thereby -ing) Seamless Cause-and-Effect Chain
[Main Clause], thereby + fostering / curbing / reducing / strengthening + [Noun Phrase]

Upgrade This Band 6.5 Sentence: "Community libraries provide free study spaces, and this fosters equal educational opportunity for low-income students."

Band 7.5+ C1 Model: Community libraries provide free study spaces, thereby fostering equal educational opportunity for low-income students.

Step 4 • Live Task 2 Paragraph Workbench

Task 2 Paragraph Depersonalisation & Word-Count Discipline

Rewrite the informal, template-heavy paragraph below into a clean, 85–110 word Band 7.5+ academic body paragraph. The live checker verifies your word count, 100% depersonalisation (no I / we / you in body arguments), and zero memorised clichés.

Informal / Template Draft to Repair

“In a nutshell, every coin has two sides when we talk about university education and artificial intelligence. I think that if students use AI tools for every assignment, you will lose your ability to think critically. Also, there are a lot of researches showing that over-reliance on automated summaries makes students passive, and this weakens their independent writing skills.”

Target Fixes: 1) Toss out "In a nutshell, every coin has two sides". 2) Remove "we / I think / you / your". 3) Fix uncountable "researches" → "a growing body of research". 4) Use at least two C1 devices (e.g. While..., What..., ..., thereby weakening...).

Band 7.5–8.0 Cambridge Model Paragraph (78 words):
“While generative software can accelerate literature reviews, uncritical reliance on automated tools poses a clear risk to intellectual development. It is widely observed that when undergraduates delegate essay drafting to software, they bypass the essential struggle of structuring an argument. Furthermore, a growing body of research indicates that habitual dependence on instant summaries encourages passive reading, thereby weakening students' capacity for independent critical analysis.”
