🎯 2. Personalised Repair Clinic (From Your Letter & Sadcore Discussion)

Structural Blueprints • Live Repair • Model Reveal
Diagnostic 1 • False Friend

Prospects vs. Perspectives

In Russian and Belarusian, перспективы covers future career opportunities. In English, future chances of success are prospects, while a perspective is your mental viewpoint.

career / job + PROSPECTS (future success)
a fresh / different + PERSPECTIVE + on [Noun] (viewpoint)
Slip 1A “My job perspectives in the company are looking very healthy.”
Cambridge Model: “My career prospects (or job prospects) in the company are looking very healthy.”
Review Bridge Use perspective accurately in your album review: “This album gave me a new [viewpoint] on solitude.”
Cambridge Model: “Listening to this album gave me a completely fresh perspective on solitude.”
Diagnostic 2 • Preposition Precision

Movement, Place & Time Prepositions

You spotted “plans on (mistake :)) next month” yourself while writing! Lock in these five high-frequency patterns:

plans FOR [time] • arrive IN [city/country] • hike IN [mountains] • fall INTO [a mood] • AT night
Slip 2A “Tell me about your plans on next month when you arrive to Armenia.”
Cambridge Model: “Tell me about your plans for next month when you arrive in Armenia.” (Note: never ‘arrive to’ — use arrive in a city/country, or come to Armenia.)
Slip 2B “We can go hiking to the mountains so we don't fall in an autumn depression, even in the night.”
Cambridge Model: “We can go hiking in the mountains so we don't fall into an autumn depression (or get the autumn blues), even at night.”
Diagnostic 3 • Tense & Aspect

Present Perfect with Lately + Get Used To

Because lately means “in the recent period leading up to right now”, it bridges past and present and requires the Present Perfect, not the Past Simple.

Lately, + Subject + HAVE / HAS + Past Participle
have / has got used to + Verb-ING (or Noun)
Slip 3A “Lately, I got used to the cooler weather and started wearing a warm pullover.”
Cambridge Model: “Lately, I've got used to (or I've gotten used to) the cooler weather and have started wearing a warm jumper (or sweater).”
Music Habit Combine Lately + Present Perfect Continuous for your listening habits:
Cambridge Model: “Lately, I've been replaying this album whenever I need to unwind after work.”
Diagnostic 4 • Articles & Natural Closings

Warm Closings & Definite Articles (the)

When a noun is made specific by a following clause (“the time [that] we spent together”) or refers to a shared seasonal event (“the approaching winter cold”), English requires the.

THE + Noun + [Relative Clause: we spent together]
They SEND you their love / hugs (Present Simple for warm wishes)
Slip 4A “I really miss you and time we spent together in Georgia before approaching winter cold.”
Cambridge Model: “I really miss you and the time we spent together in Georgia before the approaching winter cold.”
Slip 4B “The kids are fine. They are sending you their hugs!”
Cambridge Model: “The kids are doing well — they send you their love (or send you big hugs)!”

I was already an adult when I first heard Muse through their landmark 2001 record, Origin of Symmetry. While I cannot say that it (or any other album) fundamentally altered my life, it impressed and surprised me deeply. To this day, it remains on my rather short list of favourite albums.

As Muse's second studio album, Origin of Symmetry marked a period of bold experimentation with instrumentation. Not only did it feature Matthew Bellamy's soaring falsetto and arpeggiated guitar, but the songs themselves shifted effortlessly between vulnerability and explosive intensity. What struck me most was its theatrical quality, blurring the boundaries between rock and classical traditions and ultimately challenging my expectations of what rock music could sound like.

My favourite songs are “Micro Cuts”, where Bellamy adopts an almost operatic style of singing over anxious yet beautiful melodies, and “Darkshines”, whose strength lies in the contrast between subdued verses and an explosive chorus. The overall impression of the album is one of overwhelming emotion, beauty, madness and unpredictability. What fascinated me wasn't any particular instrument or melody, but the way the music made me feel.

Although the album may no longer sound revolutionary, its emotional intensity remains just as powerful today. I would unreservedly recommend it not only to rock purists, but also to classical listeners and anyone drawn to dramatic, emotionally charged music.

C1 Cohesion & Linker Drills

3 Live Sentence-Combining Drills (Directly on Your Text)

Test these three high-impact C1 sentence transformations to eliminate staccato phrasing and connect disjointed thoughts:

Drill 1 • The Prepositional / Relative Bridge (P1, S1 + S2)
Your Draft: “I was already an adult when I heard Muse for the first time. It was the ‘Origin of Symmetry’ album.”
...heard [Band] for the first time + THROUGH / VIA + [Noun Phrase]

Eliminates the abrupt “It was...” repeat and establishes a smooth narrative flow.

Model: “I was already an adult when I first heard Muse through their landmark 2001 record, 'Origin of Symmetry'.”
Drill 2 • The P2 Staccato Crusher (P2, S5 + S6 + S7)
Your Draft: “I was fascinated by the theatrical quality of the music. It was as though the boundaries between rock and classical music were blurred. The album challenged my expectations of what rock music could sound like.”
What fascinated me + WAS + [Noun], + PARTICIPLE (-ing) + and PARTICIPLE (-ing)...

Replaces three consecutive flat subject-verb statements with an elegant C1 participle chain.

Model: “What fascinated me most was the theatrical quality of the music, blurring the boundaries between rock and classical traditions and challenging my expectations of what rock music could sound like.”
Drill 3 • Correlative Coordination (P4, S1)
Your Draft: “...and I can recommend it to people who appreciate classical music as well as rock and to listeners who enjoy intense emotions and dramatic music.”
I can recommend it + NOT ONLY TO [X], + BUT ALSO TO [Y]...

Untangles the 4-fold “and... as well as... and to... and...” coordination into crisp parallel balance.

Model: “I can unreservedly recommend it not only to rock purists, but also to classical listeners and anyone drawn to dramatic, emotionally charged music.”
