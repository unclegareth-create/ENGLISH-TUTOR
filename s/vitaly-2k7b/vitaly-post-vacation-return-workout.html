🎙️ Step 1: Active Speaking Warm-Up (2-Minute Long Turn + A.R.E.A.)

1 Min Prep • 2 Min Speak • Follow-Up Discussion
Prompt A • Post-Vacation Story

Describe a memorable day off, trip, or break you enjoyed recently.

You should say:

  • where you went or how you spent the break
  • who you were with and what the atmosphere was like
  • something interesting or unexpected that happened
  • and explain why this break helped you recharge.
1. The 1-Minute Note Secret Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin (one per prompt bullet); never write full sentences.
2. The 3-Act Story Arc Past Context (30s) → Sensory Snapshot / Core Scene (45s) → Reflection / 'So What?' (45s).
3. The Cambridge Anchor Bullet points are springboards, not an interrogation checklist. If finished early, deepen the reflection and comparison until halted.
Preparation Time (60s)
01:00
Target B2+ Upgrades to Drop In:
• something unexpected (pronoun + adj)
• nobody realised that... (singular verb)
• was built / was organised (passive voice)
• absolutely fascinating / freezing
A.R.E.A. Follow-Up 1

Do you think people recharge better by travelling somewhere new, or by staying somewhere quiet at home?

Answer → Reason → Example → Alternative: Use an indefinite pronoun + adjective (e.g. somewhere peaceful, something exciting).

Cambridge B2+ Model: “It depends on how tired you are. Travelling somewhere unfamiliar gives you fresh energy because everything around you feels new. On the other hand, if you have had a busy month at school, doing nothing stressful at home is often the best way to recover.”
A.R.E.A. Follow-Up 2

Why do so many people check their phones even when they are supposed to be on holiday?

Answer → Reason → Example → Alternative: Try using a passive structure (apps are designed to...) or nobody wants to...

Cambridge B2+ Model: “The main reason is that modern apps are designed to keep our attention all day. Nobody wants to miss an important message from friends, so people keep checking notifications even when they are relaxing by the sea.”
A.R.E.A. Follow-Up 3

If you could design a new gadget for travellers, what problem would it solve?

Answer → Reason → Example → Alternative: Try using a conditional (If I designed..., it would...) or causative (have your luggage carried).

Cambridge B2+ Model: “If I could invent something useful for travellers, I would create a pocket translator that works offline anywhere in the world. That way, everyone could understand menus and train announcements immediately without needing an internet connection.”

🧭 Step 2: Targeted Grammar & Structural Upgrade Blueprints

4 Core Formulas • Live Practice Drills • Model Reveal
Blueprint 01 • Operation Nobody

Indefinite Pronouns: Singular Verbs & Adjective Order

Words with every- / some- / any- / no- always take a singular verb. Never use double negatives, and always place adjectives after the pronoun.

Everyone / Nobody + SINGULAR VERB (is / was / knows / has)
something / anywhere / nothing + ADJECTIVE (something strange)
1A. Fix the trap: “Nobody in the hotel were awake when we heard strange something outside.”
Cambridge Model: “Nobody in the hotel was awake when we heard something strange outside.”
1B. Fix the double negative: “We looked everywhere, but we couldn't find the boarding passes nowhere.”
Cambridge Model: “We looked everywhere, but we couldn't find the boarding passes anywhere.”
Blueprint 02 • Processes & Inventions

Present & Past Passive Voice

When the object or invention is more important than the person doing the action, shift the object to the front and use be + Past Participle (V3).

Present Process: Object + AM / IS / ARE + Past Participle (is heated / are packed)
Past History: Object + WAS / WERE + Past Participle (+ by Agent)
2A. Shift to Past Passive: “Walter Diemer invented pink bubble gum in 1928.”
Cambridge Model: “Pink bubble gum was invented by Walter Diemer in 1928.”
2B. Fix the factory process: “First, the liquid heats to 120 degrees and then pours into moulds.”
Cambridge Model: “First, the liquid is heated to 120 degrees and then (is) poured into moulds.”
Blueprint 03 • Time & Experience

Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

Use Past Simple for finished times in the past (last week, in 2024, two days ago). Use Present Perfect for unfinished time or duration up to now (since, for, yet, already).

Finished Past Time (ago / last week / yesterday) → PAST SIMPLE (went / saw)
Unfinished Duration (for / since / recently) → HAVE / HAS + Past Participle
3A. Fix the tense trap: “I have returned from my holiday two days ago.”
Cambridge Model: “I returned from my holiday two days ago.” (two days ago is a finished past time → Past Simple).
3B. Upgrade using Present Perfect: “I know my best friend since five years.”
Cambridge Model: “I have known my best friend for five years.” (Use Present Perfect + for with a period of time).
Blueprint 04 • B2 Power Structures

Causative (Have Something Done) & 3rd Conditional

Use have/get + object + V3 when someone else does a service for you. Use the Third Conditional to imagine a different past outcome.

Causative Service: HAVE / GET + Object + Past Participle (had my bike repaired)
3rd Conditional: IF + Past Perfect (had + V3), WOULD HAVE + Past Participle
4A. Rewrite with Causative (HAD): “A technician fixed my laptop screen yesterday.”
Cambridge Model: “I had my laptop screen fixed yesterday.”
4B. Fix the 3rd Conditional: “If we would have left earlier, we hadn't missed the train.”
Cambridge Model: “If we had left earlier, we would not have missed the train.” (Never put would inside the if-clause!).

🔑 Step 3: 8-Item Key Word Transformation Workout

3 to 6 Words • Do Not Change the Key Word • 4-Step Deconstruction
Cambridge Part 4 Strategy

The 4-Step Elimination & Deconstruction Method

Rule: Use between 3 and 6 words, including the unchanged key word.
1. Strike Out the Echoes Cross out the words from Sentence 1 that already appear in Sentence 2 to isolate what is missing.
2. Inspect Tense Harmony Check the tense of Sentence 1 (past simple, present perfect, passive) so your gap matches it.
3. Check Voice Shift Spot whether Sentence 2 shifts from active to passive, or from somebody/anybody to nobody.
4. Solve the Pattern Fit the missing meaning into 3 to 6 words around the exact, unchanged key word.
