📰 The Guardian · World & Technology News
China's 'Dancing Grannies' Silenced by Secret Anti-Speaker Guns
Open Original Article ↗

Every evening in public parks and apartment squares across China, millions of retired women meet up for 'guangchangwu' (plaza dancing). For the dancers, it is a fun, social way to stay fit and chat with friends. The trouble is that each group brings enormous rolling loudspeakers, turning up the volume to maximum and blasting traditional folk music and loud pop songs directly beneath residential bedroom windows.

For neighbours trying to study for difficult school exams, sleep after night shifts, or relax after work, the deafening music has triggered years of bitter standoffs. Frustrated residents have tried everything: throwing water balloons from balconies, letting off firecrackers, and even tossing smelly garbage. But because traditional culture strictly demands deep respect for elderly citizens, local police are extremely reluctant to issue fines or ban the dancers.

Now, fed-up residents have turned to a futuristic secret weapon: a handheld 'anti-loudspeaker jammer gun'. Looking like a heavy tactical torch, the gadget shoots a concentrated, invisible infrared beam up to 50 metres away, instantly cutting the Bluetooth connection or shutting down the dancers' speaker entirely. When the booming music abruptly dies, the bewildered dancers tap the speaker in confusion, assume it has broken down, and quietly pack up for the night—while smiling residents watch in secret from their windows.

Key Phrases & B1+ Sentence Blueprints

📐 B1+ Structural Upgrades • Everyday Opinions, Contrasts & Hypotheticals
Formula 1 · Second Conditional for Hypotheticals: If + Past Simple, would (not) + Verb (e.g. If I lived next to the park, I would buy a jammer immediately / If they used headphones, nobody would complain)
Formula 2 · Clear Contrast with Even though: Even though + [Fact], [Main Point] (e.g. Even though public parks are for everyone, people still need to respect their neighbours)
Formula 3 · Purpose & Result with So that: [Action] + so that + [Subject] could + Verb (e.g. He used the device so that he could stop the noise without starting an argument)

Myth vs. Fact Lead-In Quiz

01In Switzerland, federal law strictly forbids flushing your apartment toilet after 10:00 PM anywhere in the country.
MYTH: This is a famous worldwide internet myth! While some older apartment buildings have house rules advising consideration, there is no federal law banning night-time toilet flushing.
02The 'Mosquito Ringtone' emits a high-pitched 17.4 kHz sound that teenagers can hear clearly, but most adults over 30 cannot hear at all.
FACT: As the human ear ages, microscopic hair cells in the cochlea degrade, making adults completely deaf to ultra-high frequencies that teenagers hear effortlessly.
03Sound waves travel roughly 4 times faster through water than they do through air.
FACT: Because water molecules are much more tightly packed than gas molecules in the air, vibrational energy travels at roughly 1,480 m/s compared to 343 m/s in air.
04Across China, an estimated 100 million retired citizens take part in outdoor plaza dancing every single evening.
FACT: Square dancing ('guangchangwu') is a colossal cultural phenomenon with over 100 million regular dancers, creating massive social benefits but huge acoustic battles.
