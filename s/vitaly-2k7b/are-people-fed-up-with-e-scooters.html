1. Why do some e-scooter companies see their share prices drop?
Key points: High maintenance and charging costs, scooters breaking or getting vandalised quickly, strict city bans/caps, and difficulty making a profit on cheap short rides.
2. What problems do e-scooters solve?
Key points: They solve the "last-mile" problem (getting from a train or bus stop to your final destination), cut down short car trips, reduce traffic jams, and produce zero tailpipe emissions.
3. What are some of the rules that cities are introducing to limit the risks of e-scooters?
Key points: Speed limits (often 15 mph / 20–25 km/h), banning riding on pavements/sidewalks, mandatory parking zones, minimum age limits (18+), and slow-speed zones in crowded areas.

📐 Grammar! Modal Verbs: Rules, Obligations & Advice

Stage 03 • Structural Blueprint
Core Grammar Rule
Modal verbs come right before the base verb and never add -s (say "he must stop", never "he musts").
[Subject] + [Modal Verb] + [Base Verb]
must / mustn't Necessary / Forbidden

must = necessary or very important.
mustn't = strictly forbidden (against the rule).

  • • "You must stop at a red light." (Necessary)
  • • "You mustn't smoke in the hospital." (Forbidden)
have to Outside Law / Rule

Shows an obligation that comes from outside—from a law, a school rule, or a company.

  • • "I have to wear a uniform at work." (Required)
  • • "In Spain, you have to be 18 to vote." (The law)
don't have to Not Necessary (Choice)

Means something is NOT necessary—you have a free choice.

  • • "It's Sunday, so I don't have to get up early."
  • • "You don't have to pay—entry is free today."
should / shouldn't Good Idea / Advice

Used to give advice or an opinion—not a strict law, just a smart or bad idea.

  • • "You should wear a helmet." (Good idea)
  • • "You shouldn't eat so late at night." (Bad idea)
can / can't Allowed / Not Allowed

can = permitted or possible.
can't = not allowed or impossible.

  • • "You can pay by card here." (Allowed)
  • • "You can't park in front of the gate." (Not allowed)
⚠️ Classic Exam Trap: mustn't and don't have to are NOT opposites of meaning—they mean completely different things!
• You mustn't park here = Do NOT do it! It is forbidden / illegal.
• You don't have to park here = It isn't necessary (you can park here if you want, or somewhere else).

🃏 Pick a Card!

Stage 05 • Speaking Challenge

Pick a card. Read the situation and say the rule aloud using a modal verb (must, mustn't, have to, don't have to, should, shouldn't, can, can't).

Card 1 • Night Riding

You are riding on the road late at night, but your scooter has no lights at all and the street is very dark. A police officer waves at you to stop.

"You must stop when the police officer waves at you, and you mustn't / can't ride at night without working lights—you have to use front and back lights."
Card 2 • Wet & Slippery

It is raining hard and the road is wet and slippery. Your friend thinks it is funny and wants to race you as fast as the scooters can go.

"We shouldn't race in heavy rain! You should slow down on wet roads, and you don't have to copy your friend."
Card 3 • Park Entrance

There is a big red sign at the entrance to the city park. It shows a picture of a scooter with a line through it.

"You can't / mustn't ride your e-scooter inside the park. You have to leave it outside or walk through on foot."
Card 4 • Texting in Traffic

While riding through traffic, you take out your phone to read a message and reply to it with one hand on the handlebar.

"You mustn't / shouldn't check your phone while riding! You must keep both hands on the handlebars and pull over if you need to reply."
Card 5 • Crowded Square

You are riding across a busy square that is full of people walking, families, and small children, and you are still going quite fast.

"You must slow down immediately—or you should get off and push the scooter so you don't hit anyone."
Card 6 • Low Battery

Your scooter's battery is almost empty and you are still far from home. Luckily, there is a bus stop right next to you and a bus is coming.

"You don't have to walk all the way home! You can park the rental scooter in a marked zone (or fold your own scooter) and take the bus."

📖 Reading! Ban Them or Fix Them?

Stage 06 • Article

A few years ago, e-scooters appeared in our cities almost overnight. Bright green, orange, and pink, they promised a cheap and clean way to travel short distances. You could unlock one with your phone, ride across town, and simply walk away when you arrived. For many people, this felt like the future of city transport. But not everyone was pleased.

The biggest complaint is not really the scooters themselves: it is where riders leave them. Because you don't have to return a scooter to a station, people often drop it anywhere: in the middle of the sidewalk, in front of shops, even in rivers. For a parent with a stroller, or a person who cannot see well, a scooter lying across the path is more than annoying; it can be a real danger. Critics argue that if companies want to work in a city, they must control this mess.

Safety is the other big worry. In most places, the law says you mustn't ride on the sidewalk and you shouldn't look at your phone while riding, but these rules are very hard to enforce. Injuries have risen sharply in recent years, and very few riders wear a helmet. Some people believe that riders should pass a short safety test before their first trip, just as drivers must.

So how have cities reacted? Some have decided that enough is enough. In 2023, Paris became the first European capital to ban rental scooters completely, after its residents voted against them. Other cities, such as Madrid and Melbourne, have followed or are seriously thinking about it. If you visit these places today, you simply can't rent a scooter at all.

Other cities have chosen clever rules instead of a total ban. They tell the companies: you must use technology to fix these problems. Now, in many places, a scooter will slow down automatically in a crowded zone, and it won't lock, so the rider keeps paying, until it is parked correctly. Riders don't have to guess where to leave it, because the app shows them exactly where to go.

Supporters say we shouldn't throw away a useful idea just because it started badly. E-scooters take cars off the road, they help people who can't afford a car, and some studies suggest they may even be safer than e-bikes. The real question, they argue, is not "should scooters exist?" but "how should we share our streets?" Perhaps the answer is simple: good rules, clear parking, and riders who think about the people around them.

Answer the questions below based on the text you just read.

1. What is the biggest problem with e-scooters: the scooters themselves, or something else?
It is where riders leave them—blocking sidewalks, shop entrances, and paths for parents with strollers or people who cannot see well (plus dumped in rivers).
2. Why can't people rent an e-scooter in Paris anymore, and how did the city decide this?
In 2023, Paris banned rental scooters completely after holding a public vote where Paris residents voted against them.
3. How does technology help cities make riders park their scooters correctly?
GPS apps show riders exact parking zones, scooters slow down automatically in crowded areas, and the scooter refuses to lock (so the rider keeps paying) until it is parked inside a marked zone.
4. Do you agree that we shouldn't ban a useful idea just because it started badly? Why or why not?
Open Debate! Try using at least two modal verbs in your answer: "In my opinion, cities shouldn't ban them completely, but companies must..."

Useful phrases: rose gradually from 2014 to 2016 → climbed noticeably in 2017 → shot up / nearly doubled between 2017 and 2018, reaching almost 15,000 injuries.
2. Why do you think the number of injuries has risen so quickly (especially in 2018)?
2017–2018 was the exact moment dockless rental apps (like Bird and Lime) flooded cities with thousands of scooters—meaning first-time riders jumped on without helmets, training, or bike lanes.
3. What must cities do, and what should riders do, to make these numbers fall?
• "Cities must build separate bike lanes and enforce speed limits..."
• "Riders should wear helmets and mustn't check their phones or carry passengers."
