From Today’s Desk: Buy Now, Pay Later, Credit, Fiscal Policy & Chaos Theory

Every phrase from today’s discussion on Klarna, payment plans, banking motives, and unintended consequences. Filter by topic or click Self-Test Mode to test your recall.

▶️ Lesson Video (BNPL)
1 · Upfront
a one-off payment at P.O.S.
2 · Split Delivery
deposit + balance on delivery
3 · BNPL (Klarna)
split into three instalments
4 · Bank Credit
fixed-rate loan & credit rating
5 · Risk Zone
incur fees / default on a loan
To my understanding... / As far as I know... Framing
Natural C1 hedging openers when explaining how a system works without sounding overconfident.
“To my understanding, Klarna splits the bill without charging interest up front.”
P.O.S., pay upfront & one-off payment Payments
P.O.S. = point of sale device. Paying upfront means paying in advance before receiving goods, often as a single one-off payment.
“Paying upfront feels more satisfying because you own it straight away.”
Spread the cost & three instalments BNPL
A payment plan (or credit agreement) delays payment: “separate my payment into three instalments” to lower monthly outgoings.
“Spreading the cost makes purchases seem more appealing and less difficult.”
Deposit + balance on delivery Retail
Buy on credit (like an extendable table!): pay a deposit when you order and pay the balance on delivery.
“As a retailer, why not? Without speaking to anyone, customers click and buy.”
Merchant, rack up debt & default ESL Brains
Merchant = seller. Incur extra fees = become responsible for charges. Rack up debt = build up money owed. Default on a loan = miss required repayments (hurts your credit rating).
Fixed-rate loan vs. compound interest Banking
A fixed-rate loan locks your interest percentage; compound interest charges interest on top of previous interest, so debt snowballs during a rough patch (we don’t know what’s coming around the corner, even after a successful period).
No liability & skeletons in your closet Idiom
“If you are not a liability to the bank, if you have no skeletons in your closet, then the bank will approve.”
(Hidden past financial problems = skeletons in your closet.)
Fiscal policy & central bank paradox Macro
Fiscal policy (government spending & tax), buyer beware, and political motives behind banking:
“If every country is in debt, who is the central bank? Why can’t we just forget the debt?”
Chaos theory & unintended consequences Systems
“A butterfly flaps its wings in Japan and there is a typhoon in Italy.”
Small policy or credit shifts trigger unintended consequences across the whole economy.
Gig economy vs. a monthly paycheck Work
Irregular freelance/platform income (gig economy) vs. predictable salaried stability (a monthly paycheck), which changes how risky BNPL instalments feel.
⚡ 60-Second Recall Check

Quick-Fire Finance & BNPL Drill

Say the phrase out loud before revealing
1. Furniture Order: You pay 20% now and the rest when the lorry arrives: “Pay a ___ when you order, and pay the ___ on delivery.”
“Pay a deposit when you order, and pay the balance on delivery.”
2. Klarna Pitch: What two phrases describe splitting a €300 bill into €100 chunks?
spread the cost / separate my payment into three instalments (lowering monthly outgoings).
3. Loan Risk: What happens if you miss required repayments, and what does it damage?
You default on the loan, incur extra fees, and damage your credit rating.
4. Bank Approval Idiom: “If you have no hidden financial secrets, you have no ___ in your ___.”
“...no skeletons in your closet.”
5. Ripple Effect: One small decision in Tokyo causes a storm in Milan. What two concepts did we link?
Chaos theory (the butterfly effect) and unintended consequences.

From Today’s Desk: Negotiation, Offers, Take Care Of & Honour Idioms

Key language from today’s session on negotiating deals, peak-season staffing, and classic idioms. Filter by topic or click Self-Test Mode to test your recall.

1 · Opening Shock
an insultingly low offer
2 · Pushback
haggle over the price
3 · Composure
keep a poker face
4 · Sweetener
a generous offer & perks
5 · Sealed
Let’s shake on it.
take care of (not about) Grammar
she takes care of the negotiating • take care of yourself!
+I always take good care of my customers.
−He doesn’t take care of his family!
?Do you take care of your diet / your health?
“Why don’t you take better care of yourself?”
Offers & Haggling Over Price Negotiation
to haggle over the price: bargain back and forth (note preposition over).
a high offer • a generous offer: fair, attractive, above expectations.
an insultingly low offer: so low it feels rude or disrespectful.
expedite • conditions / perks Negotiation
we can expedite this process: crisp business verb meaning speed up / accelerate.
conditions / perks: formal contract terms vs. attractive extras (e.g. flexible hours, bonus).
initially: at first / in the beginning.
Poker Face & Disbelief Idioms & Tone
keep a poker face: stay unreadable so the other side can’t guess your reaction.
they were speechless! completely stunned; lost for words.
you’re kidding me! natural reaction of shock or disbelief at an offer.
Peak Season & Staff Cover Workplace
When is your busiest period? → December.
Are you prepared? Good stock levels? enough inventory for peak demand.
Do you make any concessions for your staff? offer flexibility or time off.
we need to organise cover: arrange for someone else to step in.
when it comes to • I want Charles gone C1 Framing
You are a traditionalist! (when it comes to shop opening hours) — use when it comes to + noun/-ing to narrow the focus.
he’s an influencer (modern contrast to a traditionalist).
I want Charles gone: punchy want + object + adjective structure (more direct than I want him to leave).
Let’s shake on it • My word is my bond Sealing Deals
Let’s shake on it. Friendly, decisive way to confirm a deal with a handshake.
my word is my bond: a solemn promise; once I say I’ll do something, you can count on it completely.
Omertà • Sleeps with the Fishes Culture & Idioms
Omertà: the traditional code of silence.
he sleeps with the fishes: classic mobster idiom (The Godfather) meaning someone was eliminated.
In Italian we have a saying: “Better to die with a full stomach than hungry.”
Insurance & a Pane of Glass Collocation
negotiating with the insurance (company) over… (negotiate with + person/firm + over + issue).
Say a pane of glass, a glass window, or a plate-glass window (watch the preposition of — avoid a pane glass window).
2-Minute Active Recall

Quick-Fire Negotiation Check

Say your answer aloud first, then reveal the model.
1. Prepositions: “She takes care ___ the negotiating, while we haggle ___ the price.”
of / over — “She takes care of the negotiating, while we haggle over the price.”
2. Formal Upgrade: Replace speed up with one sharp business verb: “We can ___ this process.”
expedite — “We can expedite this process.”
3. Peak Season: Two staff members want time off in December. What do you need to arrange?
“We need to organise cover.”
4. Punchy Structure: Say “I want Charles to be removed” in 4 crisp words.
“I want Charles gone.”
5. Collocation Fix: “Negotiating with the insurance over a pane glass window.”
“Negotiating with the insurance over a pane of glass” (or a plate-glass window).

Smart & Wearable, Agentic AI & Pronunciation

Expressions, grammar repairs, and pronunciation contrasts from 13 Nov. Use Self-Test Mode to hide the explanations and test your active recall.

Smart Kitchen & Agentic AI Tech Lexis
monitor freshness • alert you when running low
make suggestions for orders / meals • recipes
“Agentic AI can now control your browser and order for you.”
AI Companionship & Sycophancy Discussion
cyber psychosis: losing touch with reality through intense AI use.
they neglect their real lives: fail to look after real-world routines.
it lies to please you: agrees or flatters just to keep you happy.
speak by themselves / on their own Common Slip
Avoid speak by alone.
Use by themselves, on their own, or talk to themselves.
“People start speaking by themselves when they get used to voice AI.”
remember vs. remind Confusable Verbs
remember: hold something in your own memory (“I remember that.”).
remind someone to + verb: prompt someone else not to forget.
“Can you remind me to send that file?” (never remember me to).
annoying vs. annoyed by it when Grammar Pattern
annoying (-ing) = the thing causing irritation.
annoyed (-ed) = how you feel.
Pattern: I’m really annoyed by it when + [Subject + Verb] (uses preparatory it before when).
a lot of help • 100s of designs Countable / Plural
a lot of help (uncountable: never a lot of helps).
100s of designs available (or hundreds of designs — no apostrophe before s).
curly hair vs. hairs on your arm Countable / Weight
curly hair (uncountable: all the hair on your head).
hairs on your arm (countable: individual strands).
43 grams (plural unit after numbers above 1).
Do these appeal to you? • a step back Natural Phrasing
Do these appeal to you? Natural way to ask if options sound interesting or attractive.
take a step back: pause to look at the bigger picture (vs. a step backwards = regression).
autocue / auto-prompt: teleprompter / live on-screen speaking cue.
word (/wɜːd/) vs. world (/wɜːld/) Pronunciation
word (Southern England: “werd”): clean /ɜː/ vowel straight into /d/, no /l/.
world (“wehd / werld”): keep the same /ɜː/ vowel, then lift the tongue tip for the dark /l/ before /d/.
Smart & Wearable: Word Stress Patterns Word Families
oX: rely, accept, impress, protect
oXo: distracting, impressive, protection, permission
oXoo: convenience, reliable, acceptable, convenient
ooXo: independent, independence
