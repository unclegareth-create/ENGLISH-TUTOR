Prompt A • Science, Space & Speculation

Describe an interesting scientific discovery or space mission you have read about recently.

You should say:

  • what the discovery or mission involved
  • when and where you first heard or read about it
  • what scientists have already learned from it (and haven't proved yet)
  • and explain how it could change our view of the universe if further evidence were found.
1. The 1-Minute Note Secret Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin (one per prompt bullet); never write full sentences.
2. The 3-Act Story Arc Past Context (30s) → Sensory Snapshot / Core Scene (45s) → Reflection / 'So What?' (45s).
3. The Cambridge Anchor Bullet points are springboards, not an interrogation checklist. If finished early, deepen the reflection and comparison until halted.

Blueprint 1 • Hypothetical & Inverted 2nd Conditionals Never use 'would' in the if-clause
If + Past Simple, Subject + would / could + Base Verb | Were + Subject + to + Base Verb, Subject + would + Base Verb

Prompt to Upgrade: "If we would find microbial life on Europa, it would transform biology overnight." (Fix the conditional & upgrade to an inverted Were we to... opening)

Standard 2nd Conditional: If we found microbial life on Europa, it would transform biology overnight.
C1 Inverted Upgrade: Were we to find microbial life on Europa, it would transform biology overnight.
Blueprint 2 • Past Modals of Speculation & Deduction Replacing 'Maybe + Past Simple'
Subject + may / might / could (well) + have + Past Participle (V3)

Prompt to Upgrade: "Maybe ancientmars rivers dried up because the planet lost its magnetic field."

Cambridge C1 Model: Ancient Martian rivers may well have dried up because the planet lost its magnetic field.
Blueprint 3 • Present Perfect (Already / Just / Yet) vs. Past Simple Unfinished vs. Finished Time
Subject + have/has + already/just + V3 (+) | Subject + haven't/hasn't + V3 + yet (-) | Subject + have/has yet to + Base Verb

Prompt to Upgrade: "Astronomers launched the telescope in 2021, and they already discovered hundreds of exoplanets, but they didn't find an Earth twin yet."

Cambridge C1 Model: Astronomers launched the telescope in 2021, and they have already discovered hundreds of exoplanets, but they haven't found an Earth twin yet (or: have yet to find an Earth twin).
Blueprint 4 • Wh- Cleft Sentences for Focus Highlighting the Core Factor
What + Subject/Verb Clause + is / was + Noun Phrase / that-clause

Prompt to Upgrade: "Earth's stable atmosphere and liquid oceans make it unique among rocky planets." (Begin with What makes Earth...)

Cambridge C1 Model: What makes Earth unique among rocky planets is the combination of a stable atmosphere and liquid oceans.
Blueprint 5 • Resultative Participle Clauses Replacing ', and this causes...'
[Main Clause], thereby + Present Participle (-ing) + [Object]

Prompt to Upgrade: "Reusable rockets lower the cost of orbital launches, and this opens space research to smaller universities."

Cambridge C1 Model: Reusable rockets lower the cost of orbital launches, thereby opening space research to smaller universities.
