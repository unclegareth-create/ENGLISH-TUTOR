📰 ACM Queue Systems Architecture Digest
The Architecture Modernisation Dilemma: Refactor vs. Replatform
Open Original Article ↗

Software systems that endure long enough inevitably face a reckoning with their foundational architecture. Early design decisions made for rapid feature delivery calcify into structural bottlenecks, turning routine updates into high-stakes deployments.

Engineering teams are frequently tempted by the siren call of the complete rewrite. Yet historical case studies demonstrate that ground-up rewrites almost universally underestimate hidden business rules, take twice as long as projected, and introduce novel regression risks.

In contrast, the strangler-fig pattern advocates deploying intermediary routing layers, incrementally routing traffic to new microservices while the legacy core continues operating. What makes this approach demanding is not the new code, but the rigorous management of dual-write state synchronisation.

Ultimately, architectural maturity is measured not by adherence to trendy design paradigms, but by the clarity with which technical leaders articulate trade-offs to business executives.
