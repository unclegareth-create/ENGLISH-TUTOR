Stage 02 // Receptive Reading

Reading: Cybercrime-as-a-Service Explained: What You Need to Know

Read the briefing below and answer the comprehension question.

📰 TechTarget · Security & Threat Intelligence
Cybercrime-as-a-Service Explained: What You Need to Know
Open Original Article ↗

Historically, carrying out a serious cyberattack required deep programming expertise: criminals had to discover software vulnerabilities, write custom malware from scratch, and build their own network infrastructure.

Today, an underground economy mirrors the legitimate Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) industry. Experienced malware developers rent out ready-made ransomware kits, phishing templates, botnets, and even customer-support negotiation desks on the dark web for a monthly subscription or a percentage of the stolen ransom.

Because the technical barrier to entry has collapsed, almost anyone with basic computer literacy and cryptocurrency can launch attacks that once required a dedicated team of specialists.

1. Why is it now so easy for almost anyone to become a cybercriminal?
Model Summary: Rather than writing custom exploit code from scratch, novice offenders can now rent turnkey hacking kits and ransomware infrastructure on a subscription basis—drastically lowering the technical barrier to entry.

Stage 06 // Communicative Production

Executive Debate • Point & Counterpoint

Select your initial stance on each proposition and defend it. Then switch to ⚡ Devil's Advocate and argue the opposite view.

PROPOSITION 01
“Tech companies should be legally responsible when their products get hacked.”
Executive Follow-Up Angle: Where should the line be drawn between commercial software vendors selling paid enterprise tools versus open-source maintainers whose code is used for free around the world?
PROPOSITION 02
“Companies should have to report every cyberattack publicly.”
Executive Follow-Up Angle: Does immediate public disclosure protect customers, or does announcing a breach before a patch is ready simply invite copycat attackers?
PROPOSITION 03
“Most cybercrime happens because people are careless, not because hackers are clever.”
Executive Follow-Up Angle: When an employee clicks a fake invoice or leaves a supermarket freezer on a default password, is that purely human carelessness—or is it a failure of system design?
🗣️ C1 Argument & Devil's Advocate Framing Toolkit
• Preparatory 'It': "It is widely argued that..." / "It would be unreasonable to expect..."
• Objective Stance: "Evidence indicates that..." / "Critics contend that..."
• Evaluative Counter: "While on the surface it appears..., what actually determines..."
• Concessive Balance: "Compelling though that rationale may seem, in practice..."
