🧩 Part 2 · Candidate Error Buster: Dependent Prepositions

Real errors made by exam candidates in Cambridge tests. Correct the erroneous preposition and lock in the dependent collocation.

Test 1 Training · Ex 1
#01 Candidate Error

A number of customers complained for the after-sales service.

Correct preposition:
complained ABOUT: We complain about a service, situation, or defect (or complain of an illness). Never complain for.
#02 Passive Agent

All the work will be done from volunteers.

Correct preposition:
done BY: In passive constructions, the active agent is introduced by by.
#03 Adjective Collocation

Delegates experienced a number of problems related at the broadband connection.

Correct preposition:
related TO: Something is always related to something else (never related at).
#04 Noun Complement

Alexandra was able to make good use of her knowledge in foreign languages.

Correct preposition:
knowledge OF: We have a good knowledge of a subject or language (though someone may be qualified in a field).
#05 Fixed Idiom

Many people took part to the anniversary festivities.

Correct preposition:
take part IN: Fixed collocation: take part in an event, discussion, or match.
#06 Verb Complement

I must congratulate you for your excellent work.

Correct preposition:
congratulate sb ON sth: A classic Cambridge trap! Praise sb for, but congratulate sb on their success/achievement.
#07 Idiomatic Phrase

I hope my letter will be taken in consideration.

Correct preposition:
take INTO consideration: Fixed C1 academic collocation: take sth into account / into consideration.
#08 Collocation Rule

The inspector drew the management's attention at / on some problems in the workshop.

Correct preposition:
draw attention TO: We draw / call someone's attention to a matter.
#09 Noun Complement

There is an urgent need of fresh water supplies in the region.

Correct preposition:
need FOR: When expressing a requirement for something, we say there is a need for sth (in contrast to in need of).
#10 Noun Collocation

Have you seen the new advertisement of 'Lotus shampoo'?

Correct preposition:
advertisement FOR: An advert/advertisement is for a product or service.

🦔 Official Exam Practice: "Hedgehogs" (Part 2 Open Cloze)

For questions 9–16, think of the word which best fits each gap. Use only one word in each gap.

Test 1 Exam Practice · Q9–16

A hedgehog is a small mammal characterised (0) [BY] the sharp spines which cover its body. (9) hedgehogs are found in many different parts of the world, none is native to either America or Australia.

All species of hedgehog share the ability (10) roll into a tight ball when attacked, (11) that their spines point outwards. The effectiveness of this as a defence mechanism depends, of course, (12) the number of spines the hedgehog has. Some desert hedgehogs have evolved to (13) less weight, and consequently, they have fewer spines and are thus more likely to attempt to run into their attacker, using their ball-rolling ability (14) a last resort.

Hedgehogs are primarily nocturnal and sleep for much of the day, either under cover of bushes (15) in a hole in the ground. Despite the (16) that all hedgehogs can hibernate, not all choose to do so; in suitable conditions some will stay awake all year round.

  • Gap 9: ALTHOUGH / THOUGH / WHILE / WHILST – Concession clause contrasting worldwide distribution with absence in the Americas/Australia.
  • Gap 10: TO – Follows noun 'ability' (ability + to-infinitive).
  • Gap 11: SO – Purpose / result connector: 'so that' (+ subject + verb).
  • Gap 12: ON / UPON – Dependent preposition governed by 'depends': depends ON/UPON.
  • Gap 13: HAVE / CARRY / WEIGH – Bare infinitive after 'evolved to': carry/have less weight or weigh less weight (note: 'have/carry' fits naturally with 'less weight').
  • Gap 14: AS – Preposition expressing function: 'using X as a last resort'.
  • Gap 15: OR – Correlative pair governed by 'either': either... or....
  • Gap 16: FACT – Fixed complex prepositional phrase: despite the FACT that (+ full clause).
