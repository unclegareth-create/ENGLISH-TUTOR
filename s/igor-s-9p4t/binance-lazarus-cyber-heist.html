Task 4

Read the Briefing

Read the two-part analysis aloud or silently. Pay attention to the highlighted expressions in context.

Part 1 · What Happened in the Binance Hack

[Para 1]The Scale & Attribution: Roughly $6.3 billion in digital assets was drained from Binance's hot wallets across 10 blockchains in about 20 minutes—making it the largest crypto heist in history. Blockchain intelligence firms (TRM Labs and Chainalysis) and on-chain sleuth ZachXBT attributed the attack with high confidence to North Korea's state-sponsored Lazarus Group, specifically its TraderTraitor unit.

[Para 2]How They Got In: According to Binance's preliminary investigation, the core private keys weren't leaked. Instead, attackers compromised a DevOps engineer's laptop, hijacked an internal service container, and pushed unauthorized withdrawal requests, using Ethereum EIP-7702 transaction-batching to drain liquid funds at lightning speed.

[Para 3]How Much Was Frozen: Because so much of the haul was in centralized stablecoins and major tokens, issuers moved fast. Tether froze roughly $1.6 billion in USDT, Circle froze over $800 million in USDC, and other protocols locked down funds—freezing roughly $3 billion on-chain so far. Still, over $3 billion in unfreezable assets (like native ETH and BTC) remains in the hackers' control, and DeFi platforms like Aave had to pause markets when Lazarus tried using stolen funds as collateral to borrow wrapped Ether.

[Para 4]Customer Funds: Binance (backed by Yi He and CZ) paused withdrawals temporarily and stated that user balances will be covered 100% via corporate reserves and its SAFU insurance fund.

Part 2 · Why a $6.3B Cyber Heist Still Doesn't Mean a Military Invasion

[Para 5]Does This Cross a Red Line? If you are wondering whether North Korea stealing $3 billion to $6 billion in a single night crosses a red line that forces the US and South Korea to attack militarily, the answer is still no. First, this is North Korea's standard revenue model—just scaled up. Pyongyang has been using Lazarus to rob banks and crypto exchanges for over a decade (Bangladesh Bank in 2016, Ronin Bridge in 2022, Bybit in 2025) specifically to fund its nuclear and ballistic missile programs around UN sanctions.

[Para 6]Not US Sovereign Territory: Second, Binance is an offshore global crypto exchange, not US critical infrastructure (like the power grid or military command networks). The US and South Korea are not going to risk a nuclear exchange and millions of Korean lives to avenge a private crypto exchange's compromised DevOps laptop.

[Para 7]Cyber & Financial Retaliation, Not Kinetic War: Instead, the retaliation will be cyber and financial rather than kinetic. US Cyber Command (USCYBERCOM) and South Korea's cyber warfare units routinely conduct covert offensive cyber operations to disrupt Lazarus servers, burn their laundering infrastructure, and seize illicit wallets. Meanwhile, the DOJ, OFAC, and the US Treasury will aggressively sanction every mixer, OTC broker, and foreign bank that touches the unfrozen $3 billion as Lazarus tries to cash it out over the next several years.

[Para 8]The Bottom Line: It is an absolute earthquake for the crypto industry and a massive windfall for Pyongyang's weapons program if they manage to launder the remaining $3 billion—but the fight over it will happen on-chain and in cyberspace, not across the DMZ.

Task 9

Spot & Fix the Small Slip

Each sentence below sounds almost right, but contains one common grammar, article, or preposition slip that comes up in fast conversation. Spot the error, say or type the fix, then click 👁️ Reveal Cambridge Model to see the explanation.

Slip 1 · Preposition"On-chain investigators attributed the attack with North Korea's Lazarus Group."
✅ "...attributed the attack to North Korea's Lazarus Group."
Why: We always attribute [an effect/action] TO [someone/a cause]. (Compare: credit someone WITH something, but attribute something TO someone.)
Slip 2 · Article"The North Korea has been using cyber heists to bypass sanctions for over a decade."
✅ "North Korea has been using cyber heists..." (No the)
Why: Singular country names take zero article—even when they have an adjective like North Korea, South Korea, or Modern Britain. We only use the when the country name is plural or contains a common noun (the US, the UK, the Netherlands, the DPRK).
Slip 3 · Time Preposition"The attackers drained $6.3 billion from ten blockchains during twenty minutes."
✅ "...drained $6.3 billion in twenty minutes" (or within twenty minutes).
Why: Use in + [duration] to show how fast an action was completed. Use during + [noun event] to say when something happened (during the night, during the investigation)—never before a number of minutes/days/years.
Slip 4 · Like vs. As"Lazarus tried to use the stolen tokens like collateral on Aave to borrow wrapped Ether."
✅ "...tried to use the stolen tokens as collateral on Aave."
Why: Use as + noun for an actual function, role, or legal purpose (use it as collateral, work as an engineer). Use like + noun only for similarity/comparison (it spread like wildfire).
Slip 5 · Purpose"The US and South Korea won't risk a nuclear war for avenge a private crypto exchange."
✅ "...won't risk a nuclear war to avenge a private crypto exchange."
Why: In English, purpose with a verb always uses the infinitive to + base verb (to avenge, to fund, to freeze)—never for + verb.
Slip 6 · Contrast Linker"Despite of losing $6.3 billion, Binance stated that customer balances are 100% covered."
✅ "Despite losing $6.3 billion..." OR "In spite of losing $6.3 billion..."
Why: Never mix the two! Despite never takes of; In spite of always takes of.
Slip 7 · Past Conditional"If Tether and Circle wouldn't have moved so fast, the hackers would have kept all $6.3 billion."
✅ "If Tether and Circle hadn't moved so fast, the hackers would have kept..."
Why: In past counterfactuals (Third Conditional), the if-clause takes the past perfect (had + past participle), while would have + past participle belongs only in the result clause.
Slip 8 · Word Order"Tether and Circle froze on-chain roughly $3 billion within the first few hours."
✅ "Tether and Circle froze roughly $3 billion on-chain within the first few hours."
Why: Keep the verb + direct object together (froze roughly $3 billion) before adding the place/method adverbial (on-chain). Never split a short verb and its object with a manner/place adverbial.
Task 10

Natural Sentence Upgrades for Discussion

Use these two clean structural patterns when you want to emphasize your main point or contrast two courses of action in conversation.

Pattern A · Focusing with a 'What'-Cleft
What + [Subject + Verb] + is / was + [Key Point / 'that'-clause]

Instead of saying: "The attackers didn't crack the encryption; they compromised one engineer's laptop."
Try upgrading: Combine the idea using What actually let the hackers in was...

Model: "What actually let the hackers in was a single compromised DevOps laptop, not a flaw in the blockchain itself."
Pattern B · Contrasting Actions with 'Rather than + V-ing'
Rather than + [Verb-ing]..., [Subject] + will + [Main Verb]...

Instead of saying: "The US won't attack North Korea militarily. They will sanction every broker and attack Lazarus servers."
Try upgrading: Start your sentence with Rather than risking a military conflict...

Model: "Rather than risking a nuclear conflict over an offshore exchange, Washington and Seoul will disrupt Lazarus servers and sanction every broker that touches the funds."
