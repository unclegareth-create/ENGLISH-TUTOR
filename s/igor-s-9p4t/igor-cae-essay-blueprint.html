Writing Part 1: The 4-Paragraph CAE Essay Formula

Why a CAE essay isn't vague "creative writing" — it is a clean, repeatable 4-paragraph structure (220–260 words) where you evaluate two points and drop in four C1 sentence patterns.

2. The Prompt & The 4-Paragraph Formula

220–260 Words · 45 Minutes
Why this is simpler than it looks: In our Friday session you said, "I didn't realise it was that easy." That's because Cambridge examiners mark forty scripts before lunch — they are not looking for poetry or memorised thesaurus words. They want to see a clear 4-paragraph layout that answers both parts of the question without using "I think" in the body paragraphs.

Your Homework Prompt: The Role of Major Sporting Events

Your class has attended a panel discussion on the role of major international sporting events. You have made the notes below:

Three aspects of major sporting events:
  1. enriching people's lives
  2. cost to host countries
  3. influencing young children
Opinions expressed in the discussion:
  • "Nothing brings people together like watching a great tournament."
  • "Billions are spent on stadiums that sit empty a year later."
  • "Seeing elite athletes convinces kids to take up sport."

Task: Write an essay discussing TWO of the points in your notes. You should explain which aspect you think is most significant, giving reasons to support your opinion. (220–260 words)

Paragraph 1: Hook & Scope

~45–50 words · 3 sentences

1. Depersonalised opening (It is widely debated whether...).
2. Frame the tension between public excitement and public money.
3. State the two points your essay will examine.

Paragraph 2: Point 1 (e.g. Cost)

~70 words · 3–4 sentences

1. Clear topic sentence naming your first chosen point.
2. Explain the real-world consequence (debt, empty venues).
3. Close with a participle clause (..., thereby diverting funds from...).

Paragraph 3: Point 2 (e.g. Youth)

~75 words · 3–4 sentences

1. Pivot with a contrast marker (Conversely, / On the other hand,).
2. Use a C1 cleft sentence (What makes these events invaluable is...).
3. Add a negative inversion (Not only do they..., but they also...).

Paragraph 4: The Verdict

~45–50 words · 2–3 sentences

1. Weigh both points in one sentence (In conclusion, while...).
2. Explicitly answer the second question: state which aspect is most significant and why.

3. Worked Model Essay (246 Words)

Points Chosen: Cost (2) & Influencing Youth (3)

Notice how the whole essay uses zero memorised "Band 9 thesaurus clichés" — the C1 mark comes from clean paragraphing and five structural shifts.

4. C1 Sentence Upgrader Workout

5 High-Scoring Syntactic Shifts

Rewrite each flat B2 sentence using the C1 structural blueprint before checking the model.

1. Preparatory 'It' + Passive Reporting (Paragraph 1 Opening)
Formula: It + BE + Adverb + Past Participle + that + Clause

B2 Draft: Many people argue that hosting international tournaments costs too much money for taxpayers.

C1 Upgrade: It is widely argued that staging international tournaments places a disproportionate financial burden on local taxpayers.
2. Resultative Participle Clause (Paragraph 2 Development)
Formula: Main Clause + , thereby + Present Participle (-ing) + Object

B2 Draft: Governments spend billions on temporary stadiums, and this takes money away from schools and hospitals.

C1 Upgrade: Governments frequently pour billions into temporary stadiums, thereby diverting vital funding from schools and healthcare.
3. Wh- Cleft Sentence for Emphasis (Paragraph 3 Focus)
Formula: What + Subject/Verb Phrase + BE + Noun/Clause

B2 Draft: Major tournaments inspire young children to put down their phones and join local sports clubs.

C1 Upgrade: What makes major tournaments truly invaluable is their capacity to inspire young children to join grassroots sports clubs.
4. Negative Inversion (Paragraph 3 Expansion)
Formula: Not only + Auxiliary + Subject + Bare Infinitive , but + Clause

B2 Draft: International championships unite divided communities, and they also encourage long-term physical fitness.

C1 Upgrade: Not only do international championships unite divided communities, but they also foster long-term physical fitness among the youth.
5. Concessive Clause + 'It' Cleft Verdict (Paragraph 4 Conclusion)
Formula: While + Clause , it is + Noun Phrase + that + Verb

B2 Draft: In conclusion, inspiring children is good, but the economic cost to the host country is the most important point.

C1 Upgrade: In conclusion, while inspiring the next generation is undeniably beneficial, it is the economic cost to the host nation that remains the most decisive factor.

5. Use of English Part 4: Key Word Transformation Lab

3 to 6 Words · Never Alter the Keyword

Use the 4-Step Deconstruction Strategy: 1. Strike out the echoes → 2. Check tense harmony → 3. Check voice shift → 4. Solve the collocation.

Question 1 · Passive Reporting + Perfect Infinitive

1. Economists think that the city lost over two billion euros on the tournament.

THOUGHT
The city over two billion euros on the tournament.
Answer: is thought to have lost (5 words)
Deconstruction: Active reporting in the present (think) + past simple action (lost) shifts to passive reporting (is thought) + perfect infinitive (to have lost).
Question 2 · Negative Inversion

2. The organisers never doubted that the new stadium would be ready on time.

AT
the organisers doubt that the new stadium would be ready on time.
Answer: At no time did (4 words)
Deconstruction: Notice that "doubt" is already in the bare infinitive in Sentence 2. Placing a negative time phrase at the start requires past auxiliary inversion (At no time + did).
Question 3 · Contrast Preposition + Gerund/Noun

3. Although the tickets cost a lot, every single match sold out within hours.

SPITE
Every single match sold out within hours of the tickets.
Answer: in spite of the high cost (6 words)
Deconstruction: Concessive conjunction Although + clause shifts to prepositional phrase in spite of + noun phrase (the high cost [of the tickets]).
Question 4 · Noun Collocation + Phrasal Verb

4. The committee does not intend to raise ticket prices for local school children.

INTENTION
The committee up ticket prices for local school children.
Answer: has no intention of putting (5 words)
Deconstruction: Look at the word right after the gap: "up". That tells you raise must become the phrasal verb put up after has no intention of + -ing.
Question 5 · Preference with Past Subjunctive

5. Local residents would prefer the council not to build another stadium in the park.

RATHER
Local residents would another stadium in the park.
Answer: rather the council did not build (6 words)
Deconstruction: When would rather has a different subject (the council), it takes the past simple tense for present/future preference (did not build).
