Tense & Connected Speech

Present Perfect Continuous

  • "Have you been enjoying your ball pit?" — recent, ongoing action.
  • "What have you been working on / up to recently?"
  • "I've been taking a break from work." → natural contraction Ahv-bin
  • "I've been driving for 6 months now." (started 6 months ago, still ongoing).
Grammar & Precision Repairs

Countability & Hedging

  • I don't have much meetings → I don't have many meetings (countable plural).
  • "Not as much as I'd thought I would" — clean past-perfect contrast.
  • "To ensure that..." / "wait a couple of years".
  • "Not as intimidating / daunting" — stronger C1 adjective pair.
Workplace & Attention Lexis

Focus, Overload & Recovery

  • necessitating task switching / competing for your attention
  • mitigate distractions from external sources / deal with tasks instantly
  • keep on top of Slack messages / on top of / up to date
  • a torrent of information (compare: torrential rain)
  • detach yourself / remove yourself • burnout • spare / free / leisure time
Side Notes & Everyday Idiom

Pit, ESP & Dog Chat

  • pit: a pit in the ground, pitfighter, ball pit.
  • ESP: extra sensory perception — a sixth sense.
  • Dog chat: She's overweight, she drools, nibbles your chin, pay attention to her, stroke, treats.
  • Tone check: frustrating or calming? • introduce themselves • aspects.

🧩 Sensory Overload Collocation Workout

ESL Brains • Gap-Fill Recall

Complete each sentence with the exact word from Tuesday's worksheet, then reveal the answer to check.

1. Rapid switching between tasks can ____________ your working memory, so take short breaks outside.

flood — flood your working memory (overwhelm it with too much detail at once).

2. Constant email notifications ____________ our attention span, making deep focus harder.

shrink — a shrinking attention span.

3. Reduce unnecessary energy ____________ on digital admin by automating routine tasks.

expenditure — high energy expenditure (how much mental fuel a task burns).

4. Reduce the number of decisions that ____________ mental resources by using pre-written templates.

exhaust — exhaust mental resources.

5. Frequent meetings expose you to a constant ____________ of information, leaving little quiet time to process it.

influx — a constant influx of information (or from our scratchpad: a torrent of information).

6. After a demanding call, take ten minutes to ____________ without trying to be productive.

unwind — hard to unwind (relax and switch off after work).

⚙️ C1 Structure & Key Word Transformations

3–6 Words • Keyword Unchanged
Present Perfect Continuous vs. Simple Have / Has + been + -ing (ongoing activity / duration)  |  Have / Has + past participle (completed result)

Complete the second sentence using 3 to 6 words, including the key word in capitals. Do not change the key word.

1. Constantly checking Slack stops me from concentrating on deep research.
PREVENTED
I am on deep research by constantly checking Slack.

prevented from concentrating (3 words) — passive shift with prevent someone from + -ing.

2. The notifications were so frequent that she couldn't finish her report.
SUCH
There was of notifications that she couldn't finish her report.

such a constant influx (4 words) — such + a/an + adjective + noun + that.

3. I started driving six months ago.
BEEN
I for six months.

have been driving (3 words) — Present Perfect Continuous for an action started in the past and continuing now.

4. It is a good idea to step away from your desk to ensure you avoid burnout.
ORDER
You should step away from your desk burnout.

in order to avoid (4 words) — formal purpose clause replacing to ensure you avoid.

🏛️ Friday Debate: Three Company Solutions

Page 4 Follow-Up • Critical Evaluation

We will open Friday's discussion by evaluating which of these three policies would survive contact with a real engineering or research team.

1. Financial Services: Monthly Social

Problem: Long solo hours in front of screens with international clients.

Proposed Fix: Monthly team lunches, trivia quizzes, and escape rooms.

2. Digital Marketing: No-Screen Day

Problem: Daily eye strain, headaches, and mental exhaustion.

Proposed Fix: Weekly device ban with Wi-Fi switched off; pen-and-paper meetings.

3. Data Analysis: Four-Day Week

Problem: Heavy statistical modelling and complex reports all day indoors.

Proposed Fix: One full day off per week outdoors or with friends and family.

  • A Which solution treats the symptom rather than the root cause of sensory overload?
  • B If you could design one realistic policy for an AI research team without hurting output, what would it look like?
