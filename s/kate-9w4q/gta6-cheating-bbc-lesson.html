Part 2 · Unabridged BBC News Text

'It gives me the ick': Can you cheat on someone with a video game?

By Jim Reed, Health Reporter · BBC News · Published 19 October 2026

Imagine your partner spending hours flirting with someone else, trying to hold their hand and taking them on dates. Most people would have questions.

But what if that someone only exists inside a video game?

The latest instalment in Rockstar's blockbuster Grand Theft Auto franchise is set to be the biggest entertainment launch of the year when it is released next month.

Judging by previews, the connection between its two main characters is a central feature.

Rockstar has hinted that the Bonnie and Clyde-type protagonists Jason and Lucia do not have to become a full-on romantic couple.

But their relationship can grow by spending time together outside of the main crime and life-on-the-run storyline – through shopping, visiting bars and restaurants and working out together.

Smaller interactions like holding hands and quickly responding to each other's texts can help build a bond.

The idea has restarted a debate almost as old as gaming itself: if you are that invested in a fictional relationship, is your real-life partner entitled to be upset?

In September, polling company YouGov put that question to more than 4,000 British adults.

About 6% said they would regard it as cheating, while a further 19% said they would "not be okay with it". Women were considerably more likely than men to take a dim view.

Alli Franco, a 30-year-old Texan who posts anime and gaming content to more than a million followers on TikTok, says she felt "dismayed" watching the debate unfold online.

"If your partner is insecure about a fictional character, then there needs to be some serious reflection," she told the BBC.

"It's similar to having a crush on a TV star, but even further removed from cheating since there is no other actual person involved."

After Franco posted a video on the subject, hundreds of commenters appeared to agree with her, although some did have reservations.

"To be honest, it depends," wrote one. "If you're cancelling plans with your significant other or directing your intimacy towards a fictional character, then that is a little odd."

"It's very odd and it really gives me the ick," one woman told us.

Mimicking social relationships

For decades, video games have allowed players to build relationships with non-player characters, or NPCs.

Gamers could marry villagers in Harvest Moon in 1996, shape emotional storylines in Final Fantasy VII and pursue more complex romances in role-playing games like Baldur's Gate II.

In life simulator The Sims, they could flirt, date, marry and have children with computer-controlled characters.

But all those interactions were tightly scripted, based on rules and storylines written in advance by game developers.

GTA 6 appears to offer something more dynamic, allowing players to shape the relationship between Jason and Lucia through a wider range of open-world interactions and choices.

"The more sophisticated games get, the less likely it is that players get pulled out of the experience," says Arienne Ferchaud, an associate professor at Florida State University who studies how people form connections with fictional characters.

"Games are doing a better job of mimicking reality and mimicking social relationships."

Grand Theft Auto 6 may represent the current cutting edge of game design, but the next leap forward could come from artificial intelligence.

Nvidia, Ubisoft and other huge tech companies are already experimenting with generative AI systems that allow players to hold more natural conversations with NPCs.

Future characters could remember past encounters, react to a player's actions and build relationships that change over time rather than following a pre-written script.

Ferchaud believes that could make games feel even more immersive.

"You could have relationships that feel more solid or more special," she says, although she argues AI as it exists today would still be creating "a stronger illusion" rather than a genuinely meaningful connection.

Whether that is exciting or unsettling may depend on the player, and there has been some resistance to the wider use of the technology in gaming.

Franco says she is already seeing adverts for AI chatbots that let fans talk to their favourite anime characters.

"I can definitely see AI integrating into games and making it a more realistic experience," she says.

"But should that happen I would still see it as just a game. At the end of the day you can't cheat with lines of code."

Part 3 · Reading Between the Lines

Close Reading & Nuance Check

Before looking at the vocabulary, test your grasp of the arguments, statistics, and subtle distinctions in the BBC report.

1. What did the YouGov poll of 4,000 British adults actually find, and how did men and women differ?
A quarter of British adults (25% total) were unhappy with the idea: 6% called it outright cheating, and another 19% said they would "not be okay with it". Women were significantly more likely than men to disapprove ("take a dim view").
2. Why does TikTok creator Alli Franco argue that an in-game romance is "even further removed from cheating" than having a crush on a TV star?
Because a TV star is at least a real, living human being, whereas a video game character is purely fictional—there is no actual person on the other end, only "lines of code".
3. Where did the more moderate commenters draw the line between harmless gaming and a genuine relationship problem?
When it starts replacing real life—specifically if someone starts cancelling plans with their partner or directing their real emotional intimacy toward the screen instead of their partner.
4. According to Professor Arienne Ferchaud, how will generative AI change non-player characters (NPCs), and why does she still call it "a stronger illusion"?
Instead of repeating pre-written scripts like older games (The Sims, Harvest Moon), AI characters will remember past conversations and adapt over time. However, she calls it a "stronger illusion" because the AI still doesn't have real feelings—it only imitates human connection more convincingly.
Part 4 · Vocabulary in Context

16 High-Value Idioms & Expressions from the BBC Article

1. to give (someone) the ick
"...it really gives me the ick..."
Modern British/slang idiom: to cause a sudden feeling of cringe, turn-off, or mild disgust towards someone you are dating.
2. to take a dim view (of)
"Women were considerably more likely than men to take a dim view."
Classic British C1/C2 idiom: to disapprove of something or regard it with scepticism and displeasure.
3. an instalment
"The latest instalment in Rockstar's blockbuster... franchise..."
One new part, episode, or release in a long-running series of books, films, or games.
4. full-on
"...do not have to become a full-on romantic couple."
Complete, intense, or total; holding nothing back.
5. life on the run
"...outside of the main crime and life-on-the-run storyline..."
Living while hiding from the police or the law; constantly moving to avoid capture.
6. invested in (something)
"...if you are that invested in a fictional relationship..."
Putting a lot of your time, emotion, and care into something so that it matters deeply to you.
7. entitled to (feel / do)
"...is your real-life partner entitled to be upset?"
Having a fair, reasonable right to feel or do something.
8. dismayed
"...says she felt 'dismayed' watching the debate unfold..."
Shocked, disappointed, and disheartened by something unexpected.
9. far / further removed from
"...even further removed from cheating since there is no other actual person..."
Very different or distant from something; having very little connection to it.
10. to have reservations
"...although some did have reservations."
To have lingering doubts or mixed feelings about whether something is a good idea.
11. significant other
"If you're cancelling plans with your significant other..."
Your romantic partner (husband, wife, boyfriend, or girlfriend).
12. pulled out of (an experience)
"...less likely it is that players get pulled out of the experience..."
When something breaks the spell or reminds you that a story/game isn't real.
13. to mimic (reality)
"Games are doing a better job of mimicking reality..."
To copy or imitate the way someone or something looks, sounds, or behaves.
14. the cutting edge
"...may represent the current cutting edge of game design..."
The newest, most advanced stage of development in a field.
15. immersive
"Ferchaud believes that could make games feel even more immersive."
So detailed and surrounding that you feel completely inside that world.
16. at the end of the day
"At the end of the day you can't cheat with lines of code."
When all is said and done; looking at the most basic, important fact of a situation.

Part 5 · Drawing the Line

The Relationship Boundary Judge: Harmless Fun or Crossing the Line?

Read the three real-world scenarios on the spectrum below. Give your verdict on each one aloud, explaining why using at least two expressions from today's lesson (take a dim view, have reservations, give someone the ick, far removed from, invested in, entitled to, at the end of the day).

Case 1: The GTA 6 Storyline
Your partner plays GTA 6 for an hour after work. As part of the game, their character takes the other main character out to bars, holds hands, and replies to in-game texts to unlock a bonus mission.
C1 Model Response: "Honestly, this is completely far removed from real cheating. It's just part of the game's storyline. At the end of the day, you can't cheat with lines of code, so I wouldn't have any reservations about it at all."
Case 2: The Late-Night AI Companion
Your partner downloads a custom AI voice chatbot on their phone. Every night for two hours, they put on headphones to flirt and talk about their deepest personal problems with the AI—and they cancel a Friday dinner date with you because they "just want to stay home and chat with the bot".
C1 Model Response: "Even if there's no real human on the other end, any partner is entitled to be upset here. Once someone is that emotionally invested in a chatbot and starts cancelling plans with their significant other, I'd take a very dim view of it—and frankly, it would give me the ick."
Case 3: The Secret Subscription
Your partner isn't talking to a real person, but you discover they are paying £50 a month to a custom AI generator that creates romantic messages and voice notes tailored specifically to their name.
C1 Model Response: "Whether you call it 'cheating' or not, spending £50 a month on a fake AI romance is a huge red flag. It creates what the professor in the article called 'a stronger illusion', and hiding it from your partner is what really crosses the line."
Part 6 · C1 Structural Mastery

Rhetorical Precision & Key Word Transformations

Structural Blueprint 1 · Parallel Double Comparatives (From the Article)
The + comparative [adj/adv] + clause, the + comparative [adj/adv] + clause
Structural Blueprint 2 · Inverted First Conditional with "Should" (From the Final Paragraph)
Should + Subject + Base Verb ..., Subject + would/will + Base Verb
Structural Blueprint 3 · British Idiomatic Approval / Disapproval
take a dim view of + Noun / -ing  |  have reservations about + Noun / -ing

Cambridge Part 4 Rules: Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence, using the word given. Do not change the word given. You must use between three and six words, including the word given.

1. As games become more sophisticated, players are less likely to get pulled out of the experience.
THE
The more sophisticated games get, ________________________ that players get pulled out of the experience.
the less likely it is (5 words) — Exact quote from Professor Arienne Ferchaud in the article.
2. Even if AI is integrated into future games, I will still see it as just a game.
SHOULD
________________________, I would still see it as just a game.
Should that happen (3 words) — Exact inverted conditional from Alli Franco's final quote ("But should that happen I would still see it as just a game").
3. Women were much more likely than men to disapprove of their partner dating a game character.
DIM
Women were considerably more likely than men ________________________ of their partner dating a game character.
to take a dim view (5 words) — "Take a dim view of" is a top-tier British English collocation for formal and semi-formal writing.
4. Hundreds of people agreed with her video, though a few still had doubts about the idea.
RESERVATIONS
Hundreds of people agreed with her video, though a few ________________________ the idea.
still had reservations about / did have reservations about (4 words) — Always pair "reservations" with the preposition "about".
5. If your partner spends hours flirting with a chatbot, you have every right to feel upset.
ENTITLED
You ________________________ upset if your partner spends hours flirting with a chatbot.
are entitled to feel / are entitled to be (4 words).
6. Dating a video game character is completely different from real-life infidelity.
REMOVED
Dating a video game character ________________________ real-life infidelity.
is far removed from (4 words) — Note the fixed preposition: "far removed from".
7. In the past, game developers wrote all those interactions in advance.
WRITTEN
In the past, all those interactions ________________________ by game developers.
were written in advance (4 words) — Active-to-passive transformation with the fixed phrase "in advance".
8. Watching him cancel our anniversary dinner to play a video game completely turned me off.
ICK
Watching him cancel our anniversary dinner to play a video game really ________________________.
gave me the ick (4 words) — Colloquial British idiom from the headline.
Part 7 · Speaking Part 3 & Live Debate

Deep-Dive Discussion & Roleplay Challenge

1. In the YouGov poll, 25% of British adults said they would either consider it cheating (6%) or "not be okay with it" (19%), and women were much more likely than men to take a dim view. Why do you think there is such a sharp divide on this?

→ Is jealousy always about another real person, or is it about where your partner's time and attention are going?

2. Professor Arienne Ferchaud says AI characters that remember your past conversations will create "a stronger illusion" rather than a genuinely meaningful connection. As lonely people spend more time talking to AI companions, could that "illusion" stop them from forming real relationships?

→ Why is a relationship with an AI "easier" than a real human relationship, and why is that dangerous?

3. The phrase "it gives me the ick" has become one of the most popular expressions in modern English. What kinds of everyday habits—especially around phones, social media, or gaming—give people "the ick" today?

→ Has technology made people more critical and impatient in modern dating?

4. The article mentions that there has been "resistance to the wider use of AI in gaming." Why do many gamers and film fans prefer stories written by human writers over endless AI-generated dialogue?

→ Does knowing a story was crafted by a human being change how much we care about it?
