Lesson 3: What Are You Curious About? & Indirect Questions with “I wonder...”

Speaking fluent, natural indirect questions (“I wonder where Lucy is”, “I was wondering if...”), exploring curiosity across professions & life stages, and deep-diving into the ESL Brains video lab.

🎨 Launch Lesson 3 Studio Deck → ⚡ 8 Interactive Steps • Speaking Warm-Up • Video Lab
🎙️ Active Voice Warm-Up • 2-Minute Long Turn Challenge

Describe a memorable holiday or journey you took recently.

You should say:

  • where you went and how you travelled there
  • who you went with and where you stayed
  • what activities and food you enjoyed most
  • and explain why you would recommend this trip to others.
1. The 1-Min Note Secret Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin. Never write full sentences.
2. The 3-Act Story Arc Past Context (30s) → Sensory Snapshot (45s) → Reflection / 'So What?' (45s).
3. Cambridge Anchor Bullet points are springboards, not an interrogation checklist. Deepen reflection!

From Today's Desk: Normandy Journey, Autumn Weather, Chemo Pronunciation & Indirect Questions

Spoken diagnostic notes from our live discussion on your Normandy holiday, driving back across the mainland, British vs American English, historical dentistry, and mastering indirect questions. Use Self-Test Mode to hide the explanations and test your active recall.

Chemo (/ˈkiːməʊ/) Pronunciation • Medical
Target Pronunciation: /ˈkiːməʊ/ → “kee-mo” (hard ‘k’ sound, like chemist or chemistry).
Live Context: Medical and clinical patient conversations.
Trap: Never pronounce with a soft “ch” (*cheh-mo).
I wonder where we will go… Indirect Question Trap
Trap: *“I wonder where will we go when my husband’s daughter comes?” (question inversion).
Rule: Indirect/embedded questions use statement word order: Subject + Verb.
→ “I wonder where we will go when my husband’s daughter comes.”
…what the main activity of sloths is Word Order Precision
Direct Question: “What is the main activity of sloths?”
Indirect Clause: “I wonder what the main activity of sloths is.”
Key Check: The verb is moves to the end of the noun phrase clause.
Reported Questions (how my drive was) Reported Speech
Direct: “How was your drive from Normandy?”
Reported: “He asked me how my drive from Normandy was…”
Rule: Statement order (Subject + Verb) with past tense harmony: was.
Autumn vs. Fall & Seasonal Weather British vs US • Weather
Seasons: Autumn (British English) vs. Fall (American English).
Weather Collocations: foggy, cold, muddy (typical late autumn / winter weather).
Journey: “a long journey across the mainland from Normandy.”
Chips, Crisps & Pommes Frites Food Lexis • UK vs US
European: pommes frites → UK: chips | US: French fries.
Snacks: UK: crisps | US: potato chips.
Culinary Lexis: risotto, buckwheat (in a salad).
100 Years Ago in Dentistry Dental History • Lexis
Live Question: “I wonder how nervous patients had dental work 100 years ago? …what the treatment was like?”
Historical Practice: physical restraints (strapping patients down before modern anaesthesia and gentle sedation).
I haven't seen her for 10 years! Present Perfect • Duration
Rule: Use haven't seen + FOR + [duration] for a time span continuing up to today.
Live Context: “I haven’t seen her for 10 years!”
Trap: Avoid Past Simple (*I didn't see her for 10 years) when the separation connects to now.
