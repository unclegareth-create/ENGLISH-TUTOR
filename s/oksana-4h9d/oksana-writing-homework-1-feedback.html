Four Simple Patterns We Will Polish Together

Your ideas are spot on—we just need to tidy up these 4 habits!
Pattern 1 · Articles & Uncountable Nouns High Priority

In English, news is uncountable, so it never takes "a" (What a wonderful news! → What wonderful news!). Meanwhile, singular countable places and things always need a little helper word (a / an / the):

  • "it is a mining museum"
  • "there is a café"
  • "is the Planetarium"
Pattern 2 · Untangling German Habits L1 Interference

Because you use German every day in Bochum, a few German rules sneak into your English sentences:

  • Mid-sentence Capitals: Only proper names get capitals in English—keep restaurants, bars, minutes, adrenaline, universe lowercase.
  • German Slips: ist → it's · Adrenalin → adrenaline · programm → programme.
Pattern 3 · Natural Word Pairs & Verb Patterns Collocations

Small preposition and verb choices make your English sound effortlessly natural:

  • suggest + -ing: "I would suggest driving to Essen" (never suggest to drive).
  • fond of: "I am not fond of such spectacles" (not fond on).
  • take a seat / lie down: Sit is the verb, a seat is the noun; lie down means recline your body.
Pattern 4 · Contractions & Double Letters Polish

Quick final-check wins for informal emails and letters:

  • Apostrophes: Remember the apostrophe in don't (do not) and it's (it is).
  • Spelling Traps: definitely (no 'a' or second 'e'), terrace, disappointed, example.

🌟 Polished Natural Version (After) Warm, Fluent B2/C1 Email

Hello Chris, What wonderful news!
You are also very welcome to stay at my place if you like—we have a guest room ready for you.

As you know, Bochum is an industrial city, but it still has some really lovely sides to it. You should definitely visit the Deutsches Bergbau-Museum, which is a mining museum. I think it is even the largest in the world! You can explore authentic mining equipment right in action. I have never been there myself, but my daughter told me she had a great time.

If you are interested in art, you can visit the Art Museum. It is located in a city park, and there is a café and a roof terrace, so you can enjoy a beautiful view. Very close to the central park is the Planetarium. You can go there on your own, take a seat, or even lie down and observe the whole universe. If you are lucky enough to get a ticket, there is also the chance to see Starlight Express—it's a very popular musical where the actors perform on roller skates. It will definitely be an experience full of adrenaline! I am not really fond of such spectacles myself, but I have heard that people from other cities come to visit Bochum just for Starlight Express.

I know you are a big fan of Italian food. We don't have a huge variety of really good restaurants in Bochum, so I would suggest driving over to Essen and having a delicious evening at Omero Restaurant. The chef and owner is a friend of ours, so believe me, you will not be disappointed. Otherwise, you can head to the Bermuda Triangle (Bermuda3Eck)—it's a lively part of the city with nightlife, restaurants, bars, pubs, and a cinema. You should try a Currywurst there; it's a grilled sausage with a spiced tomato curry sauce, which Bochum is famous for.

I hope this gives you a little idea of what to do while you are in our city. Just let me know if you have a plan and I can help you organise tickets or book a table at a restaurant. For example, at the moment in Leverkusen (about a 45-minute drive from Bochum) there is a jazz festival with famous musicians. So tell me if you are interested, and I will gladly prepare a programme for you!
