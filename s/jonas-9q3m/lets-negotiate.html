1 What is Negotiation?

Foundational Framework
negotiation /nɪˌɡəʊʃiˈeɪʃn/ (noun)

A discussion in which two or more people or groups try to reach an agreement, often by discussing what each side (or more formally: party) wants.

3 The Functional Phrase Matrix

Core Collocations

In professional English, negotiations advance through three distinct pragmatic phases. Mastering these precise formulas ensures you establish anchors, offer measured concessions, and lock in binding agreements without sounding aggressive.

🖈 Starting Negotiations
A reasonable starting point would be...
• Establishes the anchor without claiming finality.
My initial offer is something in the region of...
• Indicates an approximate, negotiable ballpark figure.
🔁 Responding & Bargaining
We could make some concessions on... if you...
• Quid pro quo trade-off framing.
As a gesture of goodwill, I could...
• Framing a compromise as deliberate generosity.
I'd be willing to go as far as..., provided that...
• Defining the outer boundary with a strict condition.
Our original offer was..., but I could stretch to...
• Showing flexibility while signalling financial limits.
🤝 Reaching an Agreement
On the condition that..., we'd be happy to proceed...
• Conditional acceptance ready for contract execution.
I think we're at the point where we can agree on...
• Locking in convergence and closing the deal.

4 Precision Gap-Fill & Strategic Reaction

Live Practice

Type the exact missing collocation keyword into each gap, then decide which response strategy you would deploy in each real-life scenario:

A. Your employer offers a 2% pay rise. You were hoping for something closer to 10%.
1. As a gesture of , I'll accept the offer, but I'll be hoping for a larger raise next year.
2. I think a reasonable point would be somewhere around 10% for what I have achieved this year.
Key: 1. goodwill • 2. starting
Tactical Note: Option 2 firmly rejects the low anchor by re-anchoring at 10% tied to documented accomplishments. Option 1 surrenders leverage in exchange for vague future hopes.
B. Buying a second-hand sofa online. Seller asks €600. You offered €400. You raised to €450; seller came down to €550.
1. I'd be to go as far as €475, provided that delivery is included.
2. I think my offer was something in the region of €400 and I've already offered more, so you'll have to take it or leave it I'm afraid.
Key: 1. willing • 2. initial
Tactical Note: Option 1 introduces an auxiliary value trade-off (delivery) to justify bumping the price. Option 2 invokes a hard boundary ("take it or leave it").
C. Negotiating with a bathroom renovation contractor. Details agreed; price pending; June deadline essential.
1. If everything can be completed by the end of June, we'd be happy to and agree on the final price.
2. I could to around €5,000. Would that work for you?
Key: 1. proceed • 2. stretch
Tactical Note: Option 1 makes timeline the definitive condition precedent. Option 2 uses "stretch" to show you are hitting your budgetary threshold.
D. Negotiating with a catering company for a family celebration. Offered package: €900 (above budget, but sole vendor).
1. I think we're at the where we can agree on the price. You are definitely available, right?
2. Do you think you could make some on your final price? It's a little steep.
Key: 1. point • 2. concessions
Tactical Note: Option 2 politely probes for room to manoeuvre ("make some concessions") without burning bridges with the only available vendor.

5 Advanced Sentence Upgrades • Keyword Transformations

C1/C2 Reformulation

Upgrade each scenario using the mandatory bracketed words to produce fluent, professional negotiation counter-proposals:

A. "We were expecting to pay around €5,000 for the website, but we'd like to hear your proposal." [starting / region]
• "A reasonable starting point would be €5,000, though we are keen to hear your formal proposal."
• "Our initial offer is something in the region of €5,000, but we would welcome your detailed breakdown."
B. "At €10,000, I still think the car is a bit too expensive." [goodwill / willing]
• "I'd be willing to go as far as €9,000, provided that the full service and registration are included."
• "As a gesture of goodwill, would you be prepared to extend the warranty if we agree to that figure?"
C. "Material prices have increased, so it looks like the final price of the garage conversion will be more like €12,000." [concession / stretch]
• "Our original budget was €10,000, but I could stretch to €11,000 if you would consider a staged payment plan."
• "Could you make some concessions on the labour charges to offset the surge in material costs?"
D. "The house owners have accepted your offer, so all that is left is for you to agree to the purchase." [point / proceed]
• "I believe we are now at the point where we can agree on the final contract and sign today."
• "On the condition that the structural survey raises no red flags, we would be delighted to proceed."
E. "The rent is getting more and more expensive and we are paying extra for the parking space, too." [willing / concession]
• "We would be willing to commit to a 24-month lease provided you make some concessions on the parking space fees."
• "Were you to make a concession on the monthly rent, we would be willing to absorb the parking cost."
F. "There's been a lot of interest in the painting, so you may need to reconsider your offer. If you don't increase your offer, I'm afraid you're unlikely to get the painting." [willing / region]
• "My initial offer was €700, but I could offer something in the region of €850 if we conclude the sale immediately."
• "I would be willing to go as far as €900, but that represents the absolute upper limit of my budget."

🎓 C2 Inversions & Clefts for Elite Negotiation (Abitur LK & C2)

Elevating basic conditional statements into authoritative diplomatic discourse:

Inverted Conditional (Were) "Were you to guarantee completion within three weeks, we would be prepared to increase our deposit."
Inverted Conditional (Should) "Should unexpected material costs arise, we would expect immediate written notification."
Pseudo-Cleft Focus "What concerns us most is not the upfront expenditure, but the strictness of the delivery deadline."
Cleft Sentence (It-Cleft) "It is on the condition of guaranteed craftsmanship that we are willing to proceed."
