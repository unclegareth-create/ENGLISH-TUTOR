Grammar Blueprint · Indirect Questions

How “I Wonder…” Works

Indirect questions with I wonder express curiosity or soften requests. They always use statement word order.

Word-Order Clinic
Direct (Verb-Subj) WhereWh-Word isVerb LucySubject ?
Indirect (Subj-Verb) I wonder whereWh-Word LucySubject isVerb .Full Stop
Drop Auxiliary I wonder what timeWh-Phrase doesDrop the trainSubject leavesConjugate .

1. Statement Word Order

Use Subject + Verb. Never keep do, does, or did inside the indirect clause: “I wonder why the price changed.”

2. Wh-Words vs. If / Whether

Keep what, who, when, where, why, how. For Yes/No questions, insert if or whether: “I wonder whether we should wait.”

3. Variations in Tense

I wonder (thinking out loud) • I’m wondering (active curiosity / soft request) • I was wondering (polite favour).

4. Full-Stop Punctuation

Sentences beginning with I wonder are grammatically statements, so finish with a full stop (.) rather than a question mark.

Verb Pattern Common Reporting Verbs Example Structure
Verb + -ing admit, deny, regret, suggest, recommend He denied stealing the documents.
Verb + Preposition + -ing apologise for, insist on, accuse (sb) of, blame (sb) for, congratulate (sb) on She apologised for not telling us the truth.
