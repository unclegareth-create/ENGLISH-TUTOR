Precision Clinic · Indirect Questions

How "I wonder..." Works

Indirect questions with "I wonder..." express curiosity, let you think out loud, or soften a request. They use statement word order—never question inversion.

Blueprint: I wonder + [Question Word / if / whether] + Subject + Verb
Direct: Where is Lucy? → I wonder where Lucy is. (not: where is Lucy)
Direct: What time does the train leave? → I wonder what time the train leaves. (not: does the train leave)

1. No Auxiliary Do / Does / Did

Drop do, does, did inside the indirect clause and conjugate the main verb normally: "I wonder why the price changed."

2. Question Words vs. If / Whether

Keep what, who, when, where, why, how. For Yes/No questions, use if or whether: "I wonder whether we should wait."

3. Tense Variations

I wonder (thinking out loud) • I'm wondering if... (active curiosity right now) • I was wondering if... (polite request).

4. Punctuation Rule

Sentences starting with I wonder are grammatically statements—end them with a full stop (.), not a question mark.

Quick Spoken Repair

Fix the Word-Order Trap

Say each sentence aloud with the correct indirect word order, then click to check.

1. Direct: "How do GPUs scale so fast?" → Start with I've always wondered...
I've always wondered how GPUs scale so fast. (Drop do; keep Subject + Verb.)
2. Direct: "Did she receive my email?" → Start with I wonder...
I wonder if / whether she received my email. (Yes/No question uses if/whether + past simple received.)
3. Direct: "Are you free tomorrow?" → Soften politely with I was wondering...
I was wondering if you are free tomorrow. (Full stop at the end.)

CAE Use of English Part 4 · Reference Blueprint

Core Reporting Verb Patterns

At C1 level, the key word is often a reporting verb that locks you into one specific grammatical frame. Keep the key word 100% unchanged and use 3 to 6 words.

Verb Pattern Common Reporting Verbs Example Structure
Verb + -ing admit, deny, regret, suggest, recommend He denied stealing the documents.
Verb + Preposition + -ing apologise for, insist on, accuse (sb) of, blame (sb) for, congratulate (sb) on She apologised for not telling us the truth.
Verb + (not) + to + Infinitive agree, claim, offer, promise, refuse, threaten They promised not to let the team down.
Verb + Object + (not) + to + Infinitive advise, encourage, persuade, remind, urge, warn The guide warned us not to leave the path.
Verb + that + Subject + (should) + Bare Infinitive demand, insist, recommend, suggest Peter suggested that Mary try on the jacket.
