global & at our fingertips

Pro vs Con
Meaning: Immediate global access to information and people whenever needed (instant — pro vs con: brilliant for learning, yet makes distractions harder to ignore).

people are easier to reach

Syntax Repair
Repair: *easy-reach → People are easier to reach or It is easier to reach people.
Never join easy and reach into a compound adjective after be.

relatives REL-ah-tives

Word Stress
Pronunciation: /ˈrel.ə.tɪvz/ — stress syllable 1 (REL) with a weak schwa in the middle, never the second syllable like re-LA-ted.

A.R.E.A. Speaking Strategy

Part 4 Blueprint
Answer (direct) → Reason (why?) → Example (e.g. minimum wage rules or policies that stifle competition) → Alternative.

governments intervene & stifle

C1 Verbs & Spelling
stifle = suppress: Prevent ideas or market rivals from growing (stifle competition).
Spelling note: suppress takes double -pp- and double -ss-.

industrial espionage

C1 Collocation
Meaning: Theft of trade secrets, proprietary code, or research by rival companies or foreign states — high-value vocabulary for tech regulation questions.

It has been shown that… + less intelligent

Passive & Register
Repair: my generation is stupider → It has been shown that my generation is less intelligent…
Combines a C1 passive reporting opener with measured academic phrasing.

problem-solving & communication skills

Compound Noun
Usage: Hyphenate problem-solving when it modifies a noun (problem-solving skills) alongside communication skills.

I’ve seen it in myself & I would imagine that

Spoken Precision
Two natural anchors: Use “I’ve seen it in myself” to give honest personal evidence, and “I would imagine that…” to speculate smoothly without sounding dogmatic.

due to / on account of vs because + thereby + -ing

Cause & Result
Clause vs Noun: because + Subject + Verb vs due to / as a result of / on account of + Noun / -ing.
Result: “…more tools competing for your attention, thereby creating constant distractions.”

📐 Stage 3: C1 Structural Blueprints & Upgrade Drills

5 Live Sentence Repairs
1. Tough-Movement vs Preparatory ‘It’ [Person] + BE + easier to + [Verb]  ↔  It + BE + easier to + [Verb] + [Person]
2. Reason Clauses vs Noun Linkers & Resultative Participle because + [Subject + Verb]  |  due to / as a result of / on account of + [Noun / -ing]  |  [Clause], thereby + [V-ing]
3. Impersonal Evidence & Polite C1 Speculation It has been shown that + [Clause]  |  I would imagine that + [Clause]

Rewrite each draft sentence using the C1 prompt in purple, then reveal the Cambridge model to compare.

1. Draft: “Relatives in other countries are easy-reach on video apps.” EASIER TO REACH

“Relatives abroad are far easier to reach on video apps.” / “It is far easier to reach relatives abroad on video apps.”

2. Draft: “Studies prove that my generation is stupider at problem-solving because phones.” IT HAS BEEN SHOWN THAT

“It has been shown that my generation is less intelligent when it comes to problem-solving, largely due to constant phone use.”

3. Draft: “Strict rules could supress competition and stop new startups.” STIFLE + GOVERNMENTS INTERVENE

“When governments intervene too aggressively, overly strict rules can stifle competition and hold back emerging startups.” (Note spelling: suppress).

4. Draft: “Many people struggle to focus due to more tools are competing for their attention.” BECAUSE vs ON ACCOUNT OF

Clause: “Many people struggle to focus because more tools are competing for their attention.”
Noun/Gerund: “Many people struggle to focus on account of the growing number of tools competing for their attention.”

5. Draft: “Rival companies steal AI secrets, and this creates pressure to regulate. Probably, smaller firms struggle.” INDUSTRIAL ESPIONAGE + THEREBY + I WOULD IMAGINE

“Industrial espionage threatens proprietary research, thereby creating pressure on regulators; I would imagine that smaller labs find it far harder to protect their systems.”

🎯 Stage 4: CAE Part 4 Key Word Transformations

3–6 Words • Keyword Unchanged

Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence, using 3 to 6 words including the key word in capitals. Do not change the key word. Remember: contractions count as two words.

Item 1 • Tough-Movement REACH

Thanks to smartphones, it is much easier to contact relatives living abroad.
Relatives living abroad _________________________________________ thanks to smartphones.

are much easier to reach (5 words; or have become easier to reach)
4-Step Check: (1) Strike echoes relatives living abroad & thanks to smartphones. (2) Present simple is → are. (3) Subject shift from preparatory It to Relatives. (4) Collocation: easier to reach.
Item 2 • Passive Reporting SHOWN

Research proves that constant notifications weaken teenagers' problem-solving skills.
_________________________________________ constant notifications weaken teenagers' problem-solving skills.

It has been shown that (5 words)
4-Step Check: (1) Strike constant notifications weaken teenagers' problem-solving skills. (2) Present evidence → Present Perfect passive. (3) Active Research proves → passive preparatory It has been shown that.
Item 3 • Cause Linker ACCOUNT

Many students lose focus because so many apps compete for their attention.
Many students lose focus _________________________________________ so many apps competing for their attention.

on account of (3 words; or on account of there being, 5 words)
4-Step Check: (1) Strike echoes; notice competing is already a participle noun phrase, not a finite verb. (4) Replace because + clause with prepositional on account of.
Item 4 • C1 Verb Collocation STIFLE

Critics claim that strict government intervention could suppress competition in the tech sector.
Critics claim that strict government intervention could _________________________________________ the tech sector.

stifle competition in (3 words; or stifle competition within)
4-Step Check: (1) Strike echoes around could and the tech sector. (2) Bare infinitive after modal could. (4) Collocation: suppress competition = stifle competition.
Item 5 • Resultative Participle THEREBY

Social apps send constant alerts, and as a result they create unnecessary distractions.
Social apps send constant alerts, _________________________________________ distractions.

thereby creating unnecessary (3 words; or thereby causing unnecessary)
4-Step Check: (1) Strike Social apps send constant alerts & distractions. (4) Replace finite result clause and as a result they create with participle structure thereby + -ing.
Item 6 • Idiomatic Prepositional Phrase FINGERTIPS

Today we have instant access to global information whenever we need it.
Today we have global information _________________________________________ whenever we need it.

at our fingertips (3 words; or right at our fingertips, 4 words)
4-Step Check: (1) Strike Today we have, global information & whenever we need it. (4) Replace instant access to with at our fingertips.
