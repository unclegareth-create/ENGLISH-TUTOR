Latest Session Notes • 11 Nov 2026

From Today's Desk: Tech, Jensen Huang, Computer Science & Spoken C1 Precision

Spoken diagnostic notes from our live discussion on Computer Science Olympiads, biology, Jensen Huang, and computing architecture. Use Self-Test Mode to hide the explanations and test your active recall.

To be related to sth / sby Prepositional Valency
Target Pattern: be related TO something/someone
Live Context: “What’s related to your maths studies?” → “The Computer Science Olympiad is related to algorithmic problem solving.”
Note: Never drop to when linking two fields or topics.
knowledge of / about Collocation • Uncountable
Live Sentence: “I have a lot of knowledge of Biology, but…”
C1 Upgrades: have a sound working knowledge of, possess an in-depth knowledge of.
Note: Knowledge is strictly uncountable (never *a knowledge).
how computers work (Word Order) C1 Syntax • Indirect Trap
Trap: *“I’ve always wondered how do computers work?” (question inversion).
Rule: Indirect/embedded questions use statement order (Subject + Verb):
→ “I’ve always wondered how computers work.”
Key Check: No auxiliary do/does/did inversion in indirect clauses.
What blows my mind is… Wh- Cleft Focus
Blueprint: What + [Clause] + BE + [Noun / Clause]
Live Context: “What always blows my mind is…” / “What just blew my mind was [Jensen Huang’s keynote on GPU acceleration]…”
Power: Adds natural dramatic emphasis in Cambridge Part 3 and discussions.
waiting vs. waiting for Prepositional Verb
Intransitive: “They are waiting.” (no object mentioned).
Transitive with Preposition: “They’re waiting for [something/someone].”
“The industry is waiting for Nvidia’s next hardware release.” (or formal: awaiting the release).
information (never *informations) Uncountable Trap
Rule: Information is strictly uncountable in English.
Trap: Never write or say *informations or *an information.
Partitive: a piece of information, relevant items of information, or use data.
the media (never *medias) Mass Noun
Rule: The mass noun referring to broadcasting and news is the media (treated as singular mass or plural verb).
Trap: Never say *the medias in English.
“The media is/are focusing heavily on AI hardware demands.”
Eliminating -ehm- / -/ah/- / -/uh/- Spoken Delivery
Technique: Swap vocalised pauses for silent 1-second breathing pauses or C1 conversational anchors:
• “Looking at it from a computational perspective…”
• “Well, to put that into perspective…”
• “Essentially, the crux of the issue is…”
