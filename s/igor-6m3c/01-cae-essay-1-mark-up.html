Your Original Submission 283 words

Music has been in society since the beginning of humanity. It is one of the best ways of communication or describing emotions. The 20th and 21st centuries show the importance of music in our society by uniting social groups and educating young children. It is widely acknowledged that music is and always has been a key factor for human development.

The first role of music is uniting people. Not only can people unite because of a singer or a band, but they can also form friendships through shared interests. For example, when Taylor Swift was attacked on the internet, her fans united and defended a person they do not know personally. The second role is educating young children. However, uniting also has a bad side. Groups of fans can be aggressive towards fans of other bands. Sometimes it ends with destruction or vandalism, for example after concerts, when people damage cars or shops.

The best way of teaching children is by patterns like colours, shapes or music. It is the iconic alphabet song that shows it very well. Children knew the song before learning the alphabet, which helped them with their education. On the other hand, music can be a bad influence. Children might listen to songs which are not good for them, for example songs with bad words or about violence.

To sum up, although music can sometimes have a negative influence, it is strongly believed that its advantages outweigh its disadvantages. In my opinion, educating children is the most significant role of music because it helps them develop important skills from an early age. Music will always be a powerful way of bringing people together and making their lives more meaningful.

Polished Cambridge C1 Model 254 words (Ideal)

Music has played a central role in human culture throughout history, serving as one of the most effective means of communicating emotion. Recent history clearly demonstrates the societal value of music through its capacity to unite social groups and educate young children. Indeed, it is widely acknowledged that musical expression represents a key factor in human development.

First and foremost, music possesses an undeniable ability to bring people together. Not only can individuals unite around a shared passion for an artist, but they can also forge lasting friendships. For instance, when Taylor Swift faced intense online criticism, global fans mobilised to defend her. However, this collective passion also carries a darker dimension. Rival fan bases occasionally exhibit hostility towards one another, culminating in vandalism and public disorder following large-scale concerts.

Equally significant is the educational value of music in early childhood. Many educators maintain that young children learn most effectively through sensory patterns such as rhythm and melody. It is the iconic alphabet song that demonstrates this principle most clearly, as youngsters frequently learn the tune before mastering individual letters. Conversely, unmonitored exposure can exert a negative influence if minors listen to tracks containing explicit lyrics or themes glorifying antisocial behaviour.

In conclusion, although music can occasionally have adverse effects, its societal benefits substantially outweigh any drawbacks. In my view, educating children represents its most vital function, equipping them with foundational cognitive skills. Nevertheless, music will always remain an indispensable medium for uniting communities and enriching human experience.
