Part 6 · Diagnostic Drills

Part 4: Key Word Transformations

1. Strike Out Echoes Cancel matching words between Sentences 1 & 2 to isolate what's missing.
2. Tense Harmony Check Sentence 1 tense (past, perfect, modal) to lock in the target aspect.
3. Check Voice Shift Has the subject moved? Check for active-to-passive transformations.
4. Missing Collocation Slot the exact keyword into its fixed pattern (3 to 6 words).
Drill 1 · Unreal Past & Preference
I would prefer you not to discuss this matter with anyone else.
RATHER I __________________________ this matter with anyone else.
⏱️ Step 2 (Tense Check): Sentence 1 expresses preference about another person's action (would prefer you not to...) → when using would rather, this mandates the Past Subjunctive / Unreal Past verb form.
🔄 Step 3 (Voice Check): Voice remains Active → Active (the person being asked not to discuss remains the subject of the clause).
🧩 Step 4 (Collocation Blueprint): Formula: would rather + [Subject] + [Past Negative Verb]
[would rather you didn't discuss] (5 words · RATHER unchanged)
Drill 2 · Noun + Dependent Preposition
I'm sure that James didn't intend to offend anyone.
INTENTION I'm sure that James __________________________ offending anyone.
⏱️ Step 2 (Tense Check): Sentence 1 main clause is in the Past Simple (didn't intend) → the verb in the gap must harmonize with the past aspect.
🔄 Step 3 (Voice Check): Voice remains Active → Active (the subject performing the action does not change).
🧩 Step 4 (Collocation Blueprint): Formula: [Past verb] + no intention of + [-ing form]
[had no intention of] (4 words · INTENTION unchanged)
Drill 3 · Active to Passive Voice Shift
People believe that the mathematician solved the puzzle centuries ago.
SAID The mathematician __________________________ the puzzle centuries ago.
⏱️ Step 2 (Tense Check): Reporting is in the Present (believe), but the historical action happened in the past (centuries ago) → mandates a Present reporting verb + Perfect Infinitive (to have + past participle).
🔄 Step 3 (Voice Shift): CRUCIAL VOICE SHIFT! Active S1 (People believe [object]) → Passive S2 ([Subject] is reported...).
🧩 Step 4 (Collocation Blueprint): Formula: [Subject] + [BE present] + SAID + [Perfect Infinitive]
[is said to have solved] (5 words · SAID unchanged)
Part 7 · Writing Power

Escaping the "School Essay" Trap

✕ High School English (170-175)
  • • Starting with: "In my opinion, I think..."
  • • Overusing tired linkers: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In a nutshell"
  • • Simple sentences: "Technology is useful. It helps students learn faster."
✓ Cambridge C1 Academic Standard (193+)
  • • Impersonal reporting: "It is widely contended that..."
  • • Syntactic variety: Inversions and cleft sentences for emphasis
  • • Participle clauses: "..., thereby allowing students to progress rapidly."
Live Before & After
Standard High School Draft:

"Online courses are cheap and they also help people who live far away."

✓ Cambridge C1 Syntactic Architecture (Inversion + Precision):

"Not only are online courses significantly more affordable, but they also grant remote learners unprecedented access to quality education."

Formula: Not only + [BE] + [Subject]..., but [Subject] also + [Verb]...
Part 8 · High-Scoring Tools

Two Simple Tools That Lift Your Score

Tool 1: Negative Inversion (For Strong Emphasis)

Formula: Not only + auxiliary + subject + verb...

Example: "Not only does abstract mathematics train rigorous thinking, but it also underpins modern computer science."

Tool 2: The 'What' Cleft Sentence (Focus & Variety)

Formula: What makes X [adjective] is...

Example: "What makes this theorem particularly challenging is the sheer number of edge cases to consider."
