Problem 1: Gomory’s Theorem (Combinatorics & Graph Theory)

Upgrading the mutilated chessboard from a parity check to a universal constructive proof.

Interactive 8 × 8 Board
Take a standard 8 × 8 chessboard and remove any one black square and any one white square (anywhere on the board, not just corners).

Prove that the remaining 62 squares can always be tiled by 31 standard 2 × 1 dominoes with no overlaps and nothing hanging off the edge.
Follow-Up Stress Questions
  • “You cannot check all 32 × 32 = 1024 placements one by one. What single structure on the whole board makes the proof work for every pair at once?”
  • “How do you know both remaining segments of your loop have an even number of squares?”
  • “What if we remove two black and two white squares instead? Does a tiling still always exist?”
Checkpoint Hint (Small Nudge)
Ignore the two removed squares for a moment. Can you trace a single closed loop (a Hamiltonian cycle) that visits all 64 squares once, stepping only to orthogonally adjacent squares? Now ask what happens to that loop when you delete one black square and one white square.
Complete Proof & Plain-English Walkthrough
1. Build a Hamiltonian cycle: Draw a single closed path visiting all 64 squares once (for example, go down column 1, snake back and forth across columns 2 to 8, and return along row 1).

2. Colour alternation: Every step moves to an adjacent square, so the squares strictly alternate black and white around the loop (label them 1 to 64: odd indices are one colour, even indices are the other).

3. Cut the loop: Removing one black square (say index a, odd) and one white square (index b, even) breaks the cycle into two paths (or one path if they are neighbours). The number of squares strictly between them on one arc is |b − a| − 1. Since one index is odd and the other is even, |b − a| is odd, so |b − a| − 1 is even. The complementary arc has 62 − (even) = even squares.

4. Tile each path: Any path of even length is tiled immediately by pairing consecutive squares into dominoes.
Follow-Up: Why 2 Black + 2 White Fails
Counterexample: Remove the two white squares directly adjacent to the top-left black corner (a7 and b8), plus any two other black squares elsewhere on the board. The corner square a8 is now completely isolated with zero available neighbours, so no domino can cover it.

Problem 2: Primes of the Form 4k + 3 vs 4k + 1 (Number Theory)

Testing whether you understand why Euclid’s proof works, and where it breaks.

Proof & Failure Diagnosis
  1. Prove that there are infinitely many prime numbers of the form 4k + 3 (such as 3, 7, 11, 19, 23).
  2. Explain precisely why the exact same proof fails if you try to adapt it to prove there are infinitely many primes of the form 4k + 1.
  3. (Stretch) Modify the construction to prove that there are infinitely many primes of the form 4k + 1.
Follow-Up Stress Questions
  • “In Part 1, why did you construct 4(p1p2…pr) − 1 instead of + 3? What small bug happens with + 3 if 3 is in your list?”
  • “In Part 1, how do you know N has a prime factor of the form 4k + 3 rather than being prime itself?”
  • “In Part 3, which theorem forces every odd prime divisor of x2 + 1 to be congruent to 1 (mod 4)?”
Checkpoint Hint (Part 1 & Part 2)
Look at multiplication modulo 4. What happens when you multiply two numbers of the form 4a + 1 and 4b + 1? Now compare that with multiplying two numbers of the form 4a + 3 and 4b + 3.
Complete Proof: Part 1 (4k + 3) & Part 2 (Why 4k + 1 Fails)
Part 1: Suppose there are only finitely many primes of the form 4k + 3, listed as p1, p2, …, pr. Set N = 4(p1p2…pr) − 1. Then N > 1 is odd and N ≡ 3 (mod 4).
Every odd prime is congruent to either 1 or 3 (mod 4). Any product of integers congruent to 1 (mod 4) remains congruent to 1 (mod 4). Since N ≡ 3 (mod 4), N cannot be a product of only 4k + 1 primes, so it must have at least one prime factor q ≡ 3 (mod 4).
None of p1, …, pr divides N (each leaves remainder −1), so q is a new prime of the form 4k + 3. Contradiction.

Part 2 (Why 4k + 1 fails): If you form N = 4(p1…pr) + 1, you get N ≡ 1 (mod 4). However, an even number of 4k + 3 primes multiply to give 1 (mod 4) (for example, 3 × 7 = 21 ≡ 1 (mod 4)). So N could factor completely into 4k + 3 primes, giving no contradiction.
Stretch Proof: Part 3 (Infinitely Many 4k + 1 Primes)
Assume finitely many primes of the form 4k + 1: p1, …, pr. Set N = (2p1p2…pr)2 + 1 = x2 + 1.
Let q be any prime factor of N. Since N is odd, q is odd, and x2 ≡ −1 (mod q).
By Fermat’s Little Theorem, xq−1 ≡ 1 (mod q). Rewriting this as (x2)(q−1)/2 ≡ (−1)(q−1)/2 ≡ 1 (mod q) forces (q − 1)/2 to be even, so q ≡ 1 (mod 4).
None of p1, …, pr divides N (remainder 1), so q is a new prime of the form 4k + 1. Contradiction.
