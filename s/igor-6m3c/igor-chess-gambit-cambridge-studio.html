Checkpoint Hint (Small Nudge)
For 5 × 5: Count how many light vs dark squares exist on a 25-square board.
For 4 × 4: Separate the 16 squares into 4 Corners, 4 Centres, and 8 Edges. Check which squares the Corners can reach, and notice the 8 Edges split into two disconnected 4-cycles!
Complete Proofs (5 × 5 & 4 × 4)
5 × 5 Closed Tour (Impossible): Every Knight move switches square colour (Light ↔ Dark). A closed loop must visit equal numbers of light and dark squares, so total squares would have to be even. A 5 × 5 board has 13 light and 12 dark squares, making a closed tour impossible (though an open tour starting and ending on light squares exists!).

4 × 4 Open Tour (Impossible): The 4 Corners {(0,0),(3,3)} and {(0,3),(3,0)} can ONLY jump to the 4 Centre squares. Visiting each pair without locking up a 4-cycle forces both path endpoints into the Corners, leaving at most 1 exit to the 8 Edge squares. That forces all 8 Edge squares to be crossed in one unbroken chain, which is impossible because Knights on the 8 Edge squares move only inside two separate, disconnected 4-cycles!

Bonus Chess-Maths Interview Round: 32 Non-Attacking Knights

Talk through both parts cleanly: first construct a valid 32-Knight layout, then prove 33 is impossible.

5m Classical · Pigeonhole Proof
What is the maximum number of Knights you can place on a standard 8 × 8 chessboard so that no two Knights attack each other? Prove your upper bound cleanly.
  • "Why is placing 32 Knights on one colour easy to verify, yet NOT a proof that 33 is impossible?"
  • "How can you tile the 8 × 8 board into small rectangles so each rectangle breaks into Knight-attack pairs?"
Checkpoint Hint (Small Nudge)
Try slicing the 8 × 8 chessboard into eight 4 × 2 rectangles. Inside a single 4 × 2 block (8 squares), can you pair up all 8 squares into 4 pairs that attack each other?
Complete Proof (32 Knights Upper Bound)
Answer: 32 Knights.
Lower bound (32 works): Place a Knight on every dark square (all 32). Since every Knight jumps only to the opposite colour, no two Knights on dark squares can ever attack each other.
Upper bound (33 is impossible): Divide the 8 × 8 board into eight 4 × 2 blocks. Inside any 4 × 2 block, the 8 squares split cleanly into 4 Knight-attack pairs (shown on the right). With 8 × 4 = 32 disjoint pairs covering all 64 squares, at most one Knight can sit in each pair, capping the total at 32.
One 4 × 2 block = 4 disjoint Knight pairs (A, B, C, D). Eight blocks = 32 pairs.

UCAS Defence · 2m Each

1. Torricelli’s Problem (60° Rotation) & Fagnano’s Problem (Reflection)

In plain spoken English, explain how rotating a sub-triangle by 60° turns the sum of three distances into a single broken path bounded below by a straight line, and what condition on the angles can break the interior-point construction.

Plain-English Checkpoints to Hit Aloud
Rotate triangle APC by 60° around A to form an equilateral triangle APP', turning PA + PB + PC into BP + PP' + P'C'. The shortest path between fixed endpoints B and C' is a straight segment (achieved when all angles around P are 120°). Edge check: if one vertex angle is ≥ 120°, the Fermat point sits on that vertex!

2. Goldbach’s Conjecture vs Chen’s Theorem

You named both in your UCAS statement. State Chen’s theorem accurately in one clear English sentence and explain why closing that final step to Goldbach remains open.

Plain-English Checkpoints to Hit Aloud
Goldbach: Every even integer greater than 2 is the sum of two primes.
Chen’s theorem (1966/1973): Every sufficiently large even integer can be written as the sum of a prime and a semiprime (a number that is either prime or a product of two primes). The sieve methods used cannot cleanly separate primes from two-prime products (the parity barrier).
