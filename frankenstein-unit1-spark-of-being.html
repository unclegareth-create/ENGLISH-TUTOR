Formal Conditionals Without "If"

1. Read the Scene & Useful Vocabulary

Core • 10 Mins Frankenstein • Chapter 5

Read Mary Shelley's famous opening to Chapter 5, where Victor Frankenstein brings his creature to life—and immediately regrets what he has done:

Original Novel Extract (1818)
"It was on a dreary night of November that I beheld the accomplishment of my toils. With an anxiety that almost amounted to agony, I collected the instruments of life around me, that I might infuse a spark of being into the lifeless thing that lay at my feet. It was already one in the morning; the rain pattered dismally against the panes, and my candle was nearly burnt out, when, by the glimmer of the half-extinguished light, I saw the dull yellow eye of the creature open; it breathed hard, and a convulsive motion agitated its limbs. How can I describe my emotions at this catastrophe, or how delineate the wretch whom with such infinite pains and care I had endeavoured to form?... The beauty of the dream vanished, and breathless horror and disgust filled my heart." — Mary Shelley, Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818), Chapter 5 Original Text
Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1818 Fiction
19th-century Gothic words that you will see in classic novels, but shouldn't use in modern essays.
Daemon / Fiend [noun] Romantic-era words for a monster or evil spirit.
Charnel-house [noun] A historical vault where human bones were stored.
Toils [plural noun, literary] Exhausting physical or mental labour ("the accomplishment of my toils"). In modern formal English, write efforts, research, or hard work.
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
High-value vocabulary from this chapter that works naturally in modern C1/C2 essays, reports, and reviews.
Catastrophe [noun] / Catastrophic [adj] A sudden event that causes great damage or suffering. Collocations: an environmental catastrophe, catastrophic consequences.
Unquenchable [adjective] Impossible to satisfy. Collocations: an unquenchable thirst for knowledge, unquenchable curiosity.
Delineate [verb] To describe or outline something clearly and precisely. Collocations: clearly delineate the boundaries, delineate the main responsibilities.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Inverted & Mixed Conditionals

Core • 8 Mins C1/C2 Hypotheticals

In formal essays and reports, you can make conditional sentences sound much more polished by dropping if and moving Had, Were, or Should to the start of the sentence:

1. Past Conditional Inversion (Had...)

Instead of If + subject + had V3, start with Had + subject + V3:

• Had Victor listened to his professors, the tragedy would have been avoided.
(= If Victor had listened...)

2. Future/Present Inversion (Were... to)

Instead of If + subject + verb, start with Were + subject + to-infinitive:

• Were governments to ignore these warnings, the costs would be huge.
(= If governments ignored / were to ignore...)

3. Mixed Conditional (Past → Present)

Connects a past decision to a present result today:

• Had he not abandoned the creature that night, he would not now be living in fear.

4. Watch Out: Never Contract "Not"!

In inverted conditionals, never write Hadn't or Weren't. Put not after the subject:

• ✗ Hadn't Victor fled the room...
• ✓ Had Victor not fled the room...

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Stretch • 10 Mins Real-World C1/C2 Application

Inverted conditionals (Had..., Were... to...) and words like catastrophic and delineate are ideal when writing an IELTS or Cambridge C1/C2 essay on science, AI, or climate change:

Adapted from Shelley's Novel (1818)
"Driven by an unquenchable thirst for glory, Victor rushed ahead without thinking. Had he considered the consequences of abandoning his creature, the catastrophe that destroyed his family could have been avoided."
In a Modern C1/C2 Essay on Science & AI Ethics
"While humanity's unquenchable curiosity drives medical and technological progress, innovation without oversight carries real risks. Were tech companies to deploy powerful AI systems without clear safety rules, the social consequences could be catastrophic. Regulators must therefore delineate strict ethical boundaries early on."
