Verbatim Primary Source (1818)
"It was on a dreary night of November that I beheld the accomplishment of my toils. With an anxiety that almost amounted to agony, I collected the instruments of life around me, that I might infuse a spark of being into the lifeless thing that lay at my feet. It was already one in the morning; the rain pattered dismally against the panes, and my candle was nearly burnt out, when, by the glimmer of the half-extinguished light, I saw the dull yellow eye of the creature open; it breathed hard, and a convulsive motion agitated its limbs... How can I describe my emotions at this catastrophe, or how delineate the wretch whom with such infinite pains and care I had endeavoured to form? His limbs were in proportion, and I had selected his features as beautiful. Beautiful! Great God!... The beauty of the dream vanished, and breathless horror and disgust filled my heart." — Mary Shelley, Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818 First Edition), Chapter 5 Verbatim 1818 Text

2. Syntactic Architecture: Inverted & Mixed Conditionals

C1/C2 Formal Hypotheticals

In advanced academic and literary English, standard conditional conjunctions (if) are omitted and replaced by auxiliary inversion to heighten formality, dramatic irony, or moral gravity:

1. Past Inverted Counterfactual

Replaces If + past perfect with Had + subject + past participle:

• Had Victor heeded the warnings of his professors, the calamity would have been averted.
(= If Victor had heeded...)

2. Present / Future Inversion

Replaces hypothetical if with Were + subject + to-infinitive:

• Were he to reanimate the tissue, severe ethical repercussions would ensue.
(= If he were to reanimate...)

3. Mixed Conditional Framework

Connects a past counterfactual cause to an ongoing present consequence:

• Had he not succumbed to hubris, he would not now be plagued by guilt.
(Past cause $\rightarrow$ Present state)

4. The Negative Inversion Trap

In negative conditional inversions, not cannot contract with the auxiliary:

• ✗ Hadn't Victor fled the chamber...
• ✓ Had Victor not fled the chamber, he might have pacified the creature.
