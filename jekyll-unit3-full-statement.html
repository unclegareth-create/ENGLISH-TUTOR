What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Cleft Emphasis & C1/C2 Collocations

1. It was only when ... that ... Highlights the exact turning point → "It was only when the salt ran out that Jekyll panicked."
2. extricate oneself from [a situation] Means "free yourself from a difficult trap" → "He could not extricate himself from the mess."
3. have / exert a pernicious influence on Means "have a subtle, gradually harmful effect on" → "a pernicious influence on young minds"

1. Read the Scene & Useful Vocabulary

Jekyll & Hyde • Chapter 10

Read Henry Jekyll's final confession, where he explains why he created his second identity—not because he wanted to be evil, but because he wanted to keep his respectable public reputation while hiding his private faults:

Original Novel Extract (1886)
"The worst of my faults was a certain impatient gaiety of disposition, such as has made the happiness of many, but such as I found it hard to reconcile with my imperious desire to carry my head high, and wear a more than commonly grave countenance before the public. Hence it came about that I concealed my pleasures; and that when I reached years of reflection, and began to look round me and take stock of my progress and position in the world, I stood already committed to a profound duplicity of life... It was the curse of mankind that these incongruous faggots were thus bound together—that in the agonised womb of consciousness, these polar twins should be continuously struggling." — R.L. Stevenson, Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (1886), Chapter 10: Henry Jekyll's Full Statement of the Case Original Text
Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1880s Terms
Archaic or historical words from Chapter 10 that you should recognize in context, but never use in modern essays.
Cheval-glass [noun, historical] A tall full-length Victorian mirror mounted so it can tilt on a frame.
Faggot [noun, archaic literal sense] In Stevenson's phrase "these incongruous faggots", an old word for a bundle of sticks tied together. Never use this word in modern English; write elements, strands, or sides.
Draught [noun, archaic medical sense] A single dose of liquid medicine or potion (pronounced "draft").
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
High-scoring verbs, adjectives, and nouns for C1/C2 essays and reviews.
Extricate (oneself from) [verb] To free yourself or someone else from a difficult trap or situation. Collocations: extricate oneself from a difficult situation, impossible to extricate.
Pernicious [adjective] Having a harmful effect, especially in a gradual, subtle way that isn't noticed at first. Collocations: a pernicious influence, the pernicious effects of misinformation.
Imperious [adjective] Arrogant, commanding, and expecting to be obeyed. Collocations: an imperious tone, an imperious gesture.
Depravity [noun] Moral corruption or wickedness. Collocations: sink into depravity, human depravity.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Time Clefts & Verb Valency

C1/C2 Precision

Compare the two ways of emphasizing "not until / only when", and notice the exact prepositions required by C1/C2 verbs:

1. It was only when / not until ... that ...

When you put It was at the start, use that + normal word order (do NOT invert):

• Inverted (Lesson 8): Not until the salt ran out did Jekyll panic.
• Cleft (Lesson 9): It was not until the salt ran out that Jekyll panicked.

2. What brought about / precipitated ... was ...

Great way to highlight the root cause of a problem in an essay:

• What brought about Jekyll's downfall was his refusal to accept his own flaws.

3. extricate + object + from

Extricate always needs an object (himself, the company, the country) + from:

• ✓ Jekyll could not extricate himself from the trap he had made.
• ✗ Jekyll could not extricate out of the trap.

4. have / exert a pernicious influence on

Use pernicious when describing a slow, subtle, harmful influence:

• Constant online comparison can have a pernicious influence on teenagers' self-esteem.

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Real-World C1/C2 Application

See how Stevenson's exploration of double lives and anonymity transfers naturally into a modern IELTS or Cambridge C1/C2 essay on online anonymity and digital behaviour:

In Jekyll's Confession (1886)
"'Give me a mask, and I could plunge into liberty without a tremor of shame!' Yet it was only when he could no longer control the change that Jekyll realised he could not extricate himself from his second self."
In a Modern C1/C2 Essay on Online Anonymity
"Just like Jekyll's disguise, online anonymity can have a pernicious influence on public discussion. People who maintain a polite facade in everyday life sometimes post remarkably callous comments when hidden behind an anonymous username. Once false rumours spread online, victims often find it almost impossible to extricate themselves from the damage to their reputation."

Sample C1/C2 Model Answer (Option A: Online Anonymity Essay)

Natural C1/C2 Example
Much as the internet has connected people across the globe, the ability to hide behind an anonymous screen name has had a pernicious influence on public debate. More than a century ago, R.L. Stevenson showed how a respectable doctor could commit callous acts the moment he stepped behind the mask of Edward Hyde, and the same psychological pattern is visible online today.

What makes anonymous harassment so damaging is the gap between people's polite everyday facade and the hostility they express on social media. Not until platforms require stronger accountability—and schools teach young users how hard it is to extricate oneself from a damaged digital reputation—will online spaces become genuinely respectful.
