Forensic Agency Shift

The Split-Persona Register Lens: Philosophical Evasion vs. Unvarnished Confession

🪞 Jekyll's Cleft Evasion ("It was Hyde who...") ⚖️ Direct Moral Agency ("I alone...")
Jekyll's Dissociative Nominalisation (Shifting Blame via Syntax)

"It was Hyde, and Hyde alone, that bore the guilt of Carew's destruction; what transpired beneath the gaslamps was the autonomous manifestation of a depravity from which my waking conscience stood wholly detached."

Syntactic Strategy: Subject-focusing It-clefts and abstract agentless nouns (destruction, manifestation) partition the self into innocent observer and guilty agent.
Forensic Moral Accountability (C2 Legal Indictment)

"By compounding the draught with full cognisance of its pernicious effects, Dr Jekyll voluntarily relinquished moral restraint and must therefore be held criminally culpable for every atrocity committed under his alias."

Syntactic Strategy: Instrumental gerund phrase (By compounding...) + explicit human subject + high-register legal predication (held criminally culpable).
Museum Words (Passive Decoding) Receptive Only
Archaic or domestic 19th-century terms from Chapter 10. Understand them in context, but avoid them in modern essays.
Cheval-glass [noun, historical] A tall Victorian dressing mirror suspended between two upright pillars so it can swivel.
Faggot [noun, archaic literal sense] In Stevenson's phrase "these incongruous faggots", an archaic literal term for a bundle of sticks bound together. Never use in modern prose; use components, strands, or elements.
Polity [noun — Victorian Psychological Sense] Used metaphorically by Jekyll for the internal "community" of the mind; in modern C2 English, restrict polity strictly to political science (an organized society/state).
Arsenal Words (Active C1/C2 Lexis) High-Yield Production
Elite philosophical and evaluative vocabulary prized in Cambridge C2 discursive essays, reports, and reviews.
Depravity [noun] Moral corruption; wickedness. Collocates: sink into utter depravity, moral depravity.
Abominable [adjective] Causing moral revulsion; detestable. Collocates: abominable conditions, an abominable breach of ethics.
Imperious [adjective] Assuming power or authority without justification; arrogant and domineering, or urgently commanding. Collocates: an imperious demand, imperious tone.
Extricate [verb] To free someone or oneself from a constraint, difficulty, or entanglement. Collocates: extricate oneself from a predicament, impossible to extricate.
Pernicious [adjective] Having a harmful effect, especially in a gradual or subtle way. Collocates: a pernicious influence, pernicious consequences.

2. Syntactic Architecture: Cleft Sentences & Philosophical Nominalisation

C2 Information Packaging

Cleft sentences divide a single message into two clauses to spotlight a specific agent, motive, or cause—an essential tool in C2 persuasive and analytical writing:

1. The 'It'-Cleft of Agency

It + be + [Focused Element] + that/who + [Relative Clause] isolates responsibility or cause:

• It was an imperious ambition that drove Jekyll to synthesize the tincture.

2. The 'Wh'-Cleft (Pseudo-Cleft)

What + [Clause] + be + [Focused Noun/Infinitive] builds analytical suspense toward the predicate:

• What sealed Jekyll's fate was the impurity of the original salt supply.

3. 'All (that)' Exclusive Cleft

Emphasizes the sole requirement or remaining option with dramatic economy:

• All that remained was for Utterson to break down the cabinet door.

4. Philosophical Nominalisation

Transforms psychological processes into abstract head nouns modified by prepositional phrases:

• The gradual erosion of moral restraint culminated in total subjugation.

This posthumous forensic dossier evaluates the psychological trajectory and legal culpability of Dr Henry Jekyll following the recovery of his signed deposition in the Soho cabinet. Driven by an imperious ambition to reconcile public probity with clandestine indulgence, the subject engineered a volatile pharmacological compound designed to partition human consciousness. What initially appeared to be a cerebral triumph of experimental chemistry rapidly degenerated into systematic duplicity.

Little did Dr Jekyll suspect that each administration of the draught would progressively erode his autonomous willpower. Scarcely had the persona of Edward Hyde been unleashed upon the thoroughfares of London when he exhibited a callous disregard for human life, culminating in the abominable murder of Sir Danvers Carew. Behind the unimpeachable facade of a philanthropic physician lurked a predatory alter ego whose actions inspired acute repugnance among all witnesses.

Having succumbed to the pernicious ascendancy of his baser impulses, Jekyll ultimately found it impossible to extricate himself from the depravity he had voluntarily courted. It is the unequivocal conclusion of this inquiry that moral accountability cannot be dissolved by chemical artifice.
