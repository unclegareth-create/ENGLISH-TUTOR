What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Spotlighting Key Points with Cleft Sentences

1. It-Cleft (It was... that...)Highlights a specific cause or person → "It was human cruelty that turned him bitter."
2. What-Cleft (What... was...)Builds focus toward the end of the sentence → "What he wanted most was a single friend."
3. All-Cleft (All... was...)Means "The only thing" → "All the creature asked for was a chance to speak."

Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1818 Fiction
19th-century words that appear often in Frankenstein, but sound unnatural in modern essays.
Wretch [noun] An unfortunate, miserable outcast. In modern essays, write outcast or marginalised individual.
Succour [noun / verb, literary] Help given in times of hardship. Today, write support, aid, or assistance.
Hovel [noun] A tiny, squalid shed or shelter (where the Creature hides beside the De Lacey cottage).
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
High-value vocabulary for C1/C2 essays on society, psychology, and ethics.
Benevolent [adj] / Benevolence [noun] Well-meaning, kind, and generous. Collocations: a benevolent gesture, motivated by benevolence.
Ostracise [verb] / Ostracism [noun] To exclude someone from a group or society. Collocations: ostracised by their peers, social ostracism.
Repudiate [verb] To firmly reject or refuse to accept something. Collocations: repudiate responsibility, repudiate an accusation.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

C1/C2 Focus Structures

A cleft sentence splits one simple sentence into two parts so you can put a spotlight on the exact cause, person, or idea you want the reader to notice:

1. The "It"-Cleft (It is/was ... that/who)

Puts a spotlight on the exact cause, person, or time:

• Plain: Constant rejection turned the creature bitter.
• Cleft: It was constant rejection that turned the creature bitter.

2. The "What"-Cleft (What ... is/was)

Builds anticipation and places your key point at the end after is/was:

• Plain: He wanted companionship above all else.
• Cleft: What he wanted above all else was companionship.

3. The "All"-Cleft (All ... is/was)

Emphasizes that only one thing was needed or wanted:

• All the creature asked for was to be treated with basic kindness.
(= The only thing he asked for was...)

4. Useful Causal Phrasal Verbs

In Part 4 transformations, Cambridge often tests stem from and put [something] down to:

• His anger stemmed from (= originated in) deep loneliness.
• Critics put his actions down to (= attribute them to) isolation.

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Real-World C1/C2 Application

In the Novel
"'I was benevolent and good; misery made me a fiend. It was the cruelty of mankind that turned me away from virtue. All I sought was a companion who would not despise me.'"
In a Modern C1/C2 Essay on Youth & Society

Sample C1/C2 Model Answer (Option A: Science & Ethics)

Natural C1/C2 Example
Much as society benefits from rapid scientific and technological breakthroughs, history shows that innovation without accountability carries serious risks. Humanity's unquenchable drive to push boundaries has transformed medicine and communications, yet it can also cause unintended harm when safety is treated as an afterthought.

What Mary Shelley's Frankenstein illustrates so memorably is that creators cannot simply repudiate responsibility once an invention enters the world. Were technology companies to release powerful AI or biotechnology tools without testing their social impact, the consequences could be catastrophic. It is clear, practical regulation—designed to delineate ethical boundaries before products are launched—that protects the public while still allowing genuine science to flourish.
