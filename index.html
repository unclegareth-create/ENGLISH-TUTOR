Inside My 1-to-1 Writing Feedback

See How We Turn B2 Drafts into C1/C2 & Band 8.0+ Writing

It’s hard to improve when feedback just fixes a few typos and says "Good job!" In our sessions, we go through your writing line by line so you see the exact grammar structures, collocations, and register upgrades that lift your mark.

Prompt

"Discuss two ways governments can encourage people to use public transport instead of private cars, and evaluate which is more effective."

Communicative Achievement: 3/5 → 5/5
Organisation: 3/5 → 5/5
Language: 3/5 → 5/5
Gareth's Line-by-Line Markup CEFR B2 → C1/C2
Click any numbered highlight [1–4] to inspect why

In many modern cities, traffic congestion is a growing problem. Cars cause a lot of pollution, and they also waste people's time every day.Not only do private vehicles exacerbate urban air pollution, but they also erode daily productivity.1 Therefore, governments must take decisive action. First, if the government makes train and bus tickets cheaper,First and foremost, heavily subsidising rail and bus fares would allow authorities to2 encourage low-income commuters to leave their cars at home. However, cheap tickets are not enough if the buses are always late.Nevertheless, affordability alone is futile if the existing network remains unreliable.3 Because of this, I think spending money on faster trains is the best choice.Consequently, channelling investment into high-frequency infrastructure is by far the more viable long-term strategy.4

B2 / Everyday Phrasing C1/C2 Upgrade 1 Examiner Criterion Note
GM
Gareth's Feedback Summary 15+ Yrs Teaching Experience

Your argument is clear and logical, which comfortably passes at B2. To reach Grade A (200+ on the Cambridge scale), we need to replace everyday verbs like 'make tickets cheaper' with natural C1 noun phrases ('subsidising fares') and show confident control of complex structures like negative inversion.

Line-by-Line Notes

Why each upgrade lifts your mark

Inspecting Note #1 of 4
1 Negative Inversion
Language (Grammar)
✗ "Cars cause a lot of pollution, and they also waste people's time every day."
✓ "Not only do private vehicles exacerbate urban air pollution, but they also erode daily productivity."

Why it scores higher: Cambridge examiners reward ambitious C1/C2 grammar used naturally. Opening with 'Not only do...' demonstrates advanced structural control, while upgrading 'cause a lot of' to 'exacerbate' lifts the Language mark.

2 Nominalisation & Collocation
Language (Lexis)
✗ "First, if the government makes train and bus tickets cheaper,"
✓ "First and foremost, heavily subsidising rail and bus fares would allow authorities to"

Why it scores higher: Replacing the conversational conditional ('if the government makes... cheaper') with a gerund phrase and natural C1 collocation ('heavily subsidising... fares') establishes an authoritative essay register.

3 Abstract Nouns & Cohesion
Organisation
✗ "However, cheap tickets are not enough if the buses are always late."
✓ "Nevertheless, affordability alone is futile if the existing network remains unreliable."

Why it scores higher: Rather than repeating concrete nouns ('tickets', 'buses'), referencing back with abstract nouns ('affordability alone', 'existing network') and 'Nevertheless' boosts both Organisation and Communicative Achievement.

4 Evaluative Conclusion
Content & Register
✗ "Because of this, I think spending money on faster trains is the best choice."
✓ "Consequently, channelling investment into high-frequency infrastructure is by far the more viable long-term strategy."

Why it scores higher: Part 1 Essays require you to evaluate which option is more effective. Replacing 'I think... is the best choice' with 'by far the more viable long-term strategy' holds the academic tone right to the final full stop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about lesson delivery, time zones, levels, and booking.

What platform do we use, and which time zones do you teach?
All private 1-to-1 sessions take place online via Zoom or Google Meet with HD audio and live screen sharing. Based in Northern Ireland (UK time), I teach weekday and weekend slots that suit students across Europe, East Asia, the Middle East, and the Americas.
How does a typical lesson run?
Each 50-minute session is relaxed, interactive, and focused on getting you speaking. Depending on your goals, we might work on natural conversation and vocabulary, unpack specific Cambridge or IELTS tasks, or rehearse upcoming presentations. We finish with clear feedback, pronunciation tips, and a shared Google Doc of notes.
Which English levels do you accept?
I specialise in intermediate-to-advanced learners (CEFR B1+ up to C2 Proficiency): exam candidates targeting Cambridge or IELTS, professionals using English at work, and lifelong or heritage learners who want to speak more naturally.

Who I'm not right for: I do not accept complete beginners (A1/A2).
Do you provide lesson materials and writing feedback?
Yes, all lesson materials—custom slides, official Cambridge/IELTS practice papers, interactive presentations, and writing prompts—are provided free of charge. Every student gets a shared Google Doc for class notes, and students on 5- or 10-lesson packages also receive between-class markups on essays or emails.
What is your cancellation and package refund policy?
Life happens! Any lesson can be rescheduled or cancelled with at least 12 hours' advance notice at no cost. Multi-lesson packages are valid for 6 months from purchase, and if your circumstances change, any unused sessions in a pack can be refunded pro-rata.
How does payment work?
When booking your £20 trial lesson on the calendar below, payment is handled securely at checkout via Stripe. For ongoing single lessons or 5/10-lesson packs, you can pay securely via Stripe, PayPal, or Wise.
