Museum Words (Passive Decoding) Receptive Only
Historical Victorian terminology. Recognize their literary function, but do not produce them in modern essays.
Countenance [noun] A person's face or facial expression. In modern formal writing, use expression, demeanor, or bearing.
Perforce [adverb] Necessarily; by force of circumstance. Modern C1/C2 equivalents: inevitably, out of necessity, unavoidably.
Fortnight [noun — Register Note] A period of two weeks. Standard in British/Commonwealth English, but replaced by two weeks in American business contexts and fourteen days in formal international agreements.

2. Syntactic Architecture: Negative & Restrictive Inversion

C1/C2 Emphatic Syntax

When a negative or restrictive adverbial is fronted to the beginning of a clause for dramatic emphasis or formal gravitas, the subject and auxiliary verb must invert:

1. Frequency & Extent Inversion

Introduced by Seldom, Rarely, Scarcely, Little:

• Seldom had Harker encountered such chilling courtesy.
(= Harker had seldom encountered...)

2. Conditional Restriction Inversion

Introduced by Under no circumstances, On no account:

• Under no circumstances should you wander into unlit chambers.
(= You should not wander under any circumstances...)

3. Temporal Correlative Inversion

Hardly / Scarcely... when and No sooner... than:

• Hardly had the heavy door closed when the latch clicked irrevocably.

4. The Double Operator Trap

Inversion requires an auxiliary operator (did, had, should). Do NOT invert the main lexical verb:

• ✗ Rarely understood Harker the Count's motives.
• ✓ Rarely did Harker understand the Count's motives.
