1. Read the Scene & Useful Vocabulary

Dracula • Chapters 2–3

At first, Count Dracula appears to be a polite, welcoming host—until he warns Harker never to fall asleep outside his own room, and Harker discovers every exit is bolted:

Original Novel Extract (1897)
"'Welcome to my house! Enter freely and of your own will!' He made no motion of stepping to meet me, but stood like a statue, as though his gesture of welcome had fixed him into stone... Later, the Count warned me gravely: 'Let me advise you, my dear young friend—nay, let me conjure you—never to sleep in any part of the castle except where your own rooms are. This castle is old, and has many memories, and there are bad dreams for those who sleep unwisely.' When I was left alone, I systematically examined every door in my reach. Doors, doors, doors everywhere, and all locked and bolted. In no place save from the windows in the castle walls is there an available exit. The castle is a veritable prison, and I am a prisoner!" — Bram Stoker, Dracula (1897), Chapter 2: Jonathan Harker's Journal Original Text
Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1890s Fiction
Archaic words used by Count Dracula and Harker. Good to recognize when reading, but avoid them in modern essays.
Nay [adverb] Old-fashioned word for "no" or "or rather" ("let me advise you—nay, beg you"). In modern English, write or rather or in fact.
Perforce [adverb] By force of circumstance; necessarily. Today, write inevitably or out of necessity.
Countenance [noun] A person's face or facial expression. In modern English, use expression, face, or demeanour.
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
High-value C1/C2 words for describing atmosphere, difficulty, and anxiety in essays and reviews.
Sinister [adjective] Giving the impression that something harmful or bad is happening. Collocations: sinister undertones, a sinister development.
Formidable [adjective] Inspiring respect or fear because it is very large, powerful, or difficult. Collocations: a formidable challenge, a formidable opponent.
Trepidation [noun] A feeling of fear or anxiety about something that is going to happen. Collocations: view the changes with trepidation, filled with trepidation.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Negative Inversion

Core • C1/C2 Emphasis (3 Marks)

When you start a sentence with a negative or limiting word (like Never, Seldom, Little, Hardly, Under no circumstances) for dramatic emphasis, the auxiliary verb comes before the subject—just like in a question:

1. Little / Seldom / Rarely

Put did / had / have before the subject:

• Normal: Harker rarely saw the Count by day.
• Inverted: Rarely did Harker see the Count by day.
• Inverted: Little did he realise that he was a prisoner.

2. Under no circumstances / On no account

Used for strong rules, warnings, or advice:

• Under no circumstances should you fall asleep in another wing of the castle.

3. Hardly / Scarcely ... when

Shows that one event happened immediately after another:

• Hardly had the heavy door closed when the key turned in the lock.
(Tip: Hardly/Scarcely pair with when; No sooner pairs with than.)

4. Don't Invert Without an Auxiliary!

In Past Simple or Present Simple, you must add did / do / does:

• ✗ Little knew Harker what lay ahead.
• ✓ Little did Harker know what lay ahead.

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Core • Real-World C1/C2 Application (3 Marks)

Negative inversion isn't just for dramatic stories—examiners reward one well-placed inversion in a Cambridge C1/C2 or IELTS essay, article, or review to emphasize a key turning point:

Adapted from Stoker's Chapters 2–3 (Inversion Focus)
"Little did Harker suspect what lay behind the Count's polite welcome. Scarcely had the heavy oak door closed when he realised he was facing a formidable obstacle."
In a Modern C1/C2 Essay on Technology & Privacy
"Little did early internet users suspect that their personal data would become a multi-billion-dollar commodity. Under no circumstances should convenience take priority over basic digital privacy, yet many consumers view new AI tools with a mix of excitement and trepidation."
