What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Opening Sentences with Participles

1. Present Participle (-ing)Shows two actions happening at once or a clear reason → "Fearing for his safety, Harker looked out..."
2. Past Participle (-ed / V3)Describes a passive state concisely → "Hemmed in by dark pines, the road narrowed..."
3. Perfect Participle (Having + V3)Shows which action happened first → "Having crossed the bridge, we entered the pass."

Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1890s Fiction
Words you will meet in 19th-century stories, but shouldn't use in a modern essay or workplace email.
Calèche [noun] A light, low-wheeled horse-drawn carriage with a folding hood.
Beetling / Frowning crags [literary phrase] Overhanging, threatening cliff faces. Great in a Gothic novel, too dramatic for an essay.
Manifold [adjective] Many and various. Today, it sounds much more natural to write numerous, wide-ranging, or varied.
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
Natural, high-level vocabulary from this chapter that works brilliantly in modern C1/C2 essays, reviews, and articles.
Apprehensive [adjective] Anxious or fearful that something bad will happen. Natural collocations: feel apprehensive about the future, apprehensive passengers.
Impenetrable [adjective] Impossible to pass through, see through, or understand. Natural collocations: impenetrable fog, impenetrable legal jargon.
Hemmed in (by) [participle / phrasal verb] Surrounded closely so you cannot move freely. Works for places or feelings: hemmed in by high-rise buildings, feeling hemmed in by strict rules.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Participle Clauses

C1/C2 Sentence Variety

Instead of joining every sentence with "and", "because", or "after", advanced writers often start a sentence with a participle clause. Here are the four rules you need to know:

1. Present Participle (-ing)

Use an -ing opener to show the reason for an action or two things happening at the same time:

• Sensing the passengers' fear, the driver urged the horses on.
(= Because he sensed their fear...)

2. Past Participle (-ed / V3)

Use a past participle opener when the subject receives the action (a shortened passive clause):

• Hemmed in by dark pine trees, the road felt like a tunnel.
(= Because it was hemmed in by dark pine trees...)

3. Perfect Participle (Having + V3)

Use Having + past participle to make it clear that the first action finished before the second began:

• Having crossed the bridge, we entered the mountain pass.
(= After we had crossed the bridge...)

4. Watch Out: The Same-Subject Rule!

The subject right after the comma must be the person or thing doing (or receiving) the participle action:

• ✗ Crossing the bridge, the castle appeared ahead. (Castles can't cross bridges!)
• ✓ Crossing the bridge, we saw the castle ahead.
