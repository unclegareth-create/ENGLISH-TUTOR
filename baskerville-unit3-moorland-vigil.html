Museum Words (Passive Decoding) Receptive Only
19th-century clothing, breeding, and colloquial idioms. Recognize them in detective fiction, but replace them in formal C1/C2 writing.
Ulster [noun] A long, loose, heavy Victorian overcoat made of rough woollen cloth, often with a cape or half-belt.
Dewlap [noun] The loose fold of skin hanging from the neck or throat of certain dogs (such as bloodhounds) or cattle.
Throwback [noun — Victorian Hereditary Usage] An individual reverting to an ancestral physical and moral type (atavism), as when Holmes spots Hugo Baskerville's eyes in the portrait.
Arsenal Words (Active C1/C2 Lexis) High-Yield Production
Elite forensic, legal, and analytical vocabulary for Cambridge C1/C2 reports, critical reviews, and discursive essays.
Corroborate [verb] To confirm or give support to a statement, theory, or finding. Collocates: independently corroborate, empirical corroboration.
Fallacy [noun] A mistaken belief, especially one based on unsound arguments; a flaw in reasoning. Collocates: expose a logical fallacy, the popular fallacy that...
Premeditated [adjective] Thought out or planned beforehand (especially of a crime or strategic action). Collocates: a coldly premeditated deception, premeditated fraud.
Audacious [adjective] Showing a willingness to take surprisingly bold risks; impudently daring. Collocates: an audacious scheme, an audacious hypothesis.

2. Syntactic Architecture: Counterfactual Deductions & Forensic Passives

C2 Retrospective & Evidential Syntax

In retrospective case summaries and official inquiries, C2 writers pair inverted past counterfactuals (Had + Subject + Past Participle) with impersonal forensic passives and emphatic clefts. Examine Sherlock Holmes's final case summary telegram to Scotland Yard:

⚡ CASE CLOSED — RETROSPECTIVE SUMMARY #221B-FINAL
FILING INVESTIGATOR: S. HOLMES • COUNTERSIGNED: INSP. LESTRADE (SCOTLAND YARD)
DOSSIER SEALED
"THE CASE OF THE BASKERVILLE SUCCESSION IS RESOLVED STOP REVIEW THE THREE PILLARS OF THE CONSPIRACY BELOW STOP"
I. The Bloodline Motive Jack Stapleton—in reality Rodger Baskerville, nephew of Sir Charles—was discovered to have embezzled public funds in Costa Rica before establishing himself on Dartmoor under an alias.
II. The Phosphorus Mechanism Purchased from Ross & Mangles in Fulham Road, a cross-bred bloodhound-mastiff was coated with an odourless phosphorus paste so as not to impair its olfactory tracking of Sir Henry's stolen boot.
III. The Counterfactual Margin Had Laura Lyons not been coerced into silence, and had Mrs. Stapleton not been bound inside Merripit House, the baronet's life would never have been imperilled.

1. Inverted Past Counterfactuals

Omit if and invert Had + Subject + Past Participle (putting not AFTER the subject):

• Had Holmes not scrutinized the family portrait, the bloodline motive would have remained obscure.

2. 'But for' & 'Were it not for'

Compact counterfactual prepositional idioms that replace full conditional clauses:

• But for Lestrade's timely reinforcement, the ambush might have miscarried.
• Had it not been for the advancing fog, they would have waited longer.

3. Subject-Raised Forensic Passives

Combine passive reporting verbs (is presumed, was proved, is alleged) with perfect infinitives:

• The perpetrator is presumed to have perished in the depths of Grimpen Mire.

4. Emphatic 'What'-Clefts & 'It'-Clefts

Isolate the decisive forensic breakthrough for analytical emphasis:

• What ultimately corroborated Holmes's hypothesis was the discovery of Sir Henry's black boot in the mire.

This joint statutory inquest was convened by Scotland Yard and 221B Baker Street to establish the circumstances surrounding the death of Sir Charles Baskerville and the attempted assassination of his successor, Sir Henry. Ostensibly attributed by rural superstition to a supernatural curse, the fatalities were in reality the product of a coldly premeditated conspiracy engineered by Jack Stapleton—an unacknowledged heir to the baronetcy.

Beyond the granite tors of Dartmoor lay the treacherous quagmire of Grimpen Mire, within whose fastness the perpetrator concealed a gigantic cross-bred hound. Having examined the double heap of cigar ash beside the Yew Alley gate, investigators deduced that Sir Charles must have been awaiting a clandestine appointment when the phosphorus-coated beast was unleashed, precipitating fatal cardiac syncope. Had Holmes not scrutinized the seventeenth-century portrait in the dining hall, the genealogical motive behind this audacious plot might conceivably have remained obscure.

What ultimately exposed the supernatural fallacy was the forensic corroboration of topical phosphorus residue upon the destroyed animal's jaws, alongside the recovery of Sir Henry's stolen boot inside the mire. The perpetrator is presumed to have perished in the quagmire during his flight, bringing an end to a malevolent fraud that exploited ancestral trepidation to avert legal detection.
Criterion 1 (Content): Comprehensively addresses the statutory inquest mandate: accounts for Sir Charles's demise, exposes Stapleton's hereditary motive and the phosphorus hound mechanism, and records the perpetrator's presumed death in Grimpen Mire.
