What You'll Learn in This Lesson

Compact Conditionals & Evidence Verbs

1. Had it not been for / But for + Noun Means "If it hadn't been for..." → "But for Holmes's quick reaction, Sir Henry would have died."
2. corroborate [a claim / theory / finding] Means "confirm with solid evidence" → "The boot found in the mire corroborated Holmes's theory."
3. expose the fallacy (of / that...) Means "prove that a popular belief is false" → "The phosphorus paint exposed the fallacy of a curse."

Original Novel Extract (1901)
"Every minute that white woolly plain which covered one half of the moor was drifting closer and closer to the house. If it reached the path before Sir Henry came out, our ambush would be useless. Suddenly, out of the fog bounded an enormous coal-black creature, such as mortal eyes have never looked upon. Fire burst from its open mouth, its eyes glowed with a smouldering glare, and its muzzle and hackles were outlined in flickering flame...

After five shots brought the beast down, I placed my hand upon its glowing muzzle and held up my own fingers—gleaming in the darkness. 'Phosphorus,' I said.
'A cunning preparation of it,' said Holmes, sniffing at the dead animal. 'There is no smell which might have interfered with his power of scent. Stapleton bought the strongest and most savage hound in London, painted it with phosphorus, and used the old family legend to frighten Sir Charles to death.'" — Sir Arthur Conan Doyle, The Hound of the Baskervilles (1901), Chapters 14–15 Original Text

2. The Grammar Pattern: "But For" & "Had It Not Been For"

C1/C2 Compact Conditionals

In Cambridge Part 4 and formal essays, you can replace a long "If X hadn't happened..." clause with a compact noun phrase using Had it not been for or But for:

1. Had it not been for + Noun Phrase

Means "If it had not been for / Without" in the past:

• Had it not been for the seventeenth-century portrait, Holmes might never have guessed Stapleton's motive.

2. But for + Noun Phrase

An even shorter way to say "If it hadn't been for":

• But for the sudden wall of fog, they would have seen the hound much earlier.

3. Negative Past Conditional: Had + S + not + V3

Remember from Lesson 4: put not after the subject, never contracted:

• Had Holmes not examined the portrait closely, the family likeness would have gone unnoticed.

4. Expose / Disprove the fallacy that...

A great C1/C2 phrase for challenging a popular myth in an essay:

• Recent studies have exposed the fallacy that longer working hours always lead to higher output.

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Real-World C1/C2 Application

See how Had it not been for..., corroborate, and expose the fallacy that... work naturally in a modern IELTS or Cambridge C1/C2 essay on critical thinking, science, and work:

In Holmes's Solution (1901)
"Had Holmes not looked closely at the family portrait, Stapleton's motive might have remained hidden. Finding the phosphorus paint corroborated Holmes's deduction and exposed the fallacy of a supernatural curse."
In a Modern C1/C2 Essay on Workplace Productivity
"Recent workplace trials have exposed the fallacy that working sixty hours a week leads to better results. In fact, independent studies across several industries corroborate the view that well-rested teams make fewer costly mistakes. Had companies not tested flexible schedules during the past decade, this evidence might never have come to light."

Sample C1/C2 Model Answer (Option B: Mystery Review)

Natural C1/C2 Example
Nowhere is Arthur Conan Doyle's skill as a storyteller clearer than in The Hound of the Baskervilles. Set on the bleak, desolate hills of Dartmoor, where the treacherous bogs of Grimpen Mire hide an escaped convict and a family secret, the novel blends Gothic suspense with sharp detective work.

What makes the mystery so satisfying is the way Sherlock Holmes refuses to accept superstition on the basis of circumstantial rumours. While the locals are convinced that a supernatural curse must have killed Sir Charles, Holmes looks for a plausible human motive. Had he not noticed the family resemblance in an old portrait, the killer's audacious plot might never have been uncovered. By the final chapter, every clue is corroborated, exposing the fallacy of the legend in a truly memorable climax.
