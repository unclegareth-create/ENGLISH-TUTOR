Gothic Syntax Quick Diagnostic (5 Live C1/C2 Part 4 Challenges)

Test your command of the five core C1/C2 grammar structures across the course. Complete the second sentence in 3 to 6 words using the key word in bold without changing its form. Use ✂️ Strike Out the Echoes to isolate the exact words you need to transform.

Challenge 1 • Negative Inversion (Bram Stoker's Dracula) Formula: Negative Adverbial + Aux + Subject + Bare Infinitive
Harker did not suspect at all that every door inside Castle Dracula was locked against him.
KEY WORD: LITTLE
0 / 3–6 words
Sentence 2: ________________________ that every door inside Castle Dracula was locked against him.
Cambridge Model Answer (4 words): Little did | Harker suspect (1 mark for Little did + 1 mark for Harker suspect)
  1. Step 1 (Strike Out the Echoes): Cross out that every door inside Castle Dracula was locked against him. Deficit left: Harker did not suspect at all.
  2. Step 2 (Inspect Tense Harmony): Sentence 1 is Past Simple (did not suspect) → use auxiliary did + bare infinitive suspect.
  3. Step 3 (Check Inversion Shift): Fronting negative adverbial Little forces subject-auxiliary inversion (Little did Harker...).
  4. Step 4 (Solve the Pattern): Little did Harker suspect (4 words, unchanged keyword LITTLE).
Challenge 2 • Perfect Passive Participle Clause (Dracula: Log of the Demeter) Formula: Having been + Past Participle + Preposition, [Main Clause]
Because he had been warned about the coastal reef, the captain ordered the crew to lash the helm.
KEY WORD: WARNED
0 / 3–6 words
Sentence 2: ________________________ the coastal reef, the captain ordered the crew to lash the helm.
Cambridge Model Answer (4 words): Having been | warned about / of (1 mark for Having been + 1 mark for warned about / of)
  1. Step 1 (Strike Out the Echoes): Cross out the coastal reef, the captain ordered the crew to lash the helm. Deficit left: Because he had been warned about.
  2. Step 2 (Inspect Tense Harmony): Prior past action (Past Perfect had been warned) before the main clause → Perfect Participle (Having...).
  3. Step 3 (Check Voice Shift): The captain received the warning (passive) → Having been warned.
  4. Step 4 (Solve the Pattern): Having been warned about (4 words, unchanged keyword WARNED).
Challenge 3 • Emphatic Wh- Cleft Sentence (Dr Jekyll & Mr Hyde) Formula: What + Verb + Object + BE + Focused Noun Phrase
The witness was shocked most by Hyde's callous disregard for the child.
KEY WORD: WHAT
0 / 3–6 words
Sentence 2: ________________________ most was Hyde's callous disregard for the child.
Cambridge Model Answer (4 words): What shocked | the witness (1 mark for What shocked + 1 mark for the witness)
  1. Step 1 (Strike Out the Echoes): Cross out most ... Hyde's callous disregard for the child. Deficit left: The witness was shocked.
  2. Step 2 (Inspect Tense Harmony): Past Simple passive was shocked converts into Past Simple active inside the What-clause (shocked).
  3. Step 3 (Check Voice Shift): Passive-to-active cleft shift puts the witness as the object of shocked.
  4. Step 4 (Solve the Pattern): What shocked the witness (4 words, unchanged keyword WHAT).
Challenge 4 • Past Modal Deduction (The Hound of the Baskervilles) Formula: must + have + been + -ing (Continuous Past Deduction)
Holmes was certain that someone had been waiting outside the moorland wicket-gate before midnight.
KEY WORD: MUST
0 / 3–6 words
Sentence 2: Someone ________________________ outside the moorland wicket-gate before midnight.
Cambridge Model Answer (4 words): must have | been waiting (1 mark for must have + 1 mark for been waiting)
  1. Step 1 (Strike Out the Echoes): Cross out someone ... outside the moorland wicket-gate before midnight. Deficit left: Holmes was certain that ... had been waiting.
  2. Step 2 (Inspect Tense Harmony): Past Perfect Continuous (had been waiting) maps to Perfect Continuous Infinitive (have been waiting).
  3. Step 3 (Check Evidential Shift): Logical certainty in the past (was certain that) maps to must have.
  4. Step 4 (Solve the Pattern): must have been waiting (4 words, unchanged keyword MUST).
Challenge 5 • Inverted Third Conditional (Mary Shelley's Frankenstein) Formula: Had + Subject + (not) + Past Participle, [Main Clause]
If Victor had not abandoned his creature on that dreary November night, the tragedy might never have occurred.
KEY WORD: HAD
0 / 3–6 words
Sentence 2: ________________________ his creature on that dreary November night, the tragedy might never have occurred.
Cambridge Model Answer (4 words): Had Victor | not abandoned (1 mark for Had Victor + 1 mark for not abandoned)
  1. Step 1 (Strike Out the Echoes): Cross out his creature on that dreary November night, the tragedy might never have occurred. Deficit left: If Victor had not abandoned.
  2. Step 2 (Inspect Tense Harmony): Past Counterfactual (If + Past Perfect) inverts by omitting if and fronting Had.
  3. Step 3 (Check Negation Placement): In formal inverted conditionals, not follows the subject (Had Victor not abandoned, never Hadn't Victor).
  4. Step 4 (Solve the Pattern): Had Victor not abandoned (4 words, unchanged keyword HAD).
