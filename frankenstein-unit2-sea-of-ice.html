1. The Literary Text & Forensic Corpus Filter

Frankenstein • Ch. 10 (The Montanvert Encounter)

High on the Mer de Glace, Victor encounters the towering form of his creation. Observe Shelley's use of elevated concessive rhetoric and legalistic volition as creator and created negotiate moral duty:

Verbatim Primary Source (1818)
"I suddenly beheld the figure of a man, at some distance, advancing towards me with superhuman speed. As he drew nearer, its unearthly ugliness rendered it almost too horrible for human eyes. 'Devil,' I exclaimed, 'do you dare approach me? And do not you fear the fierce vengeance of my arm wreaked on your miserable head?' 'Much as I expected this reception,' said the daemon, 'I will not be driven away. I demand that you hear me before you vent your hatred upon my devoted head. Have I not suffered enough, that you seek to increase my misery? Life, although it may only be an accumulation of anguish, is dear to me, and I will defend it. Remember, thou hast made me more powerful than thyself; but I will not tempt thee to a fight. I am thy creature, and I will be even mild and docile to my natural lord and king, if thou wilt also perform thy part, the which thou owest me...'" — Mary Shelley, Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818 First Edition), Chapter 10 Verbatim 1818 Text
Museum Words (Passive Decoding) Romantic Relics
Archaic literary words. Understand their dramatic function, but do not produce them in modern Cambridge essays.
Offal [noun] Waste parts of butchered animals; carrion. Used figuratively for degraded food. Modern formal: refuse, scraps.
Clemency [noun] Mercy or leniency shown by an authority. High-register historical/judicial term.
Spurn [verb] Originally to kick away with the foot; reject with contempt. Modern formal equivalent: dismiss, reject out of hand.
Arsenal Words (Active C1/C2 Lexis) High-Yield Production
Sophisticated lexis of conflict, ethics, and discourse highly rewarded in Cambridge C1/C2 examinations.
Abhorrence [noun] A feeling of repulsive disgust and loathing. Collocates: inspire universal abhorrence, view with deep abhorrence.
Inexorable [adjective] Impossible to stop or prevent; unyielding. Collocates: inexorable march of technology, inexorable logic.
Insolent [adjective] Showing a rude and arrogant lack of respect. Collocates: insolent demeanor, insolent disregard for authority.

2. Syntactic Architecture: Concessives & Mandative Subjunctives

C1/C2 Register & Volition

Concessive clauses allow a writer to grant a counter-point before delivering a devastating refutation. Mandative subjunctives enforce formal institutional authority:

1. Fronted Concessives (Much as / Adj + as)

Inverts degree adverbs or adjectives with as / though to intensify contrast:

• Much as Victor despised the creature, he could not deny its eloquence.
(= Although Victor despised him very much...)

2. Mandative Subjunctive (Bare Infinitive)

Follows verbs of demand, request, or resolution without third-person -s or auxiliaries:

• The creature insisted that Victor listen to his complete history.
(NOT: *listened or *listens)

3. The Double Comparative (The... The...)

Expresses parallel proportionality with optional subject-verb inversion for dramatic effect:

• The further he ascended into the glaciers, the more palpable became his dread.

4. The Albeit Adverbial Rule

Albeit governs an adjective, adverb, or participle phrase, NEVER an independent finite clause:

• ✗ Albeit he was exhausted, he climbed.
• ✓ He continued his ascent, albeit exhausted by the altitude.
