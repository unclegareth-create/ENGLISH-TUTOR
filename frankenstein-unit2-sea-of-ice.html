1. Read the Scene & Useful Vocabulary

Core • 10 Mins Frankenstein • Chapter 10

High on the Mer de Glace glacier in the Alps, Victor comes face to face with the Creature he abandoned. Expecting a mindless monster, Victor is astonished by the Creature's articulate, persuasive speech:

Original Novel Extract (1818)
"I suddenly beheld the figure of a man, at some distance, advancing towards me with superhuman speed... 'Devil,' I exclaimed, 'do you dare approach me? Begone, vile insect! or rather, stay, that I may trample you to dust!'

'I expected this reception,' said the daemon. 'All men hate the wretched; how, then, must I be hated, who am miserable beyond all living things! Yet you, my creator, detest and spurn me, thy creature, to whom thou art bound by ties only dissoluble by the annihilation of one of us. You purpose to kill me. How dare you sport thus with life? Do your duty towards me, and I will do mine towards you and the rest of mankind. Have I not suffered enough, that you seek to increase my misery? Life, although it may only be an accumulation of anguish, is dear to me, and I will defend it.'" — Mary Shelley, Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus (1818), Chapter 10 Original Text
Old-Fashioned Words (Just for Reading) 1818 Fiction
Archaic pronouns and dramatic commands used in Chapter 10. Recognize them in classic literature, but never use them in modern writing.
Thou / Thee / Thy / Wilt [archaic pronouns & verb] Old English singular "you / your / will", which Shelley uses to give the Creature's speech a biblical, Miltonic weight.
Begone! [archaic exclamation] "Go away at once!" Strictly theatrical or historical.
Spurn [verb, literary] To reject with disdain or contempt. In modern essays, reject, dismiss, or turn down sounds much more natural.
Modern C1/C2 Words (Great for Exams) Use in Writing & Speaking
Strong C1/C2 words for essays on ethics, law, technology, and society.
Inexorable [adjective] / Inexorably [adverb] Impossible to stop or prevent. Great collocations: the inexorable rise of automation, inexorable pressure.
Abhorrence [noun] / Abhor [verb] Strong disgust or moral hatred. Collocations: view violence with abhorrence, abhor cruelty.
Albeit [conjunction] Meaning "although / even if" before a short phrase (not a full verb clause): progress has been made, albeit slowly.

2. The Grammar Pattern: Advanced Contrast & The Subjunctive

Core • 8 Mins C1/C2 Discursive Writing

In a C1/C2 or IELTS essay, you constantly need to acknowledge an opposing point before giving your own argument, or make formal recommendations. Here are four high-scoring ways to do it:

1. Adjective + though / as + S + V

Put the adjective at the very start before though or as to emphasize a contrast:

• Impressive though the technology is, it cannot replace human judgment.
(= Although the technology is impressive...)

2. Much as + Subject + Verb

Means "Even though [someone] wants/likes/hates something very much":

• Much as Victor despised the creature, he felt compelled to listen.
• Much as consumers welcome lower prices, quality often suffers.

3. The Subjunctive (that + base verb)

After insist, demand, recommend, suggest, or it is vital that, use the base verb (be, listen, pay) even with he/she/it:

• The creature demanded that Victor hear his side of the story.
• The report recommends that the law be updated immediately.

4. How to Use "Albeit"

Albeit means "although", but it can only be followed by an adjective, adverb, or short phrase—never a full subject + verb:

• ✗ Albeit he was exhausted, he kept climbing.
• ✓ He kept climbing, albeit slowly / albeit with difficulty.

4. From Classic Fiction to Modern Exam Writing

Stretch • 10 Mins Real-World C1/C2 Application

See how Much as..., Adjective + though..., and albeit let you balance two sides of an argument naturally in a Cambridge C1/C2 or IELTS Discussion Essay:

Adapted from Shelley's Novel (1818)
"Much as Victor abhorred the creature's appearance, he could not deny the justice of its argument. Fierce though his anger was, he agreed to listen to the creature's story, albeit reluctantly."
In a Modern C1/C2 Essay on Automation & Jobs
"Much as businesses welcome the efficiency of automated systems, the impact on entry-level workers cannot be ignored. Inexorable though the rise of AI may seem, economists insist that governments support retraining programmes—albeit as a gradual transition rather than an overnight fix."

Sample C1/C2 Model Paragraph (Automation & Retraining)

Natural C1/C2 Example
Much as corporations welcome the efficiency of automated logistics, the social cost to displaced workers cannot be ignored. Inexorable though the expansion of artificial intelligence may appear, labour economists insist that every government establish practical retraining grants before entire sectors are restructured.
