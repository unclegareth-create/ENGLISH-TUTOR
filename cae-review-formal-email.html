Review & Formal Email Register Control

In my nineteen years teaching Cambridge candidates, including fifteen at Belfast Metropolitan College, I have watched strong C1 writers drop straight from Band 5 to Band 3 on Communicative Achievement for one avoidable reason: they write a magazine Review like a dry committee report, or let chatty text-message slang bleed into a Formal Letter. Here is how to hold both voices cleanly across 220 to 260 words.

Desk A · Critic's Column (Review)

Vivid, engaging, reader-aware. Compound modifiers, subtle humour, balanced praise and caveat.

Desk B · Formal Letterhead (Email / Letter)

Measured, diplomatic, executive. Clear opening purpose, polite hedging, zero contractions or slang.

Band 5/5 · Critic's Column 238 words · Verified 220-260w
Task Brief: An English-language arts magazine has invited readers to review a newly restored cultural venue in their city, describing its strengths, mentioning anything that could be improved, and explaining who would most enjoy visiting (220 to 260 words).

A Second Act Worth Booking: The Lyric Picture House

Few landmarks in Belfast carry a century of local cinema history as effortlessly as the newly reopened Lyric Picture House. After four years behind scaffolding, this restored 1924 art-deco theatre has returned not as a fragile heritage exhibit, but as a vibrant neighbourhood screen with genuine warmth.

Step past the brass swing doors and the craftsmanship immediately steals the show. Restored terrazzo floors lead into a single, richly upholstered auditorium where razor-sharp digital projection meets an eclectic programme of European drama, classic retrospectives, and weekend family matinees. What truly sets the venue apart, however, is its unhurried atmosphere: rather than herding audiences past glowing concession counters, knowledgeable staff greet regular visitors by name and linger to recommend upcoming screenings.

That said, a handful of opening-month wrinkles remain. Viewers seated in the upper circle may find quieter dialogue noticeably muffled, an acoustic flaw that occasionally dulls subtler performances. Equally, the charming but cramped foyer bar struggles to cope during a fifteen-minute interval, leaving slower queues empty-handed when the warning bell rings.

Minor teething troubles aside, the Lyric Picture House is an unmistakable triumph for the city's cultural life. If you are planning an evening there, book central stalls seats for the clearest sound and arrive twenty minutes early for a relaxed pre-film coffee. Anyone weary of soulless multiplexes will find plenty here to justify a permanent place in their diary.

Examiner Annotations (Band 5/5)

1. Engaging Magazine Title

"A Second Act Worth Booking" uses a light cinema pun to signal a lively print review immediately, never a generic heading like "Cinema Review".

2. Vivid Lexis & C1 Wh-Cleft

Natural compound modifiers ("razor-sharp", "richly upholstered", "unhurried") sit beside a clean C1 Wh-cleft ("What truly sets the venue apart, however, is...").

3. Nuanced Caveat

"That said, a handful of opening-month wrinkles remain" pivots smoothly into concrete, fair criticism ("noticeably muffled") without sounding bitter.

4. Audience-Aware Verdict

Ends with an insider seat tip and tells the exact right reader ("Anyone weary of soulless multiplexes") why the venue earns a spot in their diary.

Band 5/5 · Formal Letterhead 235 words · Verified 220-260w
Task Brief: You recently attended an evening screening at the newly reopened Lyric Picture House. Although you admired the restoration, you encountered two practical problems in the upper balcony. Write a formal letter to the Venue Director praising the refurbishment, explaining your concerns, and proposing sensible improvements (220 to 260 words).

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to share my impressions following an evening screening at the Lyric Picture House on 14 October, and to draw your attention to two practical shortcomings in the upper balcony that affected the comfort and safety of patrons.

First and foremost, the architectural restoration is wholly commendable. The preserved plasterwork, thoughtful lighting in the main foyer, and courteous welcome from your box-office staff create a rare sense of occasion that reflects immense credit on everyone involved in the four-year project.

While the refurbishment itself is deeply impressive, two operational issues detracted significantly from the evening. Specifically, the sound distribution in the upper circle appears unevenly balanced, rendering softer dialogue difficult to follow from the rear rows. In addition, the steep balcony steps lack low-level strip lighting once the house lights dim, which caused several older patrons to hesitate and nearly stumble when returning after the interval.

Accordingly, I would urge the management committee to arrange a thorough acoustic inspection of the upper tier before the winter programme begins. Furthermore, fitting discreet LED step markers along the balcony aisles would resolve the visibility hazard promptly and at modest expense.

Thank you for taking the time to consider these observations. I remain confident that the Lyric Picture House will become a flagship venue for Belfast, and I look forward to hearing how these matters might be addressed.

Yours faithfully,
Hannah Gallagher

Examiner Annotations (Band 5/5)

1. Immediate Opening Purpose

"I am writing to share my impressions... and to draw your attention to..." states the date, purpose, and diplomatic scope inside the opening sentence.

2. Diplomatic Hedging & Participle Clause

"While the refurbishment itself is deeply impressive..." cushions the complaint, and "rendering softer dialogue difficult to follow" elevates grammatical range cleanly.

3. Actionable, Cost-Aware Proposal

"I would urge..." paired with a gerund subject ("fitting discreet LED step markers... would resolve...") sounds practical and executive rather than demanding.

4. Exact Salutation & Sign-Off Match

Because the prompt gave a title (Venue Director) with no personal name, opening with "Dear Sir or Madam," pairs strictly with "Yours faithfully,".

Cambridge Sign-Off Golden Rule
  • Dear Sir or Madam, (name unknown) → Yours faithfully,
  • Dear Ms Reid / Dear Mr Hughes, (name known) → Yours sincerely,
