Interactive Lesson: B2 First Exam Review (“Things That Matter”)

Preparing for the Cambridge B2 First exam? Warm up your voice with the 2-minute Speaking Part 2 prompt below, then work through my 5-slide interactive lecture deck and 6-item Part 4 Key Word Transformation Clinic.

Step 1 • Active Voice First

Speaking Warm-Up: Something You Refuse to Get Rid Of

2-Min Long Turn

Describe a possession, hobby, or routine that has mattered to you for a long time.

You should say:

  • how long you have had or done it (use for and since)
  • how you used to feel about it when you first started
  • why you have never got rid of it or given it up
  • and explain how your life would be different without it.
Speaking Timer 02:00

Gareth’s 3 Talk-Structuring Rules

1. The 1-Minute Note Secret: Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin (one per prompt bullet); never write full sentences.

2. The 3-Act Story Arc: Past Context (30s) → Sensory Snapshot / Core Scene (45s) → Reflection / ‘So What?’ (45s).

3. The Cambridge Anchor: Bullet points are springboards, not an interrogation checklist. If finished early, deepen the reflection and comparison until halted.

Slide 1 • Use of English Part 1 & Speaking Part 1

Adjectives + Dependent Prepositions

In B2 First, -ed adjectives describe how a person feels (“I’m bored with this programme”), while -ing adjectives describe the thing or situation causing the feeling (“This programme is boring”). Examiners love testing which preposition follows each adjective.

Core Formula
Subject + BE / get + -ed Adjective + Preposition + Noun / -ing
Speaking Warm-Up Prompt

If I gave you £1,000 tonight to spend on something you are passionate about, how would you spend it?

Live Check: Click the correct preposition for each B2 sentence

1. “What are you most excited _______ learning this year?”

2. “Many candidates get frustrated _______ losing easy marks on prepositions.”

3. “The interviewers were deeply impressed _______ her clear examples.”

Slide 2 • B2 Tense Harmony

Present Perfect vs Past Simple (& For vs Since)

Use the Past Simple when the time period is finished (in 2010, last month, six months ago). Use the Present Perfect when an action started in the past and continues now, or happened at an indefinite time connected to the present.

FOR + Period of Time

for a week, for six months, for ages, for ten years

SINCE + Point in Time / Past Simple Clause

since last week, since 25th September, since I was fourteen

Live Check: Choose the verb form that fits the time marker

1. “I _______ mad about surfing since the first time I tried it at fourteen.”

2. “Before moving to the coast six months ago, Liam _______ a six-hour drive from the nearest beach.”

Slide 3 • Narrative Grammar Trap

Past Habits: Used to vs Would

Both used to + infinitive and would + infinitive describe repeated actions in the past that no longer happen. The exam trap: for past states (verbs like be, live, have, know, belong), you can ONLY use used to—never would.

Structural Blueprint

• Repeated Past Action: Every summer, we used to swim / would swim in the bay. (Both work)

• Past State: I used to have a surfboard. (I would have a surfboard is wrong)

Live Check: Which form is grammatically valid here?

1. “When I was at primary school, I _______ be terrified of speaking in front of the class.”

2. “Did you _______ play team sports when you were a teenager?”

Slide 4 • Use of English Parts 1, 2 & 4

6 Essential B2 First Phrasal Verbs

Cambridge B2 First tests everyday phrasal verbs across Reading and Use of English. Review these six core items from Unit 2 and test them below:

get rid of
throw away / eliminate something unwanted
look up to
admire and respect someone
turn up
arrive (often unexpectedly or late)
take off
suddenly become successful
make it
manage to attend or succeed
get away
escape or go on a short break

1. “We invited thirty people to the party, but over fifty _______!”

2. “Her photography business really _______ after she won the national prize.”

Slide 5 • Reading & Use of English Part 4

Live Part 4 Key Word Transformation Workout

Complete the second sentence so that it has a similar meaning to the first sentence, using the word given. Do not change the word given. You must use between two and five words, including the word given.

Item 1 • Present Perfect + SINCE KEY WORD: SINCE

1. The last time I went surfing was six months ago.

I six months ago.

Item 2 • Negative Past Habit + USED KEY WORD: USED

2. When Liam was younger, he lived a long way from the beach, though he lives by the sea now.

Liam a long way from the beach.

Item 3 • Three-Part Phrasal Verb + RID KEY WORD: RID

3. You should throw away those old textbooks now that you have passed the exam.

You should those old textbooks now that you have passed the exam.

Why interactive practice works for B2 First

The B2 First exam tests not just your passive knowledge of grammar rules, but your ability to spot prepositions, tense shifts, and phrasal verbs under timed conditions. Working through active drills builds the pattern recognition you need for Reading and Use of English Parts 1 to 4.

Key areas to focus on before exam day:

  • Use of English Part 4 (Key Word Transformations): Watch out for three-part phrasal verbs (get rid of, look up to), Present Perfect for/since shifts, and dependent prepositions.
  • Speaking Part 2 (Long Turn): Compare the two photographs directly rather than describing each one in isolation, and speculate naturally about how the people feel.
  • Writing Parts 1 & 2: Keep a clear distinction between formal essay register and engaging article or review phrasing.
