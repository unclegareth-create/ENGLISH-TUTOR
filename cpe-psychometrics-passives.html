"Once the preserve of military recruitment, psychometric testing is now routinely deployed by corporate HR departments eager to have applicants' cognitive agility and emotional resilience quantified before a single interview takes place. Proponents maintain that standardised testing strips away unconscious interviewer bias, allowing introverted yet highly capable candidates to be evaluated on an equal footing. Furthermore, longitudinal data suggests that staff turnover can be significantly reduced when personality profiles are matched to company culture.

Yet sceptics remain unconvinced. Not only can seasoned applicants get themselves coached to manipulate multiple-choice personality inventories, but rigid algorithmic filtering also risks screening out neurodivergent or unconventional thinkers whose creativity resists neat categorisation. Worse still, when complex human temperament is reduced to an arbitrary percentile, hiring managers may be lulled into a false sense of scientific certainty."

2. Four Pillars of C2 Passive & Causative Grammar

Grammar Reference

1. Uses of HAVE and GET

Both form causatives (have/get something done), but differ in active agency and register:

• Have sb do sth (authority): The board had the consultant sign an NDA.
• Get sb to do sth (persuasion): She got her referee to submit the form early.

2. Passives with "HAVE" (Experiencer)

Can describe either a planned service OR an unwanted event that happens to the subject:

• He had his application rejected without explanation. (Unwanted experience — he didn't arrange it!)

3. The Passive with "GET"

More dynamic and emphatic than be; used for unplanned actions or overcoming difficulty (avoid in very formal essays):

• Over 500 candidates got shortlisted.
• My CV got lost in the spam folder.

4. The Passive with "BY" Phrases

Omit by... when the agent is obvious or unknown. Keep "by" when the agent carries new, heavy, or surprising information:

• Applicants were screened by an untrained AI algorithm. (Crucial end-focus)

3. Passive Reporting & Subject Elevation Blueprint Matrix

C1/C2 Structural Formulas

When Cambridge moves the object of a that-clause to the front of Sentence 2, you must shift from an impersonal It is + V3 + that frame into a Subject Elevation + Infinitive structure. Match the infinitive aspect to the exact timeframe of Sentence 1:

Structure Type Formation Blueprint Cambridge Part 4 Example
1. Preparatory 'It' Reporting
Impersonal Academic Distance		 It + BE + (widely) + contended / believed / thought / alleged + that + Clause It is widely contended that aptitude tests reduce staff turnover.
2. Subject Elevation + Simple Infinitive
General / Present State or Habit		 Subject + BE + believed / thought / said / known + to + Base Verb Unconscious bias is believed to distort traditional job interviews.
3. Subject Elevation + Continuous Infinitive
Action in Progress Now		 Subject + BE + thought / rumoured / said + to be + V-ing The firm is thought to be developing an automated screening tool.
4. Subject Elevation + Perfect Infinitive
Prior Past Active Action		 Subject + BE + alleged / said / believed + to have + Past Participle (V3) The HR director is alleged to have falsified the metrics.
5. Subject Elevation + Perfect Passive Infinitive
Prior Past Passive Action		 Subject + BE + rumoured / thought / said + to have been + Past Participle (V3) The test results are rumoured to have been manipulated.
6. Passive of 'Make' & Experiential 'Have/Get'
Coercion & Causative Shifts		 BE + made + to + Infinitive // have/get + Object + V3 // get + Object + to + Inf Marcus objects to being made to sit three-hour aptitude tests.
