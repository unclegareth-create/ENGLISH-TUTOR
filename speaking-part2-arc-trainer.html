The 1-Minute Note Secret & 3-Act Story Arc Trainer

In nineteen years of preparing IELTS and Cambridge C1 candidates, the single most common reason a fluent speaker freezes at the 70-second mark in Part 2 is simple: they spent their one-minute preparation time writing two full sentences on the notepad, read them aloud in ten seconds, and then ran straight off a cliff.

"Toss out the checklist mentality. Write four single trigger keywords down the margin, anchor your opening thirty seconds in the past, paint one vivid sensory scene, and spend your final forty-five seconds reflecting on why it still matters today." — Gareth Montgomery
RULE 01 • PREP MINUTE

The 1-Minute Note Secret

Write only 4 single trigger keywords down the margin (one per prompt bullet). Never write full sentences—your eyes lock onto the paper and your natural intonation flattens out.

4 Margin Triggers → [Where/When] • [Who] • [Core Scene] • [So What?]
RULE 02 • PACING ARC

The 3-Act Story Arc (120s)

Divide your two-minute long turn into three distinct acts: Act I: Past Context (30s) → Act II: Sensory Snapshot / Core Scene (45s) → Act III: Reflection / 'So What?' (45s).

Had been + -ing (Act I) → What struck me was... (Act II) → Had it not been for... (Act III)
RULE 03 • EXTENSION

The Cambridge Anchor

The bullet points on the card are springboards, not a police interrogation checklist. If you cover all four bullets by 1:15, deepen your reflection and contrast past vs. present until the examiner halts you.

Looking back in the cold light of day, what has changed most is...

    A.R.E.A. Blueprint: [Assertion: It is widely argued that...] + [Reason: What drives this is...] + [Evidence: For instance, ..., thereby + -ing] + [Alternative: That said, were we to...]
    Live Spoken Syntax Drill

    Upgrade Flat B2 Spoken Sentences into C1 Long-Turn Hinges

    Work through these three spoken sentence upgrades aloud before clicking the reveal button to compare against the Cambridge C1 model.

    What + Clause + BE + Noun/Clause

    Flat B2: "I really liked the natural timber roof of the library."

    C1 Wh-Cleft: "What struck me immediately was the vaulted timber ceiling above the reading desks."
    Had it not been for + Noun Phrase, Subject + would have + V3

    Flat B2: "If my grandfather hadn't helped me, I would have given up."

    C1 Inverted Conditional: "Had it not been for my grandfather's steady patience, I would have abandoned the bench within a fortnight."
    Having + Past Participle, Main Clause

    Flat B2: "After I spent three months repairing the watch, I became much more patient."

    C1 Perfect Participle: "Having spent three months coaxing that seized escapement back to life, I no longer rush delicate work."
