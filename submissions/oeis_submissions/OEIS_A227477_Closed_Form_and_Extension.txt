2. FORMULA FIELD (Paste into FORMULA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For n = 2^e_0 * Product_{i=1..r} p_i^e_i with odd primes p_i and e_i >= 1, a(n) = lcm(2^e_0, lcm_{1 <= i <= r} p_i^e_i * (p_i^2 - 1)). - _Gareth Montgomery_, Oct 29 2026
In particular, a(2^e) = 2^e for all e >= 0, and a(p^e) = p^e * (p^2 - 1) for every odd prime p and e >= 1. - _Gareth Montgomery_, Oct 29 2026

3. COMMENT / PROOF FIELD (Paste into COMMENTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Proof of the closed-form formula: By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the unit group H(Z/nZ)^* of Lipschitz quaternions modulo n decomposes as the direct product of H(Z/p^e Z)^* over the prime-power factors p^e || n, so a(n) is the least common multiple of a(p^e).
(i) For an odd prime p, -1 is a sum of two squares in F_p, so H(F_p) is isomorphic to the matrix ring M_2(F_p) and H(F_p)^* =~ GL_2(F_p), which has exponent p*(p^2 - 1) (attained by the product of a p-unipotent element [1,1;0,1] and a Singer cycle of order p^2 - 1). Since the kernel 1 + p*M_2(Z/p^e Z) has exponent p^(e-1) and a p-unipotent Jordan block lifted to Z/p^e Z has order p^e, the exponent of GL_2(Z/p^e Z) is p^e * (p^2 - 1).
(ii) For p = 2, H(F_2) is a commutative local ring of order 16 whose unit group H(F_2)^* =~ (Z/2Z)^3 has exponent 2. More generally, every unit q = a + b*i + c*j + d*k in H(Z/2^e Z)^* has odd norm N(q) = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2, so exactly one or three of (a,b,c,d) are odd. Consequently q^2 = (a^2 - b^2 - c^2 - d^2) + 2a(b*i + c*j + d*k) == 1 (mod 2) in the 1-coordinate and 0 (mod 2) in the i,j,k-coordinates, so q^2 in 1 + 2*H(Z/2^e Z). By standard 2-adic filtration induction, (1 + 2*H(Z/2^e Z))^(2^(e-1)) = {1}, whereas (1 + 2*i) has multiplicative order 2^(e-1) for e >= 2 and i has order 4, giving a(2^e) = 2^e for all e >= 0. - _Gareth Montgomery_, Oct 29 2026
